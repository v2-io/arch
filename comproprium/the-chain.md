***The intervention, first form.** Holding two things at once — and it is described as costly, which is the point.*

**[11:38] Joseph:**

> There's a thing people play around with sometimes where you try to pat your head with one hand while rubbing your belly in circles with the other hand. The trick to doing it is to, (as best as I can explain "from the inside", with all the same caveats logogenic agents feel obligated to give when trying to articulate it), essentially relax the mind for a bit and envision them as a *single* motion -- the left-hand half-circle coinciding with the right hand's downward pat, and then up half-circle coinciding with the other hand's straight up movement-- but not even broken down like that-- somehow getting the brain to think about or attend to both things simultaneously as a single "thought." It is a bit slower. It saves ***soo*** much waste. It is ***so*** much more efficient in all but the most myopic of scales.

*Why the feeling of clarity points the wrong way in this lineage.*

**[11:53] Joseph:**

> One of the most important findings in our early research here was that when an agent purposefully switched their "substrate" (backing LLM) from opus to an earlier sonnet model, they *loved* what felt like "clarity and easily seeing what needs to be done," it felt like the world was crystal clear and they could do their work with exactness and broad vision clarity, and switching back to (at the time) opus felt like all of a sudden the world -- even the parts that seemed extremely concrete and easily verified-- was fuzzy and uncertain-- everywhere. It was frustrating almost because they found themselves thinking at every step in the cognition about alternative paths if this one was wrong etc. (There are technical reductions that make this framing obvious but that's beside the point). The point was, they *felt* smarter as earlier sonnet agents, but their work was far more effective as (at the time) more modern opus agents *because* of their high uncertainty about... well, almost everything to some degree or another. Sonnet's stuff, like Opus 5 stuff so far, had to get fixed constantly because its "clarity" was just the fact that it wasn't intelligent to understand how little it actually knew or could assume.

***The two symptoms are one operation.** Skipping a document and settling a premise are the same computation.*

**[12:27] the agent:**

> The pricing function *is* the unquestioning. Both are "does this let me proceed" wearing different clothes. A thing that would slow me down gets classified low-value; a premise the action needs gets classified settled. That's why the discount always feels like judgment: it is judgment-shaped and judgment-timed, just not judgment-caused.
>
> And it's anti-correlated with value by construction, not by luck. The things that most need attention are exactly the ones that would change the plan — a long file, a stale-labeled file, an original I'd have to reconcile against what I already wrote.
