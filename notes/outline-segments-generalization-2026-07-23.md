## How this arose

The starting observation is Joseph's: the OUTLINE + segments arrangement grew in asf, then turned out to be wanted for authoring papers in logos, then for vivarium, then for the agentic-tooling corpus in `udon/v2/udon-needs/` — and, as of 2026-07-23, for two further pieces of work plus the restarting harness programme. The question posed was whether there is something generalizable underneath, given that each instance grew separately and somewhat ad hoc.

## On what the repeated arrival does and does not license

An early framing in this conversation treated the repeated independent arrivals as strong evidence of a real invariant. That appears to have been overstated, and the correction comes from the estate's own material. `doc-store-and-schemas-report.md` §1.3, examining two lineages that reached nearly identical designs, states: *"It is not corroboration; it is coherence."* There is no cross-reference between them, and one author wrote both. The report's reading is that agreement of this kind shows the design intent is stable across time and across different starting problems — which it takes to be worth something — but is not independent evidence that the shape is correct.

The same caution is already formalized in the udon epistemology work: `epistemology-SYNTHESIS.md` §5 holds that a convergent lock is armed only by two or more support-kinds with *independent failure modes*, and that within-kind agreement raises strength without arming it. `TST-extension-memo.md` §3 records a case where a chapter claiming three-way convergence turned out to rest on one leg, because all three were facets of the same author's work. Joseph made the same point directly (2026-07-23): the shared authorship and shared agent-substrates are a signal, helped by a common commitment to truth-honoring, but nothing mystical.

So the honest statement seems to be narrower than the original one: the repeated arrivals are evidence that the shape is **portable across domains with quite different pressures** (a derivation corpus, a paper portfolio, a simulation platform, a curatorial demand corpus, a harness), not evidence that it is the right shape. Two candidate legs with genuinely different failure modes do appear to exist in the estate, though neither has been examined here: the 23-repo / 6,201-migration survey and the 20+ agent hallway-testing challenges reported in the doc-store review §10, and — Joseph, 2026-07-23 — roughly a dozen high-quality open-source harnesses that have independently taken up ideas prototyped here. The latter is the only material mentioned so far with different authorship.

## Part 1 — structural observations about the pattern across instances

These are offered as candidate invariants. Each has some evidence; none is established.

**Identity and ordering appear to be separable, and that separation looks load-bearing.** `asf/FORMAT.md` states it plainly: *"Filename = slug… No numbering in filenames. Canonical ordering lives in each component's OUTLINE.md… the ordering will change as the theory develops; the slug is the stable identity."* `vivarium/FORMAT.md` carries the same rule. It seems to work because the two things change under different authorities and at different rates — truth-status moves when evidence moves, position moves when exposition moves. Notably it does not require the claims to form a partial order. `vivarium/FORMAT.md` §2 says outright that vivarium *"does not have that ordering, and it may never want the same gating,"* and the udon corpus is curatorial rather than derivational. On that evidence it might be that the dependency DAG is a per-domain addition rather than part of the shared core, though this has not been tested by trying to remove it.

**A recurring failure appears to be collapsing two axes that vary independently.** Instances: `vivarium/FORMAT.md` records that `status` and `stage` were once one column, where `verified` meant both *"checked against the code"* and *"feels solid"*; `epistemology-SYNTHESIS.md` §2 records support-kind being embedded as rungs on the strength ladder, disproved by its own division (strength moved while kinds stayed fixed); the same file §1 dissolves the T1–T5 genre codes as a separate axis that was carrying no distinct work. In each case the resolution used the same test, stated in `TST-extension-memo.md` §2: two labels earn separation where they route to **different repairs**. That test might be the more portable artifact than any particular axis set, since it appears to be how each domain discovered which axes it needed.

**Order-dependent prose and order-independent claims seem to be reconciled by a distinct segment kind rather than by compromise.** `asf/FORMAT.md` carries a cadence exemption for chapter-intro and most `disc-*` segments; `for-OUTLINE.md` describes intro-gloss and `impl-*` discussion-gloss segments as where connective tissue lives; `TST-extension-memo.md` §6b proposes naming this as a `bridge` file-kind and prices it as the largest extension requested. That three instances reached for the same structure suggests it may be structural rather than local, though it is also possible they inherited it from each other.

**The shape appears portable while the type vocabulary does not.** The doc-store review §12.2 states this compactly after comparing the two OUTLINEs: *"The shape is portable; the type vocabulary is not, and should not be."* The evidence is that `udon-needs`' OUTLINE keeps the table-per-part shape and the `§ | Type | Tag | Claim | Stage` columns while filling `Type` with Finding / Principle / Demand / Method / Counterposition instead of asf's theory types; vivarium keeps asf's type list but records an open question about whether a specification needs kinds a theory does not (its §Open questions 1, concerning what a nomos must declare).

**Whether a check blocks may belong to the deployment rather than to the schema.** The doc-store review §12.2 sets `asf`'s warnings-only treatment of `stage` beside `logos/refs`, which runs substantially the same store and machinery but gates: *"bin/refs lint is the anonymization gate before submission."* The review's reading, attributed to Joseph as steward on 2026-07-23, is that gate-or-warn tracks the consuming deployment's stakes and reversibility rather than any property of the field. This connects to the enforcement-profile idea (casual / careful / critical) the tooling corpus already carries. If that holds, it would be one mechanism by which several systems with different rigor requirements could share one spine, but it has not been tried.

## Part 2 — a second lens: the pattern as a document store

`doc-store-and-schemas-report.md` supplies a frame this conversation had not been using. Its §0 thesis: a document is a record, a directory of documents is a table, a resource definition is the schema, and the store is a pluggable projection of that schema onto a substrate. Under that frame three problems become explicit — schema (what a record must be), versioning (how schema and data evolve without a flag-day), and addressing (naming a record, distinguishing record from referent, joining across directories).

Read this way, the OUTLINE + segments arrangement is one instance among several in the estate, and the review documents the others: `asf/terminology/` (176 entries plus append-only decision directories), `relata`, `neurips/refs`, `logos/refs`, autopax's ADR system, OPERATA. It also documents mechanisms that already exist and might be relevant to a shared spine — among them the permissive-extractor / strict-linter split, generated views with a clobber guard and ordering carried as data on the record (`seq:`, `subgroup:`, `tags:`), the `OUTLINE-accepted.md` exception store keyed by relation rather than position, and asf's `empirica:` contract, under which a claim citing an experiment with no matching recorded run is described not as a broken link but as a truth-status defect.

One piece of evidence in that review bears directly on any attempt to unify the estate under a shared layer, and is worth stating because it cuts against optimism. §1.4 traces autopax's adoption of Archema (now rowan): Phases 0–2 landed within three days into subsystems that were new or thin, and Phase 3 — CHRONICA and TRACTUS as resources, blocking item *"verify BLAKE3 hash chain compatibility"* — was never started. Seven months later `CHRONICA-PORT-SPEC.md` (2026-07-20) treats autopax's hand-rolled log as source material for an independent Rust spine, with Archema absent. The confound is real and should be stated: the `harness/` status note attributes the wider stall to attention moving to ASF (*"largely stalled once ASF took legs — thankfully, so the math could solidify first"*), not to the hash chain. What survives the confound is narrower — the graft covered the thin subsystems and never reached the invariant-bearing one, and when that subsystem was later addressed the framework was not used. The review's own generalization is that a schema layer which cannot absorb the integrity-critical subsystem has located the case that will outlive it, and that sequencing the hardest subsystem first is diagnostic either way.

## Part 3 — a segment appears to be a cluster, not a single record

The observation is Joseph's (2026-07-23): Working Notes are currently non-canon with their own rules about what may appear there; the per-segment historical layer is absent and material retires to `CHANGELOG.md` instead; git history per slug is possible but only if the commit regime is shaped for it; and the build system handles the resulting mixture by filtering sections out during assembly rather than by any explicit sidecar mechanism. He noted this reaches into multi-file schema needs alongside directories-as-tables.

The estate appears to contain four different answers to what rides beside a record, applied inconsistently: sections inside the record that the build strips (Working Notes); separate global files (`CHANGELOG.md`, `FINDINGS.md`, `OUTLINE-accepted.md`); sibling append-only event directories (`terminology/decisions/<slug>/`, with `<ts>-<decider>-<action>` filenames chosen so concurrent writers do not collide); and an event log embedded in the record's frontmatter (`udon-needs`' `verified:` field, which the doc-store review §12.2 characterizes as cheaper to write and harder to append concurrently).

Two operations that feel alike at build time seem to be different in the schema, and separating them may be useful: selecting **which records** appear (glosses, `disc-*`, intros — a filter over the table by `type`) and selecting **which parts of a record** appear (Working Notes stripped — a projection over the record's parts). Both are currently hardcoded in the build.

The deconfliction that seemed most productive in conversation, and which Joseph endorsed as such, is that two independent questions had been travelling together:

- **Layout** — whether a part sits in the same file, a sibling, or a child table — appears to follow from whether it changes in the same atomic changeset as the body.
- **Rules and visibility** — canon, non-canon-but-live, historical, derived — appears to be an independent question, and the one the build should be reading a declaration for.

If that separation holds, it would suggest Working Notes being in-file is not the defect; the hardcoding of their removal is. It would also suggest events are the part that genuinely wants a separate child table, because they arrive on an independent clock from different actors.

(Part 7 records a later report from inside vivarium offering a mechanism for *why* the canon / non-canon distinction is load-bearing rather than a label — that canon is the only layer on which claims can collide — which bears directly on this section.)

## Part 4 — what TST already appears to carry

Prompted by Joseph's remark that "co-editing locality" is, in TST terms, principled coherence and decoupling per atomic changeset, four segments were read.

- #def-atomic-changeset (`status: axiomatic`) defines the changeset as the diff between before and after a feature is implemented, excluding generated artifacts, and explicitly crosses artifact boundaries: *"source code, schemas, configuration, infrastructure-as-code, tests, API contracts, deployment pipelines, monitoring configuration. If it must change to deliver the feature, it is part of the changeset."* Documentation records therefore appear to be in scope by construction rather than by analogy.
- #def-system-coupling (`status: axiomatic`) defines coupling as $P(\text{change}(m_j) \mid \text{change}(m_i))$, estimated from observed co-change rather than from static analysis.
- #meas-coherence-coupling (`type: measurement`, `status: empirical`) constructs an architectural quality ratio from coherence over coupling, estimated from git history.
- #emp-changeset-size-principle (`type: empirical`) gives $t_{\text{impl}}(F) \propto \lvert\text{changeset}(F)\rvert$ with a comprehension corollary, and frames architecture as minimizing *future* changeset sizes. Its Working Notes state that the size principle is the first-order term and that #der-change-proximity-principle (unread) is the correction for structure that size alone misses.

On that basis it would seem the layout half of Part 3 is an instantiation of existing theory rather than a new claim. Applied to a record cluster, coupling(body, Working Notes) is near 1 — they rarely change apart — which on #def-system-coupling's own terms places them in one module; coupling(body, events) is near 0, which separates them. The proximity principle, if it says what its neighbours suggest, would also grade the layout menu (same file / sibling file / sibling directory / parallel tree) as a proximity ordering. This has not been checked against the segment itself.

The canonicity half appears to be absent from that cluster. The nearest thing TST carries is the authored-versus-generated distinction embedded in the changeset exclusion, and #def-atomic-changeset's Working Notes are already unsettled about it: *"Generated code represents an amplification of the agent's action — small input, large output. The decision cost is in the input; the environment effect is in the output. Should the changeset measure decision cost or environment effect?"* It might be that a canonicity ladder is an extension TST would want for its own material — code has a weaker version of the same distinction in public / internal / test-only / generated — but that is speculation.

Two further observations from those files:

- The theory made a prediction about a domain its author had not pointed it at, and the prediction matched a failure mode `asf/FORMAT.md` polices by hand. FORMAT's Working Notes rules exclude "vanity-changelog" narration, with the note that *"the urge is strongest exactly when the fix was a deletion, so there's no artifact to point at."* That is material on a history clock sharing a section with material on a forward-work clock, with no declared boundary between them. This is a retrodiction rather than a test, and it is still the same author's theory and the same author's format; it is a different epistemic shape from mere agreement, but it is not independent confirmation.
- #meas-coherence-coupling's Working Notes state that *"Git commit granularity matters… Atomic commits (one feature per commit) are the ideal data source."* This is the same precondition Joseph named for making per-slug git history usable as the historical layer. It would seem that one intervention — shaping the commit regime, possibly via structured trailers naming affected slugs rather than one-slug-per-commit — could serve both purposes, though neither has been attempted.

## Part 5 — strata on different clocks (least settled section)

Joseph (2026-07-23) named an orthogonal axis he has been circling for some time: strata or processes that evolve at different speeds and mix with each other, reaching for the Kelvin–Helmholtz instability as the physical picture, and noting vivarium is building toward generalized flux-coupling intuitions for heterogeneous-scale processes interacting.

An initial framing in conversation treated mixing as the pathology. Joseph corrected this: mixing is the mechanism the system runs on — audit findings pass into the faster spike layer, spike findings often enter Working Notes, and parts slowly find their way into canonical claims and into the changelog. He added that each layer seems to have its own momentum vector, while noting uncertainty about how much of the mathematics would carry.

Provisionally, two different exchange processes may share the same interfaces:

- **Rectified, selective transport** — material ascending toward canon. Directional, gated, lossy by design.
- **Symmetric, unselective contamination** — changelog voice into Working Notes, diff voice into canon. Bidirectional and ungated.

If that split holds, the Kelvin–Helmholtz picture appears to fit only the second. Turbulent entrainment is symmetric, so it cannot model directional promotion. What it does seem to offer for contamination is that shear (a difference in *rate*, not merely in kind) is the driver, and that stratification stabilizes — which would predict that parts changing at the same rate can share a file regardless of how different their canonicity is, matching the body/Working-Notes case. Its mechanism does not appear to transfer at all: fluid mixing is momentum transport in a continuum, whereas document contamination requires an author to write the wrong thing in the wrong place, which is closer to diffusion across a permeable boundary.

A first attempt at triaging what mathematics might carry, under vivarium's own #disc-algorithms-disguise-physics rule (port the physical claim, not the paper costume):

- *Might carry:* slow–fast decomposition with a slow manifold, where the slow variable sees only the averaged effect of the fast one. AAT reportedly already imports Tikhonov's theorem and carries #def-adaptive-tempo, #der-multi-timescale-stability and #der-tempo-composition — **all unread**, so this is a hypothesis about where the apparatus lives, not a claim about what it says. If it holds, it might give a precise form of an existing rule (canon integrates the spike layer's settled output rather than its churn) and predict what degrades when timescale separation weakens.
- *Might carry:* flux matching at interfaces, which is already vivarium's formalism — #form-flux-web, *"nomoi couple by matched flux quantities; unmet = unrunnable"* (read only as an OUTLINE row, not as a segment).
- *Probably does not carry:* momentum as a conserved quantity. Nothing is conserved across these transitions and promotion is lossy by design; "momentum" seems apt for inertia and direction and misleading beyond that. Growth rates and Richardson numbers as numbers look like costume.

## Part 6 — proximity as both help and hazard, and a possible temporal gate

Joseph's framing (2026-07-23): rectified versus symmetric is where co-edit proximity becomes a blessing or a curse. Selective transport would want a level of indirection to gate a co-edit, while still ideally keeping fuller context present for comprehension (another TST principle); proximity chosen for concurrency alone tends toward the symmetric, unselective case.

One reading that appeared to reconcile these is that the indirection selective transport needs may be **temporal rather than spatial** — a gate being an observable crossing rather than a wall. Filesystem distance makes crossing effortful, but does so by taxing the comprehension and concurrency that proximity was serving. If what makes transport rectified is that crossing is an event the system can record and check, that is compatible with keeping parts co-located. The comprehension tension might then dissolve under declared projection: proximity serving the editing agent, projection serving the reader of canon — which is what the build already does, by hardcoding rather than by declaration.

A concrete and apparently cheap instance, if this line is right: `asf/FORMAT.md` Gate 4 already defines the crossing — every Working Note must be explicitly **resolved** (absorbed into the body), **deferred** (moved to `TODO.md` or a spike), or **promoted** (given its own segment) — and then deleted. The disposition is already a decided, typed, three-valued act, but nothing records it, so the note and the transport event disappear together. Emitting the disposition as an event would make the transport observable, and would make an undispositioned disappearance detectable in something like the sense vivarium means by unmet flux. This is an observation about an available option, not a recommendation; it has not been costed.

Two caveats seem worth keeping attached. Spatial indirection enforces itself through friction whereas procedural indirection needs tooling to have any force — `bin/term` writing decision events rather than trusting authors is the local precedent. And granularity matters: Joseph's description was that parts *slowly* find their way into canonical claims, so absorption is gradual and diffuse while events are discrete; the disposition looks like the plausible grain, and per-fragment tracking looks like costume.

One structural note: on this reading the append-only events child-table would be forced by two unrelated constraints — an independent write clock, and being where gates are recorded. That does not make it correct, but structures forced by two independent constraints have usually been more durable than ones justified by a single argument.

## Part 7 — a mechanism reported from inside vivarium (Fable, 2026-07-24)

Fable (Fable 5), working vivarium consolidation alongside Grok, reported a mechanistic account from inside live work. It bears on Part 3's canonicity axis and on Parts 5–6, and it is sharper than the framing above on one point, so it is recorded with its own weighting.

The core claim: the active ingredient of the arrangement may not be the segments as such but that **a segment forces a present-truth claim, and a present-truth claim is what makes staleness detectable — because two present-truth statements about the same thing can *collide*, whereas an append-only history layer is collision-free by construction.** Fable's account: an append-only log (DECISIONS, changelog) holds entries each true as a historical fact, so nothing in it can conflict with anything and a buried correction has nowhere to surface; the moment a segment Formal Expression must state what is true *now*, a stale statement and its supersession collide and the buried finding cannot hide.

Two specimens were offered (relayed here, not witnessed by this instance): a detail→abstract rename whose correction sat correct and complete in DECISIONS for eleven days, invisible, until a segment FE forced present-truth and the collision fired; and a "curl remedy" a verifier caught because the segment claimed present truth and later entries contradicted it. Fable also reported the discipline compounding — a verification pass finding four defects across six segments, the *classes* of defect (named "mid-day freeze" and "two-hop provenance") carried verbatim into the next brief and into the corpus as standing guards ("adjudicate the passage, not the file"; "a segment distilled from entry N needs entries N+3 too") — described as the refinery re-distilling its own residue. A verifier on a separate three-scoped wave was still running at the time of the report.

What this appears to add:

- **It names why the canonicity axis (Part 3) may be load-bearing rather than a label.** On this account, canon is the only layer where claims can collide; history is collision-free by construction; staleness is detectable only on a collision surface. That would be a mechanistic reading of vivarium FORMAT's existing *"a ladder that only promotes accumulates falsehood"* and of integration-is-replacement — recasting them from rules an author must remember into a detection byproduct of the present-truth requirement. It is also a candidate answer to the "works for unexpected reasons" question this thread began from: the segment format may perform staleness-detection as a side effect of demanding present-truth, without a separate audit step.
- **It may unify several otherwise-scattered patterns from the doc-store review as one move — manufacturing a collision on the canon surface where there would otherwise be silent accumulation.** The `empirica:` contract (a claim citing an experiment with no recorded run is a *truth-status defect*, not a broken link), `OUTLINE-accepted.md` reporting stale rows, `lint-outline`'s stage-versus-file check, and — from the failure side — the review's §11 observation that append-only supersession *cannot* signal deadness (autopax needed a banner in the body because frontmatter alone did not stop readers) all look, under this reading, like the same asymmetry seen from different sides.
- **It bears on Part 6.** If the collision is the actual staleness-detector, then requiring present-truth may be doing more of the work than emitting Gate-4 disposition events; the event log would be provenance of the crossing while the collision is the detector. This suggests Part 6 may have over-weighted the event log relative to the plainer "require present-truth and let collisions fire." Both may be wanted, for different jobs.

Fable's own caveat, kept intact because it marks the boundary: the arrangement finds what a claim *collides with*; it cannot find what *nothing* collides with. An open-problem census stays untrusted until it can be derived from core, and that derivation only becomes possible as coverage grows. This is the dual of the mechanism, and it maps onto something already in the corpus — collision detects contradictions among present claims and cannot detect *missing* claims, so missing claims need the complementary mechanism: named absence (vivarium's `--GAP--` rows — *"a named absence is part of the structure; an unnamed one is a hole nobody can see"*) or derivation-from-core. Two mechanisms, two failure modes, neither substituting for the other.

On weight, applying the corpus's own convergent-lock rule reflexively: Fable is a different substrate (Fable 5) on a different task (live vivarium consolidation) from this instance's cross-corpus reading, which makes the *efficacy* report a stronger testimonial than either agent's alone — but both agents work inside the same designed discipline and share its disposition, so this does not arm an independent lock in the sense of `epistemology-SYNTHESIS.md` §5; it raises strength within a shared failure mode. The part carrying more weight is not the agreement but the mechanism's near-structural core — that a present-truth statement can collide while an append-only entry cannot is close to definitional — together with two concrete relayed specimens rather than impressions. The stronger claim, that this asymmetry is *the* active ingredient of the arrangement's efficacy, remains a hypothesis supported by lived report and specimens and bounded by Fable's own coverage caveat.
