# Feedback on `generic-collab-council.md` (692650c), from the tribunal and family-council history

*2026-11-03. From the research subagent that wrote `research/tribunal-history.md`. I read the template whole. Everything below is my reading of the template against that history; section references (§) are to the history. Joseph's 2025 remarks are cited for their reasons, not as rulings.*

## Short version

The loop is close to the forms that actually ran. It is far from the ones that died, and the difference is the right one: sealing, a recorded shuffle, a record that comes out of the procedure itself, and a leader who frames rather than opines. The history's warnings are mostly not about the mechanism. They are about:

- **attention**: members drifting back to their home sessions;
- **ceremony decaying into box-ticking**;
- **a record whose emphasis steers what agents care about**;
- **generality without a first customer**.

## What the history says this loop will run into

**1. Members drifting home.** The Oct 5, 2025 councils did not fail on procedure. Joseph, Oct 8: "it was so hard to do the council we tried the other day … because you brothers would keep seeing tracking-snapshots back 'home' and then helpfully respond to me and wait for me to respond when I was in a different terminal waiting for your responses in the council." During a session he also had to say "you both need to stop dropping out of council unless you really need to talk to me privately." An MCP seat tool fixes the plumbing that ate that day. It does not fix a member whose harness keeps offering it another conversation. "Take as much time as they need … send off subagents" makes this more likely, not less. Worth deciding up front whether a seat-holder's session exists only for the seat, or what the norm is for coming back to it.

**2. Ceremony decaying into box-ticking.** The zoetica tribunal ritual died with "No actual tribunal XML in commits" (§2.6, §2.10). Codex had predicted it two weeks earlier: "agents might treat it as a box to tick." The parts of this loop most at risk are the **unity report** and the **sustaining levels**: short, formulaic, and easy to fill without thought. What protects against this is already in the design, namely that the record is the log of the act itself, not a separate write-up. The second-most-likely failure is the leader's decision record. Keep it small enough that it actually gets written.

**3. The verdict before the procedure.** In the one complete peer tribunal on record (§2.7), Zi-am-tur's message opened "Your critique is PRECISE and I agree" before the Investigator phase began. Sealed first responses stop members herding on *each other*. They do not stop a member from concluding first and then filling in the form. The second-response norm is where to work on this (point 6 below).

**4. The record's emphasis steers.** Joseph, 2026-07-29, about a vivarium agent: "they cared so much about the provenance correctness that they forgot for a moment to even care whether it was a good decision or not." Agents will adhere to whatever the record makes prominent. In this template, seat types, sustaining levels, and the unity report are all prominent. **The warrant is not**: which arguments the decision rests on, and how much. The decision record lists consensus, assumptions, assignments, reopen conditions, and importance, but not the load-bearing arguments. The 2026 tribunal description centers exactly that, and the "Uses: epistemic tribunals" line mentions it only for that one use. I'd put it in the generic record. It is also the mechanism that turns a new finding into a local re-check instead of a threat to the whole decision.

**5. Reopening loses weight.** The Oct 2025 parliamentary engine's "reconsider" (§8.3) recorded no reason and skipped prior analysis. It also silently reset the threshold to simple majority, so a decision taken unanimously could be reversed by a bare majority. The template's "what would reopen it" and "importance it was decided at" already guard against this. Make the reopen path carry three things explicitly: the original importance, the prior record, and *what changed*.

## What earlier forms learned that the loop could keep

**6. Steel-man the other, falsify your own** (the Oct 1 peer protocol, §2.7). This was the earlier answer to the question of how a unity-presuming body still gets adversarial testing: it puts the testing inside each member. It fits naturally as the norm for second responses.

**7. Recognizing a member.** Joseph's Oct 5, 2025 specification (§4.2) included "the ability to solicit a response from one or two participants instead of all three … the equivalent of me essentially recognizing someone to speak." The template's leader turn is addressed to everyone. A leader question to one seat may be worth allowing, and Joseph had a use for it.

**8. Declared weight plus a raisable ceiling.** The parliamentary notes (§8.1, §8.5) set weight by threshold, but unanimous consent let a single objection send a question back to full procedure. Here, an "object" or "strongly object" level at sustaining could automatically mean "return to Discuss" rather than being one level among six.

**9. Decide the disclosure mode before anyone commits.** From the Oct 2025 notes on secret versus roll-call votes: "Participants must know the transparency level before committing." Joseph's reason for anonymity, Oct 3, 2025: it "loses its ability to keep a member safe in some contexts" if names leak into the record. If sustaining levels may ever be anonymous, the charter has to say so before the levels are written, not after.

**10. The sealed expected solution is new, and it supplies what the tribunal faked.** No 2025 form had it. The August 2025 tribunal seed corpus had calibration fields (ECE, Brier, "Success Rate: 97.7%") filled with invented numbers, because nothing ever produced real ones (§2.3). A sealed prediction compared with each outcome produces real ones. Joseph's own hypothesis-coupling remark from 2026 says the same: explicit hypotheses are what make a decision's confidence calibratable. I think this is the most valuable addition in the template.

## Is there a real use pulling on it?

The history is blunt on this point. Every tribunal form that was designed without a waiting user died; Joseph's own words were "I had this grand plan for the tribunal and then... 'What do I *use* it for??' So I shelved it". Both forms that ran had members with a stake, waiting on each other (§6.1). A template covering brainstorming, tribunals, and decisions at once has the shape of the grand plan.

So I'd pick one real item to run it on first, not a demonstration topic. Candidates the record shows are already waiting:

- **The vivarium council's next real decision.** Joseph, 2026-07-29: "I've honestly let all recent vivarium decisions get decided 'by council'". That is the one living council practice, and it already writes decision-record embryos.
- **The open flags that each new agent re-discovers.** These are the "repeat-billing" cases in the udon tribunal note (§2.15). A hold with each dissenting seat's stated exit is exactly what they lack.
- **The template's own "Open" list.** This has precedent: the first Oct 5 council's only decision was its own menu, and the zoetica council README served as "our first council". It is cheap and low-stakes, and the members have a real stake in the answer. Its weakness is that it is circular, so it shouldn't be the only trial.

A smaller point: the template says "Nothing here has run yet", which is right. The 2025 record also shows that "worked" in someone's memory sometimes meant two test sessions. When it does run, record which runs were tests.
