## Leads from the whole read — places neither audit has yet looked

Each with my confidence marked; all want first-hand verification.

1. **The chair-stonewall self-help path is rights-critical and, I believe, unmodeled.** 62:8–9: if the chair ignores a point of order (or an appeal) repeatedly, the member may — standing in place — put the question to a vote *themselves*, without the chair. Same mechanism at 62:11n4 for the declare-the-chair-vacant motion. This is the assembly's last-resort enforcement against a captured chair; it belongs in `actions.json` and on the phase-7 pin list (it is very nearly the definition of rights-machinery-whose-exercise-is-rare). High confidence the passages exist as described; verify the exact preconditions (repetition counts, second requirements).
2. **23:7–23:8's remedies are compilable arithmetic, not judgment.** The vote-invalidation conditions for excluded members and improper votes turn on whether the affected count *could have changed the outcome* — a margin comparison over data the event log holds. I don't recall any report or model file carrying these as expressions; the continuing-breach hooks [23:6] cover the grounds but not these remedy conditions. Medium-high confidence.
3. **Order semantics: hour-set vs. duration-set closure differ under interruption.** 15:16: an order closing debate *at an hour* eats any time lost to interruptions; an order allotting a *duration* does not — tabled time doesn't count against the budget. This is a second Order attribute beyond your A3 (`deadline` vs. `budget` semantics), consumed by the same guards. High confidence.
4. **A partly-executed Previous Question is unreconsiderable in a specific structural way.** 16:15: if questions under a PQ order come back after postponement, reconsidering the PQ order is *never* possible — because the vote that postponed was itself execution of the order. A subtle lifecycle flag (order-partly-executed) with a non-obvious trigger. Medium-high confidence; verify against 16:13–15 whole.
5. **The members-present vote basis has mechanical peculiarities.** 44:9(a): for thresholds based on members *present*, the negative is not taken at all (intrinsically irrelevant), and the chair must count those present immediately after the affirmative, before attendance can change. If `voting.json`'s threshold vocabulary lacks a members-present basis (I believe the audits only exercised majority / two-thirds / entire-membership / two-thirds-neg), both the basis and its procedure are missing. Medium confidence on the model gap; high on the book content.
6. **The chair's own vote is a conditional affordance.** 4:56 and 44:12 (with its footnote): the chair, if a member, may vote exactly when the vote would affect the result — including the two-thirds cases, and including a case where the chair's vote provably *cannot* affect the result. All computable from the tally. If `vote` in `actions.json` doesn't carry the chair-conditional, it's an affordance-layer gap of exactly the kind your report hunts. Medium-high confidence.
7. **Stand at ease.** 8:2(4): a chair-controlled pause distinct from recess — no interruption of the meeting, resumed at the chair's word or on any member's objection. Trivial but real protocol state; I don't believe it exists anywhere in the model. High confidence on content.
8. **Session identity in serial mass meetings.** 53:32: in a series of mass meetings, fixed periodic meeting rules make each meeting a session, but next-meeting-set-each-time (or at-the-call-of-the-chair) makes the *whole series one session* — directly relevant to the phase-5 bootstrap path, where session boundaries gate renewal and same-question bars during founding. Medium-high confidence.
9. **Election finality as a timer-like event.** 46:46: an election becomes final on declaration (if present, not declining) or on notification (if absent, having consented) — after which reconsideration is impossible. The registry carries the reconsiderability bar (you verified adjacent rows); whether the *finality event itself* is modeled — notification as an event kind — I doubt. Relevant to phase 5's artifact/event inventory. Medium confidence on the gap.
10. **Committee-of-the-whole as a ready-made child-config test.** 52:9's severely restricted in-committee motion set (adopt/amend/rise, plus a short incidental list) is an enumerated, compilable config the current `council.json` sketches but — I believe — does not compile. It would make a good second proof-case after small-board, since its restriction list is explicit where most overlays are deltas. High confidence on content.
