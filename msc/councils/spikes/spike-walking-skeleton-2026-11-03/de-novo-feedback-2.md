## 0. The short version

Everything the author and the first verifier claimed as measured still holds on `694a770`, and I re-derived it rather than read it (§1). The revision was done carefully: every one of the first report's eight concrete changes landed as the response says, the token redaction is complete in the tree (and the two named commits do still carry the values), and the regression suite passes 19/19 on my run with output identical to the committed one.

What this pass adds is five properties of the *revised* design that neither the first report nor the regression suite reaches, each reproduced model-free in `verify-2/verify2.py`, plus two live runs (§2.7, §2.8). In order of consequence:

1. **A stalled seat has no way back** (test 1). The fix for the first report's D3 treats a live-but-silent seat as gone "until it acts again", but the only act that counts is a submission that lands in a round's close, and rounds now close the instant the other members submit. A returning member that loops `council_wait` → think 0.3 s → `council_submit`, exactly as the brief tells it to, was refused 13 times across 35 rounds and the meeting adjourned without it. Its view said nothing about being stalled. This is the latency race the whole design exists to remove, reintroduced for every member that comes back, and it sits on the path the plan now puts first: a `--bg` or interactive member that was idle.
2. **The stall streak counts rounds that never waited for the seat** (test 3). `absent` includes `gone`, so a seat that *crashed* and was absent-and-gone in two rounds that ran to the backstop for *someone else* returns live and is immediately `stalled`. The plan's §1.4 wording ("rounds that each waited the full backstop *for it*") describes a check the code does not make.
3. **An abandoned `council_wait` advances the cursor and consumes the next reveal** (test 2). The spike documents that Claude Code's idle abort and codex's default timeout abandon a call without telling the server (`probes/p4`, `c2`). The abandoned wait thread keeps running; when the round opens it renders a view, advances the seat's persisted cursor, and replies into the void. The member's live wait then renders with the advanced cursor: in 4 of 4 trials exactly one of the two replies carried the "since your last look" delta, and which one is not deterministic. A member whose live request drew the empty copy never sees that round's revealed intents, yet can act in the round, so `council_submit(round=rN)` does not prove it saw the state the plan's §2.1 and §5 say it proves.
4. **The driver relaunches a turn that did not act, about once a second, with no cap** (test 5). With a fake `claude` on PATH that answers like a turn and never calls the server, the driver ran 16 resumed turns in 8 s for two rounds in which nobody acted; the only exit was backstop → stall → gone → inquorate → adjourned. At real haiku turn costs that is unbounded spend for a member whose turn ends without a submission (an API error, a refused tool, a model that writes "I'll wait for the next round").
5. **The one-holder rule is keyed to a principal the client asserts** (test 6). Two processes presenting the same `_meta.sessionId` are one holder; the second's submission is accepted. That is fine for what it was built against (an accidental second session), but harness-notes' "a server can *attest* caller identity from codex and agy `_meta`" and the plan's §3.3 "Attest caller identity" overstate: the server records what the client says, and the holder's `_meta` sits in the server's own log.

Plus one place where the response says more than the code does (§2.6): "the minutes say why the seat was not waited for" — `view.describe` prints a stalled seat as `gone`, in the minutes and in every member's view.

And two live runs (§2.7, §2.8). The README's four-harness recipe reproduces on the final build by a stranger (replay and witness clean, 3–0; grok quota-dead, so r1 waited the full 180 s arrival grace for a seat that never came). The `--bg` member, run for the first time against the real server with progress on, showed that the 120 s auto-background does happen with progress, that under the spike's own brief the member does **not** go idle but opens another wait (so the "wake" in the debrief is a property of `b1`'s prompt, not of the harness), that the two pending waits then reproduce §2.3 live, and that a `--bg` session launched with the launchers' flags is seated with **22 browser-automation tools** from this machine's Chrome integration, which a `-p` member is not. The plan's step 3 rests on this adapter; §2.7 is the first evidence about it in the server's own configuration, and it cuts both ways.

## 2. What this pass found

Each item below was reproduced by `verify-2/verify2.py` (output in `verify2-output.txt`); the test number is the section number.

### 2.1 Stall escalation is one-way (test 1; defect)

`store._stalled` marks a seat stalled after `absent_rounds_to_gone` consecutive closes in which it was absent and the round ran to the backstop (or had already treated it as stalled). `try_close` then closes the round without waiting for it. The response (§4) says the seat is treated as gone "until it acts again". The code's meaning of "acts again": its seat appears in `submitted` of a later close, which ends the streak. Nothing else clears it; not a `council_status`, not a `council_wait`, not a late submission.

What that does to a returning member. Once the seat is stalled, every round closes the moment the other members have submitted (`all-submitted-or-gone`, 0.0–0.1 s after opening in the test). The returning member's loop is the one the brief prescribes: `council_wait` returns "A round is open for you" at once; the member reads the view, thinks, submits; its submission meets "round rN has closed without your submission; it is recorded as late. The open round is rM"; it tries rM; same. In the test, A is live throughout (its server heartbeats; its holder lease is fresh), stays silent through two 2 s backstop rounds, then loops with 0.3 s of thinking: 0 accepted, 13 refused, 35 rounds closed without it, meeting adjourned "agenda complete". It gets back in only by submitting before the *fastest* other member does, which is a latency race, and the view it reads while stalled shows an ordinary open round with its available actions and nothing about its status.

Why this matters more than its trigger suggests. The trigger is 2 × `backstop_s` of silence while live (30 min with the trial charters), which the blocking clients in the runs never hit. But the plan's build order now puts first the member kind that *does* go silent while live: a `--bg` or interactive session whose model ended its turn and whose server stays up (§2.7 below shows one). A human-attended session that sits for half an hour, a `--bg` member whose wake notification was lost, a member whose harness abandoned two waits: each returns to a council it cannot rejoin. The first report's suggested fix was rejected for bounding thinking time, and that argument is right; but the replacement bounds something else: the *return*.

A late first arrival does not have this problem (test 7): a seat that was `gone` for never having arrived becomes live on its first call and the next round waits for it. So the asymmetry is specific to the stall path.

What I would try, offered not prescribed: let any call from a stalled seat (status, wait, or a late submit) clear the stall for the *next* round, recorded as a receipt the close reads; "acts again" then means what the response says it means, and no bound on thinking time is introduced. The returning member's view should also say it was stalled and that the next round will wait for it.

### 2.2 The streak counts rounds that never waited for the seat (test 3; defect)

`_stalled` tests `seat in c["absent"]`, and `absent` is every addressed seat not in `submitted`, including seats in `gone`. So a seat whose holder lease had lapsed (crashed) is "absent" in a round that closed `backstop-deadline` because a *different*, live seat was slow. Two such rounds and the crashed seat is stalled. In the test: A crashed (holder lease 100 s stale), C live and silent (the reason for the backstop), B answering; after two backstop rounds `_stalled` returns `{A, C}`; A returns live, B submits, and the round closes 0.00 s later with `stalled: [A, C]`. A is now in §2.1's race though the council never once waited for it. The plan's §1.4 says the escalation counts "k rounds that each waited the full backstop *for it*"; the check for "for it" is `seat not in c["gone"]`, and it is not made.

### 2.3 An abandoned wait eats the delta (test 2; defect, reachable in documented harness behavior)

harness-notes establishes, and the plan's §2.1 builds on, that a harness can abandon a `council_wait` without a cancellation reaching the server (`p4`: Claude Code's idle abort; `c2`: codex's 60 s default). The server's handling of that case is incomplete. The abandoned wait's thread keeps polling; when the member's turn comes it does what every wait does: `witness(seat)`, `view.render(…, cursor)`, `set_cursor(…, last)`, reply. The member, following the brief ("if a tool call errors or times out, call council_wait again"), has a second wait pending on the same seat. Both return within the same half-second poll. Whichever renders second reads the cursor the first just advanced and shows no "since your last look" section.

Test 2 sends two waits on one connection, closes the round from the other seat, and reads both replies: in 4 of 4 trials exactly one carried the delta, and it was the first-sent request in two trials and the second-sent in the other two. If the harness kept the request that drew the empty copy, the member never sees the previous round's revealed intents (the motion that was moved, who voted how), while the view still shows the open round and the actions available, so it acts. Three consequences:

- `council_submit(round=rN)` is offered (plan §2.1, §5, debrief finding 3) as the member's proof that it held state s_N: "the only proof a member held a state is its own act naming the round". After this, naming the round proves it saw *a* view rendered from s_N, not that it saw what s_N contained; the part it is most likely to have missed is the reveal. The charter-agenda spike's informed-acts property (its I1, which the walking skeleton says is "enforceable exactly that way") is weaker than claimed at the one place the harness misbehaves.
- The abandoned thread writes a `head-observed` witness record for a view nobody saw, and a `wait-returned` receipt. Harmless for tamper-evidence (a head is a head), but `witness()`'s docstring, "the chain head this process is about to show a member", is not what it records.
- With the launchers as shipped this does not occur: codex's `tool_timeout_sec` is raised to 900 and Claude Code's idle timer is kept alive by progress. It occurs the first time a harness or a config does not do that, and the spike's own §2.1 is the argument that this will happen.

What I would try: the server already has the shape of the fix in its own principle ("a returned result is not a delivered result"). The cursor is the one piece of delivery state that is still advanced on *sending*. Either advance it on the member's next call (the reply says "events a–b"; the next call is the acknowledgement), or let a new wait on the same seat and connection supersede any older one, which returns without rendering. Both are small; the second also stops the ghost witness and receipt.

### 2.4 The driver has no cap on turns per round (test 5; design hazard, not seen in the runs)

`members/driver.py` launches a turn for a seat whenever a round is open for it, it is not sealed, and no turn is busy, polling every second. A turn that ends without a submission is therefore relaunched about a second later, for as long as the round stays open. Test 5 puts a fake `claude` on PATH that emits a session id and a result ("I will wait for the next round.") and never touches the server: 16 turns in 8.1 s for two rounds (2 seats × ~4 turns per round), ended only by backstop → stall → gone → inquorate → adjourned. In `trial-5c` every turn acted, so this was never exercised; with real turns at ~5 s and $0.003–0.005 each (`trial-5c`'s per-turn figures), a seat whose model ends its turn without acting costs on the order of $0.05 per minute until two backstops have passed. The same loop fires on a non-zero exit (an API error, a rate limit), since `turn()` records `rc` and relaunches regardless.

What I would try: at most one turn per (seat, round); a second one only when the round changes. The stall path then does what it was built for, and the driver never pays twice for the same question.

### 2.5 The principal is asserted, not attested (test 6; a wording defect with a design consequence)

`principal_for` takes a launcher-declared principal, else codex's `_meta.sessionId`, else agy's `root_conversation_id`, else the parent process. The first three are strings the client sends. Test 6: two server processes for one seat, both given `_meta.sessionId = "01a1a3-the-real-codex-session"`; both are held under one `codex-session:…` principal (one `seat-held` receipt, no refusal), and the second's `council_submit` is accepted. That is the `trial-3` race back, for any client that presents the holder's `_meta`, and the holder's `_meta` is in the holder's own `server.jsonl` on the same machine.

This is not a defect in what the holder rule was built for (an accidental second session has a different `sessionId`). It is a defect in what the documents say about it: harness-notes' "A server can *attest* caller identity from codex and agy `_meta`", the plan's §3.3 title, and the `attest(...)` name in `server.py` all describe recording a claim as attesting a fact. Locally, with one user, the distinction costs nothing; the plan's own §3.4 (the council directory must move somewhere members cannot write) is the moment it starts to cost, because a daemon that trusts `_meta` for seat identity is a daemon any client can sit on any seat of, given the token. The honest wording is "record what the harness asserts", and seat exclusivity for a remote or multi-user server needs a secret per holder, not an id.

### 2.6 The minutes show a stalled seat as gone (test 4; text/record defect)

Response §4: "The close records `stalled: [...]`, so the minutes say why the seat was not waited for." The event body does carry `stalled`; `view.describe` renders a round-closed as `absent: A (gone: A)` and never mentions it, so `council_record`, `steward minutes` and every member's delta say the seat was gone. The one place the reason survives is the raw event. The first verifier's §4.1 asked for exactly this distinction to be visible.

### 2.7 A `--bg` member on a real council, through the real server (`verify-2/runs/bg-wait-1-haiku/`, `bg-wait-2-sonnet/`)

The spike's only evidence for the backgrounded wait (`probes/b1`) is one haiku session against the probe server with `progress_every=0`. The real server sends a progress notification every 10 s, and harness-notes shows progress resets at least one of Claude Code's timers. So the question the plan's build order rests on, "does a `council_wait` that is sending progress still get backgrounded at 120 s, and does the server's answer wake the idle session", had not been run. `bg-meeting.py` seats seat A as a `claude --bg` session (the launchers' flags: `--strict-mcp-config`, `--setting-sources ""`, `--tools ""`, the four `--allowedTools`, `members/brief.md` unchanged), with the scripted seat B answering the roll only after A has, plus 150 s, then every round promptly. The cwd is the scratchpad directory, outside any git repo, as in `b1`.

**Attempt 1, haiku (`bg-wait-1-haiku/`): the member never reached the council.** The server saw `initialize` and `tools/list` and no tool call in the 330 s before I stopped the session. The transcript shows why. The model's single turn (8.7 s) is a text message containing a hand-written `<invoke:ToolSearch>` with `select:council_wait,council_submit,council_status,council_record`, followed by its own observation that no result came back, and `end_turn`. It then sat idle. The reason is in the session's system prompt: a `--bg` session gets Claude Code's full interactive prompt, which on this machine includes a built-in "Claude in Chrome browser automation" section telling the model it has `mcp__claude-in-chrome__*` tools and to load deferred tools "in ONE ToolSearch call". `--tools ""` removes `ToolSearch`, so the model had instructions to use a tool that did not exist, applied them to the council tools, and fabricated the call. The `-p` members' transcripts (`trial-4c` indep, my re-run's indep) contain neither "Claude in Chrome" nor "ToolSearch"; their `init` message lists exactly the four council tools and one connected server. So **`-p` and `--bg` sessions with identical flags do not get the same prompt**: `--setting-sources ""` and `--strict-mcp-config` govern settings and MCP config, not the harness's built-in integration sections, and those sections reach a `--bg` member. That is a harness fact the plan's step 3 needs and harness-notes does not have. It also means a `--bg` member is not as sandboxed as a `-p` one: it is told it has browser tools (whether they were callable I could not tell from the record; the model did not try them).

Attempt 1 is also, incidentally, a live specimen of §2.1's trigger: a live server, a holder that was never claimed, a model that ended its turn, and a council that would have treated the seat as never-arrived after the 300 s grace.

**Attempt 2, sonnet (`bg-wait-2-sonnet/`): the member reached the council, the wait was backgrounded with progress on, and the member did not go idle.** Timeline from `A/server.jsonl` and the session transcript (times from the server's start):

My orchestrator then crashed on a keyword collision in its own logging (the traceback is `bg-wait-2-sonnet/orchestrator-stderr.txt`; the record's `orchestrator.jsonl` ends at `A-in-r2`, and the fix is marked in `bg-meeting.py`), so seat B stopped answering, the meeting did not reach adjournment, and I stopped the session by hand (`claude stop`). What was measured before that is the whole of what the run was for; what was not measured is the member's behavior over a second backgrounding or at adjournment.

Three results from the two attempts, for the plan's step 3:

1. **The 120 s auto-background happens with progress on.** The plan's assumption holds; `b1`'s `progress_every=0` was not load-bearing.
2. **It is not a wake mechanism by itself.** In `b1` the model went idle because its prompt said "then stop". Under `members/brief.md` the sonnet member treated the backgrounding message as a timeout and opened a new wait, exactly as the brief tells it to. So a `--bg` member under this brief behaves as a blocking client that accumulates one backgrounded wait per 120 s of silence, each of which returns when the round opens, each advancing the cursor (§2.3), each delivered as a notification. Over a ten-minute silence that is five ghost waits plus the live one. The debrief's "it calls `council_wait`, goes idle, and the server's 'your turn' wakes it" needs a brief that says *end your turn after a backgrounding*, which no brief in the spike says, and which then has to coexist with the same brief's "call `council_wait` again" for real timeouts. Which message the member is looking at is the member's problem to solve, with text, each time.
3. **A `--bg` session is not configured the way a `-p` session is, with the same flags.** Attempt 2's first API request (the harness's own `api-request-shape` record) carried **29 tools**: the four council tools, `EndConversation`, `advisor`, `DeferredToolPlaceholder`, and **22 `mcp__claude-in-chrome__*` tools**, under `--strict-mcp-config --tools ""`. Attempt 1's first request carried **0 tools** (a startup race: the council server had listed its tools 0.2 s earlier), which is what left haiku with nothing to call. A `-p` member's `init` lists one server and four tools. So "no global configuration is touched" is true of what the launchers *write* and not of what a `--bg` member *gets*: it is seated with this machine's browser automation, and the plan's §3.4 sandboxing argument (sealing holds because launchers withhold tools) does not hold for the `--bg` adapter as launched here. Whether some flag closes this I did not look for; the finding is that the launchers' flags do not.

Costs were not collected for the `--bg` sessions (no `result` message in a background session's transcript); the plan's "at client cost" for this adapter is still an inference.

## 4. On the design, beyond the defects

Two observations that are judgments, not measurements.

- **The stall mechanism and the "named round" proof pull in opposite directions.** The spike's strongest design principle, stated in three places, is that the only fact the server may rely on is a member's own act. The revision added two facts the server now infers about members: that a silent seat has stopped looping (§2.1–2.2), and that a rendered view was seen (§2.3, already there, now load-bearing). Both inferences fail exactly in the harness conditions the spike itself documented. The common repair is the same discipline applied one level down: a stalled seat is un-stalled by its own act (any call), and a cursor is advanced by the member's own next act, never by the server's send. That would make the two new mechanisms obey the principle that motivated them.
- **The plan's build order leans on the `--bg` wake, and §2.7 is the first run of it in the server's own configuration.** Whatever §2.7 shows, the member that wakes from a backgrounded wait is the member that §2.1 refuses to wait for if it was idle through two backstops; the two findings meet on the plan's step 3. Step 1 ("port `core` + `store` as they now stand") would port both defects with the acceptance suite that does not see them.
