# Debrief — for Joseph

*Revision 2, written after an independent verifier's pass (`de-novo-feedback-1.md`) and my repairs (`response-to-feedback-1.md`), by re-reading the corrected files rather than from memory. Where the first debrief said something the verifier showed false, this one says so. Pre-spike vocabulary: council, charter, agenda, member, round, report, parent, veto.*

## The problem you set

You wanted to know what a council's charter and agenda are as things the server loads: what has to be written down to seat one, how it comes into being, how it takes a question to a recorded decision and reports to you, how it grows, and which parts of Robert's Rules still mean anything when the members are agents and time is counted in turns. You weren't sure whether to start narrow (a 2–3 agent council with one decision) or broad (the whole book, cut down), or whether those are one road or two.

## What I did, in order

1. Read the whole model and the parts of the book it leans on, and the estate pieces a council plugs into. Wrote my own map before reading the coordinator's drafts.
2. Took the hardest thing first: the book says a group deciding in writing is not a deliberative assembly. You said mid-spike that you care about the principles the book surfaces, not its labels, so I used that passage for the principle under it — **a decision belongs to the body only if every member could hear every argument and answer it before the vote closed** — and listed what being in one room provides toward that, and what a turn-based round must provide instead.
3. Wrote the charter as a real artifact, twice (a 3-seat quick council; a 5-seat standing council), with a checker; then the clock-word translation, a motion-by-motion survey, your two in-meeting acts as event streams, a full walkthrough on a real vsect specimen, eleven adversarial cases, and a list of defects in the model.
4. An independent verifier then read everything and checked it against the book and the model's expressions. It was right on every point of fact. I repaired what could be repaired, declared what couldn't, and re-did the parts that were wrong.

## What I found, and what survived verification

**A charter is an instance, not a type.** The model's configurations (committee, small board, …) are types. What no type carries — who sits, under what grant, on what question, with what instructions, under which pinned version of the rules, and how turns and time work — is the charter. The rule for what goes in it is vsect's: **declare only what the engine cannot compute.** Survived.

**For a quick council, the charter and the agenda are the same document.** The book's *special* committee has one task and dies when it reports; its agenda is its referral, and it can't change it. A *standing* committee has an order of business and adopts an agenda per sitting. Your two routes are this distinction. **One format expresses both, on one engine** — that survived. What did *not* survive is the way I first put it: "one road, differing in one field and two lists." The two kinds also differ in chair mode, quorum basis, session structure and time mappings, and — more to the point — a quick council doesn't *grow into* a standing one by editing; the book has a standing committee constituted by a separate, heavier act of the parent, and it bars a quick council inside a standing one's class. So: one format, two kinds, and growth within a kind is widening a list; growth between kinds is your act.

**"Growing is widening the list" is true for motions, after four model fixes, and false for two other things.** Turning a motion on or off leaves nothing dangling. But the verifier found that the model's own small-board configuration can't actually state a question — its structural patch is written in English, not in the model's expression language — so no council on that chain runs until that's compiled (the charters now carry the compiled form). And two kinds of growth aren't list edits at all: new records (a committee record for subcommittees, a sitting record for standing councils) and an executor for acts that fork an agent or hand out a credential.

**The round replaces the room — under a stronger rule than I first wrote.** My first formulation allowed several acts to be admitted in one round. The contention-rounds spike showed that breaks the principle: a member who moved "postpone" without having seen the amendment admitted in the same round acted blind. So the charters now declare that spike's rule — **one floor-taking act per round, and every transition that opens a window is followed by a round** — and the walkthrough was redone under it. Under that rule the room's ten mechanisms translate, four things must be written in the charter because they can't be derived (how presence is established; who wins among simultaneous claims; whether a motion is stated the moment it's made; what the timeout does), and — this is new from verification — **two things the room gives are deliberately dropped**, with the cost named: you cannot interrupt a speech *in progress* (you object to it in the next round), and you do not hear others' votes before casting yours (that's the point of sealing). Two more the verifier raised are carried by rules: a vote may not introduce reasons nobody can answer (reasons are given in debate, and a vote points to them), and when your token budget cuts debate off, one reply round follows so the last argument isn't unanswerable.

**The honest status of "is it a meeting."** I first wrote that the book "left a slot open" for turn-based rules. It didn't: paragraph 9:34 says in so many words that a written process, whatever the technology, is "consultative" and "foreign to the deliberative process," and for such a committee the book allows a report only of what *every* member agreed to. So the claim is now stated the only honest way: **by the book's letter, a turn-based council may report only unanimity; by the principle, a council that runs rounds under the rule above is claiming the majority tier as a declared departure.** The charters say that where they claim it. The two-tier result still stands as a design fact: polling agents separately is valid exactly when unanimous; running rounds is what earns majority decisions — by principle.

**Your veto and today's delegation both have names in the book.** A parent can only *ratify* an act taken by staff "in excess of their instructions or authority — including … approval obtained separately" from members. That's what you've been doing after the fact. A council produces a *report* you adopt or decline. Survived.

**Your two in-meeting acts.** Adding a member mid-meeting: the book lets a referral authorize a committee "even to add to its own membership," and it's a question of the assembly's organization. A research subcommittee that reports back: the book's breakout groups "report in the nature of debate" — but, the verifier pointed out, they meet *during a recess*; running the inquiry while debate continues has no precedent, so the charter declares it as a departure. The warrant for staffing it with expert forks is the referral's power to "employ an expert consultant," not (as I first wrote) a privilege question — without a grant, a council can only ask you. Both acts are now proper motions that go through the model's normal gate, so they inherit the quorum, floor and dilatory checks; my first version let two seats co-opt a third while the council was inquorate. That was the verifier's most consequential catch.

**The vote.** A sealed, per-seat, revealed-together vote is the book's **signed ballot** (45:46), not — as I first wrote — something the book "could never have." Its secrecy purpose is dropped by design, and the cost is named: per-seat votes you read are a channel for voting toward you. The untaken option (seal per-seat votes from you until you've acted on the recommendation) is recorded. The prevailing-side rule for reconsideration *can* become enforceable, but only after a guard in the model gains its committee branch — available, not delivered.

**The quorum mistake.** I set the quick council's quorum at all three seats "because every perspective matters." With the timeout rule that makes a slow seat absent, that gave every seat a veto by silence. Quorum is now the book's majority, and "every perspective heard" is a rule on the final vote instead (it's out of order until every seat has sat through a debate round). And the timeout does decide something: presence, which feeds quorum and thresholds. I'd written that it "decides nothing."

**The referral.** I had pre-drafted one side of the specimen ("keep the lines") as the resolution, so "remove" could only arrive as a reversing amendment. The book's device for a choice is a **blank** — "Resolved, That vsect ____ the computed lines" — with both alternatives as proposals and neither privileged. The charter and walkthrough now use it. The seat "perspectives" I wrote were also positions in disguise; they now say what a seat looks at, not what it concludes.

**What has no meaning anymore.** Rising votes, the voice→rising→counted ladder, tellers, division of the assembly — all exist to make an uncounted vote conclusive. Stand-at-ease under a mechanical chair. Call of the house. Survived.

**The walking-skeleton's missing `amend`.** Members routed around it three ways; the third (reject your own motion, then move the agreed text) would be sent by the model to a "same question?" ruling and likely refused. Survived, with the hedge the verifier asked for.

## Outcomes

- Two charters, revision 2, well-formed against the model's namespace and types; a checker that now refuses the verifier's seven-way broken charter (my first checker passed it).
- A mechanical chair for ≤ 3 seats, with a declared sequencing rule — without it the model's own expression would make the chair put every question the instant it was stated.
- Fifteen model defects or gaps, four of them real defects: the committee-of-the-whole overlay (read literally) turns off the chair, omits the act that makes motions, and omits the lone objection to unanimous consent; the small-board chain's structural patches are prose; added acts could bypass the model's gate.
- A walkthrough of twenty-two rounds for a three-seat council deciding one amended recommendation with a live inquiry (I had said thirteen; I was counting rows). That is the price of one floor act per round; standing policies ("pass unless X") are the mitigation.

## Tangents worth your attention

- **Three identities for a forked seat.** The book uses one notion of "person" for counting, for accountability for one's own past vote, and for continuity of judgment. A re-forked seat splits them. The charters now declare them separately; the useful consequence is that a re-fork "did not vote" on earlier questions and so may move to reconsider them under the book's committee rule.
- **The corridor is about the world, not memory.** The book's reason for not binding a later session is "the freedom of each new session." For agents what moves between sittings is main, the facts, the papers a question rests on — so a standing council's corridor should close when the record the question depends on lapses (vsect's sense). Form of that rule: my guess.
- **Executive session for agents** (unchanged): hiding debate from *later agents* might protect later councils' independence. Disposition territory; recorded only.
