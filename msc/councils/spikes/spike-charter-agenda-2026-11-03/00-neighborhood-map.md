# Neighborhood map — written before reading the coordinator's drafts

*Spike: executable charter and agenda for agentic councils. Started 2026-11-03. This file is my own map of the ground, formed from the model (`../../model/`), the book (`../../RONR-12/`, primaries read whole for every paragraph I cite), the ROADMAP, and the estate pieces the brief named. Written **before** `initial-thoughts.md` / `mechanics.md`, on the coordinator's suggestion, so that what follows can be checked against an unanchored read. Labels: **[read]** = I read the primary whole; **[model]** = the model file says it; **[guess]** = mine, unverified.*

## 1. What the model already is, and what it is not

- The model is one machine + a configuration layer. `council.json` is explicit that a *configuration* is a point in a seven-axis space plus an overlay (param / patch / disable / add_action / restrict_actions / note) applied **at load**, before any evaluation. [model]
- A configuration is a **type**, not an **instance**. Nothing in `council.json` carries a roll, a grant, a referred question, a reporting path, a time bound, or a pinned model hash. `schema.json`'s `ctx.config` holds `{configuration: id, <params>}` only. The instance-level data lives nowhere yet. [model]
- `gaps.json` names exactly the instance-level holes: `meeting-session-objects` (meetings and sessions are ids, no kind / call / formation rule), `committee-identity` (no Committee record; `committed` does not say to whom; `subordinate_to` untyped). Both are flagged as "first thing phase 5 needs". [model]
- The ROADMAP's Phase 5 already states, in prose, nearly the whole answer: "A council comes into being by charter: rule-stack instantiation, council-type configuration, initial roll, role assignments, quorum and meeting/notice provisions, the charter's own amendment rule — and a pinned version hash of the model data." And: "The referral or establishing act *is* a fiat charter (13:8 carries composition, selection, chairmanship, instructions, powers, meeting-mode): parent act → child council.json instance." [read] So the question I was handed is not "invent the charter" but "**make that prose executable, and find out where it breaks when the members are agents and time is logical.**"

## 2. The book's own charter vocabulary (what a referral may carry)

- 13:8(a–d) [read]: the motion to commit names the method (which committee / committee of the whole / informal), the committee by name or by size + selection method or by naming members, and **instructions** that are "binding on the committee": when to meet, how to consider, whether to employ an expert consultant, when to report; it may give "full power", authorize spending, "or even to add to its own membership". Majority adopts it even when the instructions suspend or conflict with rules of order that would otherwise apply to the committee's meetings.
- 9:35 [read]: a committee not established by bylaws may be authorized to hold electronic meetings "by the motion establishing the particular committee, or by instructions included in a motion referring an individual matter to the committee". 9:36 lists what those rules should cover: equipment and malfunctions, quorum determination, point-of-order-doubting-quorum, seeking recognition, submitting motions in writing, taking and verifying votes.
- 13:22 [read]: subsequent instructions by majority at any time before the report, even at another session. 13:23 / 50:14 [read]: the appointing power fills vacancies and removes/replaces.
- 50:3, 50:10, 50:23, 50:30 [read]: special committees go out of existence on presenting the final report; all meetings of a special committee are one session; "rise" = adjourn sine die; a special committee expires with the body that appointed it unless appointed to report later. 50:8 [read]: a standing committee with standing authority to act, or automatic referral of a class of business, must be constituted by bylaw or by a resolution that is a special rule of order (notice + 2/3, or majority of entire membership).
- 50:18 [read]: a committee for deliberation or investigation "should represent, as far as possible, all points of view … The usefulness of the committee will be greatly impaired … if any important faction … is not represented." This is Joseph's diversity-of-perspective rule stated by the book.
- 50:25–26 [read]: 49:21 relaxations apply unless instructed otherwise; no close/limit-debate in committees; "A committee may not adopt its own rules except as authorized in the rules of the society or in instructions given to the committee by its parent."
- 51:2 [read]: a report may contain only what a majority agreed at a proper meeting with quorum — except a committee that cannot meet may report what *every* member agreed to. 51:7: detailed-report form = charge / facts / findings / recommendations. 51:36–43: a referred question re-pends automatically on report; the question is always put on adoption. 51:64–71: minority report by leave (majority, undebatable), concur-except signing.
- 52:9–10 [read]: the committee-of-the-whole whitelist and its consequences; 52:15–16: rise to request instructions; "ask permission to sit again". 52:28 [read]: breakout groups report back "after it has been reconvened and the matter under consideration is again pending. These reports are in the nature of debate."
- 36:4(7) [read]: discharge by bare majority if the committee missed its prescribed reporting time, or while a partial report is under consideration. 36:7: instruct-to-report-by-time is majority if no time was set.
- 19:7 [read]: privileges of the assembly include "its organization or existence". 9:24: executive session is a question of privilege.
- 41:57 [read]: *the* special order for the meeting — taken up right after minutes, outranks every other order.
- 53:9 [read]: the call of a mass meeting has bylaws force: bounds germaneness and defines membership. 9:13–16: a special meeting transacts only what its call specifies (subsidiary / incidental / privileged still in order); action outside the call must be ratified.

## 3. The confrontation the brief did not name

- 1:1 bullet 2 and its footnote 1 [read]: a deliberative assembly "meets in a single room or area or under equivalent conditions of opportunity for **simultaneous aural communication**"; the footnote: "A group that attempts to conduct the deliberative process in writing—such as by postal mail, electronic mail (e-mail), or facsimile transmission (fax)—**does not constitute a deliberative assembly**. When making decisions by such means, many situations unprecedented in parliamentary law will arise, and many of its rules and customs will not be applicable." 49:16 [read]: separate approval "even from every member" is not the board's approval "since the members lacked the opportunity to mutually debate and decide the matter as a deliberative body."
- Joseph's council is written and turn-based. On the book's own terms it is not a deliberative assembly. Either the spike finds the functional thing simultaneity protects and shows the round discipline supplies it (so the charter's electronic-meeting rules make the council an assembly "under equivalent conditions"), or it concedes that RONR applies only persuasively (1:2, 2:19) and says which rules fall. This is the hardest part and I will take it first.

## 4. The estate pieces, as constraints on the artifact

- Decision authority (vivarium `norm-decision-authority.md`; udon `adr/decision-authority.md`) [read]: `council` is a **role in `deciders`**, standing comes from a grant; an unacted council decision is `decided-by: delegated`; `council-accepted` sits between proposed and decided and never upgrades `:by`. So a council's report must land as a record whose deciders are the members under a named grant, and whose `decided-by` stays `delegated` until Joseph acts. The grant slug is therefore charter data.
- Agent identity [read]: who acted is read from `CC_AGENT`, never typed; forks show `type=fork`. The roll must bind a seat to a harness key (or, for non-Claude members, an MCP endpoint and a declared identity, `identity: declared`).
- Source experts [read]: members will often be forks of frozen expert sessions; a fork is one task; nothing returns to the expert. Forking is how a council member is instantiated *and* how a research subcommittee could be staffed.
- vsect [read]: the event trail is content-addressed and never edited; claims are intents visible across worktrees; checks bind to versions. The council's event log should have the same shape (append-only, hashed) so minutes are a projection (48:2: minutes record what was *done*).

## 5. Where I expect the question to crack open (hypotheses, pre-reading)

- H1 [guess]: For a **special** committee the charter and the agenda are **one artifact** — the referral. For a **standing** committee they are two: the standing charter and the stream of referrals. Joseph's two routes (narrow quick-council vs. broad RONR) are the book's own special/standing distinction, run on the same engine.
- H2 [guess]: A quick council's agenda is literally `the-special-order-for-meeting` (41:57) with one item, and its exit is the committee-of-the-whole's `move-rise` pattern ("the committee's only exit").
- H3 [guess]: The chair's deterministic duties (state, put, announce, recognize-in-order, announce-scheduled-item) can be performed by the server; its judgment duties route to the assembly through the adjudication machine's existing `submitted` path (23:18–21). A **mechanical chair** is a configuration, not a new mechanism. Cost: the chair's sua-sponte duties (dilatory, decorum) are lost unless a member raises them.
- H4 [guess]: Almost all RONR timers are event-anchored already; only the calendar-bound anchors (`same-day`, `next-business-day`, `quarterly-interval`) need a logical-time mapping, and the charter declares it (sitting, round, token budget).
- H5 [guess]: A round-based vote is at once a roll-call (recorded, 45:45) and a ballot (independent — nobody sees another's vote before casting). RONR could never have both. Same for prevailing-side (RONR-OBSERVATIONS §3): enforceable for the first time. *(Revision-2 note: wrong on both counts — the book's signed ballot [45:46] is exactly this, and the prevailing-side rule needs a guard change the model lacks; feedback-1 §§3.3–3.4.)*
- H6 [guess]: Joseph's "research subcommittee without interrupting" is not subsidiary commit (that suspends the pending question) but either (a) a question of privilege of the assembly (19:7, "organization") that pushes a context, adopts a main motion creating a subcommittee on a *different* question, pops, and the subcommittee's return is "in the nature of debate" (52:28) — an advisory input, gating nothing; or (b) a charter-granted standing power. "Add a member before continuing" is (a) under 19:7 "organization" plus 13:8(d)'s "add to its own membership" authority.
- H7 [guess]: Some RONR machinery has **no** meaning for agents and should be disabled by the charter, demonstrably: division of the assembly (29) presupposes an uncounted vote; the inconclusive-vote ladder (4:50–51); ballot secrecy as a *separate method* (every round-vote is already blind); tellers (45:26–30); call of the house. Each needs a real attempt at a meaning before the no-go lands.
