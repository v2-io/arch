# Defects and gaps in the model that the charter work ran into

*Things in `../../model/` this spike hit (F1–F9) and the verifier found (F10–F15; `de-novo-feedback-1.md` §§3–4, 9–10). Each was checked against the file text and, where a rule is involved, the book. For the integrator; not landed by this spike (write-scope: this directory only). `CHANGELOG.md` notes the third audit was integrated by its own auditor; F1–F2 are in that audit's additions. F1, F2, F10 and F14 are defects; the rest are gaps or costs.*

**F1 — `restrict_actions` does not say whose actions it restricts.** `council.json overlay_mechanism.ops.restrict_actions`: "every motion/action not listed is disabled". The committee-of-the-whole configuration's `plus` list contains only member actions (`second`, `claim-floor`, `speak-in-debate`, `vote`, `change-vote`, `demand-division`, `raise-point-of-order`, `appeal`, `request`, `put-ignored-question`). Read literally, `state-question`, `put-question`, `announce-result`, `recognize`, `rule-out-of-order`, `respond-to-inquiry`, `declare-recess-or-adjournment`, `announce-scheduled-item`, `declare-quorum-absent` and the secretary's `read-paper` are all disabled, and the configuration cannot state or put a question. The intended semantics are almost certainly "member-initiated acts only; duties are not options" (`actions.json chair_duty_note`). Fix: give the op a `scope` field (this spike's charters set `scope: member-initiated`) and say in the op's desc that chair and secretary duties are never whitelisted.

**F2 — the committee-of-the-whole whitelist omits `action/move` and rights-critical member acts.** The largest omission (feedback §10): `plus` lacks `move`, the action that offers every motion, so read with F1's literalism COTW members cannot move the `amend` their whitelist allows. Also not in `plus`: `object-to-unanimous-consent` (the lone objection — on the ROADMAP's phase-7 pin list), `withdraw-own-motion`, `demand-separate-vote`, `give-previous-notice`, `object-to-standing-at-ease`, plus the moot `call-up-reconsider` (no reconsider in COTW, 37:35 last sentence) and `nominate`. The first is serious: under 52:9 a COTW still does most things by unanimous consent, and an unobjectable consent is a vote. Fix: add `action/object-to-unanimous-consent`, `action/withdraw-own-motion`, `action/demand-separate-vote` to the COTW `plus`; better, derive the rights-critical floor of every whitelist from the model (the ROADMAP already says the pin list must be computed, not curated) so no whitelist can drop it.

**F3 — `add_action` cannot declare Standard Descriptive Characteristics.** `actions.json` records carry `roles`, `params`, `expr`, `cite`, `note`; motions in `registry.json` carry `second`, `debatable`, `amendable`, `vote`, `reconsider`, `interrupt`. An overlay that adds a motion-like act (COTW's `move-rise`; this spike's three) has nowhere to put its threshold or debatability except a prose `note` — the "prose wearing an expression costume" the INTERFACE bans. Fix: an `add_motion` op whose record is registry-shaped, or SDC fields on `add_action` records; `check-charter.py` already warns when they are absent.

**F4 — `ScheduledItem.hour` and `before` admit only clock times; and the charters' time and event vocabulary is new.** Event-anchored scheduling ("postpone until S reports") needs either `before(now, event-ref)` ≡ `not event-occurred(event-ref, session)` or `anchor` resolving event refs (03 §4). The operator set is closed, so this is an INTERFACE change to record — together with every per-council redefinition of `within-quarterly`, `same-day`, `anchor` and `now` (03 §5), the event-scope token definitions, and the events the charters introduce (`charter-loaded`, `seat-credentialed`, `asked`, `round-closed`, `member-seated`, `consultant-reported`; the council's emission is the model's own `reported` — feedback-2 §4).

**F5 — `speech_limit` is `{max_speeches, max_minutes}`; `Member.speech_counters` has `minutes_used`.** For agents the unit is tokens. The rename is cosmetic, but 15:16's deadline-vs-budget closure depends on which unit an order is in; the charter's `logical_time.minutes` mapping is where that is declared. Suggest the schema name the unit generically and the configuration declare it.

**F6 — `Question.effect` cannot say "recommendation".** A committee's disposition vote adopts a *recommendation*, not the question [52:8, 51:36–43]. The lifecycle's `adopted` state and the `Effect` record have no field distinguishing "adopted as this body's act" from "adopted as this body's recommendation to its parent". The ROADMAP's advisory-input class covers the parent's side; the child's side needs one flag (`effect.kind: act | recommendation`) so the report projection knows what to emit and the parent's engine knows what re-pends.

**F7 — `gaps.json committee-identity` and `meeting-session-objects` are prerequisites, confirmed.** Both charters needed `ctx.subcommittees` (declared as a schema extension, flagged by the checker) and a sitting/session record. `gaps.json` already lists `Committee {id, kind, roll, charter_ref, parent}`; this spike adds only `state` and the `Sitting` record: `Committee {id, kind, roll, charter_ref, parent, state}`, `Sitting {id, session_ref, call_ref, kind: regular|adjourned|special}`.

**F8 — `adjudication.json privilege-admissible` has `default: null`.** Under a mechanical chair every raised question of privilege therefore *must* become a submitted ruling round. Correct for the book (the chair must rule, 19:2); it means Route A of 05 §1 always costs a round, which is why the charter-granted acts exist. Not a defect; a cost to name where the chair mode is chosen.

**F9 — a checker worth keeping as acceptance, now that it refuses the verifier's mutant.** Revision 1 of `tools/check-charter.py` certified only reference resolution and passed a charter with seven deliberate breakages (feedback §5). Revision 2 checks parameter types and enums, patch surfaces, allowed∩disabled, declared params behind every `ctx.config` reference, the 9:36 keys and the clock fields; `tools/mutant.py` reproduces the seven and the checker reports all seven. Both charters are well-formed against the model's namespace and types (the quick council whitelists 21 of 31 registry motions plus three added, the standing 26 plus three). It still evaluates nothing. Suggest folding its charter rules into `tools/sweep.py`.

**F10 — the small-board chain's structural patches are prose, so no small-board-derived configuration can state a question** (feedback §3.1). `state-question` requires state `seconded`; small-board's fix is `"surface": "lifecycle.transitions: moved -> pending", "replace": "on stated directly …"` — English, not an expression. Likewise the chair-votes-on-all-questions patch to `protocol.decorum` is prose while `vote`'s compiled expression still limits the chair to result-affecting votes, which cannot be evaluated under sealed casting. Fix: compile both (the charters show the form: an added transition `moved —stated→ pending`; `state-question` admitting `moved | seconded`; `vote` ≡ `voting-in-progress` when `chair_participates`). This is F1's class of defect on the chain every council actually loads.

**F11 — `put-question` has no timeliness conjunct.** Its third disjunct (no debate claims) is satisfied the instant a question is stated, before any member has been asked (feedback §3.2); under `chair_duty_note` a mechanical chair would then be *obliged* to put every question at once. The charters declare a duty-sequencing rule (chair duties fire at round boundaries; `put-question` only after a closed round with zero debate claims following a stating or speech), using the note's own hook that sequencing is stage-5 policy. The model owes the conjunct, or an event (`round-closed`) the expression can reference.

**F12 — `guard/mover-was-prevailing-side` has no committee branch and keeps the assertion path on recorded votes** (feedback §3.3). Its desc says "committee: anyone not on the losing side [37:35]"; its expr has no such disjunct, so an abstaining or absent seat is routed to `prevailing-side-claim` (default `null` → a ruling round), and a recorded losing-side voter is never refused outright. Fix: add `member ∉ losing_members` under committee body kinds; make the recorded case exclusive of assertion; record presence per event (F15). Until then the "enforceable prevailing side" of `RONR-OBSERVATIONS.md` §3 is available, not delivered.

**F13 — `Vote.method` has no value for a signed ballot** (feedback §3.4). The charters' vote is the book's signed ballot [45:46]: cast sealed, recorded per member. `ballot` makes `change-vote` inadmissible and leaves `prevailing_members` unrecorded; `roll-call` fits the expressions but is not what 45:45 describes. The charters classify as `roll-call`; the enum wants `signed-ballot`.

**F14 — `add_action` records bypass `move`'s protective conjuncts** (feedback §4). An added act with its own expr skips quorum, floor, rank, dilatory and governing-rules checks; revision 1's three added acts could be adopted inquorate. Fix: an `add_motion` op whose record is registry-shaped with an applicability row, reached through `action/move` — the charters use it, and the checker warns on any `add_action`.

**F15 — per-round presence history is not recorded** (`gaps.json presence-history`, confirmed load-bearing). The threshold drops of 35:2(7)/37:35(3), the `every-seat-heard` instruction, retroactive voiding of inquorate action [40:12], and the prevailing-side fix all need it. One event per round naming the present seats would supply it — and the asked/answered record the `every-seat-asked` instruction needs, and seat-time for the no-vote-on-pending-questions rule for co-opted seats.

**F16 — the model has no schema for blanks or proposals** (feedback-2 §3). `fill-blank-proposal` is a registry record whose one-per-member limit and ordering rules are prose; `schema.json` has no Blank or Proposal type; `actions.json` has no action for making or putting a proposal; `gaps.json` lists the ordering as annotation-grade. A proposal is not a Question, so `reconsider`'s applicability row cannot target a vote that filled a blank. Until compiled, a charter should not route a choice through the blank device; revision 3's charters refer the subject and let the council draft [51:4].

**F17 — the main-motion applicability row has no scope bar.** A committee's referral (or a standing council's class of business) bounds what main motions are in order [9:15, 53:9 by analogy, 36:2], but `precedence.json`'s main-motion row carries only the no-question-pending, substantially-same and decided-this-session bars; nothing compiled refuses an out-of-scope motion (feedback-2 §9). The charters patch the row with an added `out_of_order_when` using the model's `germane` predicate against the referral; a referral-scoped predicate of its own would be cleaner.

**Note on the ops the charters use.** `declare_param`, `add_motion`, `patch` with `add`, and `restrict_actions.scope` are proposed here (F1/F3/F10/F14) and not in `council.json`; the checker's result line says so. A loader built from `council.json` as written rejects both charters until they land.
