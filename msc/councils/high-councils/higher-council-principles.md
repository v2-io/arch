## 1. The mechanisms

### 1.1 Unity of purpose comes before the meeting, and it gates the agenda

Every council exists for a purpose its members already share, and the purpose is not up for debate in the meeting. Ballard turns that into an admission test for agenda items:

> "If an agenda item can't logically and naturally be linked to one of those great and wonderful eternal pursuits—without having to be stretched beyond recognition in order to fit—then perhaps it does not belong on the agenda."

RONR asks whether an amendment is germane to the motion pending. This asks whether an item is germane to the council's purpose, and it changes how members hold their seats: "our council meetings are about duties and responsibilities, not turf."

Unity of purpose is also what lets opinions differ safely: "If we are one in purpose, spirit, principle, and faith, then it doesn't really matter if we are always of the same opinion. Opinions change and can be easily altered by time, experience, and circumstance."

Joseph's highlights carry the same structure. Mosiah 18:21: "that there should be no contention one with another, but that they should look forward with one eye." Mosiah 25:22: "notwithstanding there being many churches they were all one church." These describe many units that are one body because they share one purpose.

### 1.2 One presider answers for the decision; everyone else counsels

> "The council is not a democratic forum. There are no veto overrides, and there is no majority rule."

> "Functioning successfully as a council doesn't mean making group decisions."

> "Decision making isn't the primary responsibility of counselors. Counselors are called to do just that—to provide counsel—as well as to assist, strengthen, and support. Their role is to participate—actively and candidly—in the decision-making process, to support and sustain all council decisions, and to execute their implementation through their respective organizations."

The current Handbook keeps this, and goes further about what a decision is (4.4.3):

> "After the discussion, the leader may either decide on a course of action or postpone a decision while seeking additional information and guidance. The decision should be informed by the discussion and confirmed by the Spirit. The council process helps bring about inspired decisions that go beyond a leader's own best judgment. The leader may also refer the matter to a different council."

The last sentences matter for design. A decision is not the leader's choice with advice attached. The council process produces something that goes beyond any one judgment, the leader's included, and the leader is responsible for confirming it. A ward council "does not make major decisions without the bishop" (29.2.5), so the accountable officer's presence is a quorum condition for major decisions.

The presider is not above the council's purpose. D&C 58:20, from Joseph's highlights: "Let no man think he is ruler; but let God rule him that judgeth, according to the counsel of his own will, or, in other words, him that counseleth or sitteth upon the judgment seat."

### 1.3 The presider's obligations restrain him

**Bring vision, not necessarily a plan:**

> "That vision doesn't necessarily have to include every detail of what we want to do. But if the council is going to arrive at any meaningful decisions, the leader needs to know where we want to go and what we want to have happen. Without that leadership and sense of vision, how will the council ever know when an appropriate decision has been made?"

**Speak last.** Ballard:

> "It is usually helpful to hear other opinions before offering your own. Too often, when a leader expresses his or her opinion first, the discussions conclude prematurely."

The Handbook (4.4.3):

> "A leader guides the council's discussions. However, he or she should listen more than talk. When a council leader shares his or her perspective too early, it can inhibit the contributions of others."

**Ask, and listen.** "You really listen to what your counselors and other council members are saying and feeling, and … you ask meaningful, penetrating questions when you don't understand their perspective."

**Go looking for the voices nobody asks for.** In the book these are the sisters. The underlying principle is that the presider answers for any perspective the council structurally fails to hear. The Handbook generalizes it: "The diverse backgrounds, ages, experiences, and viewpoints of council members enrich the council." Joseph's Unity tag carries a small exemplar, Joseph Smith to a discouraged boy in Zion's Camp: "Is there no place for you, my boy? If not, we must make one."

**Founding story.** Ballard ran a training exercise many times, giving a simulated ward council a problem family. Every bishop led the same way: "Here's the problem, and here's what I think we should do to solve it." Then he made assignments. Told to try again and ask first, the council opened up: "it was like opening the floodgates of heaven."

A stake president's account says the same: "It occurred to us that we had the habit of telling the high council how we were going to do things, as opposed to counseling with them and receiving their ideas and input." Ballard names the cost: high councilors "quickly become 'dry' councilors."

**Honest not-knowing opens the room.** Chapter 9's model ward council stalls after the bishop states the problem: "What would you have us do, Bishop?" His answer: "I don't know," … "I honestly don't have any answers here. I have some thoughts on the matter, but I'm really interested in hearing what you folks have to say."

### 1.4 Every member has two responsibilities

> "All council members have a dual responsibility: not only do they represent the needs and perspectives of the individual organizations they have been called to lead, but each one also serves as a member of the council, sharing equally with the others a sense of stewardship for the success of the Lord's work in that area."

The Handbook (29.2.5) lists the two capacities with the whole first: "As members of the ward council who help bless all ward members," then "As representatives of their organizations."

In the chapter 9 debrief, members speak "from their own perspective as individuals and not just as representatives."

Members are seated by **stewardship**: an ongoing responsibility for some domain that continues outside the meeting. They carry decisions out through their own organizations, and each is "entitled to divine direction within their respective areas of responsibility." Ballard's advice for choosing counselors is to look "for people who are strong where you feel you are weak."

### 1.5 The council seeks unanimity; when it isn't there, it holds over, or the presider decides and all sustain

Presiding quorums require unanimity (D&C 107:27). Ballard: "decisions that lack unanimity are always held over for further thought, prayer, and discussion," sometimes for years. Hinckley: "Out of this very process of men speaking their minds has come a sifting and winnowing of ideas and concepts."

Where time doesn't allow, the presider decides. Members then "support and sustain the decision of the council leader as if it were a unanimous council decision":

> "If we can't find unanimity in the specific decision, we can at least find unanimity in our support of our sustained leader and our desire to see the work of the Lord go forward in a positive, cooperative way."

Faust, quoted by Ballard: "In some legislative assemblies of the world, there are some groups termed the 'loyal opposition.' I find no such principle in the gospel of Jesus Christ."

**An honest way out.** In the stake welfare-budget story, two bishops voted against the budget twice. The first time, the stake president gave them a meeting to pray about it. The second time: "either God is working through us or we are fallen leaders. That means that your choice here is simple: sustain us and this budget, or write a letter to the First Presidency and ask that we be released." Authority stakes itself on being right, and a dissenter's honest move is to escalate the leader's fitness, not to obstruct the decision.

**Unsettledness is a reason to wait.** The Handbook adds this (4.4.3), and it does not require an objection:

> "Sometimes council members may have unsettled feelings about an important decision. When this happens, the leader may wait for another meeting to consider the matter further and seek spiritual confirmation and unity. In some cases, a council member may want to meet with the leader individually to discuss his or her concerns."

Joseph's highlights carry the ground for waiting. Marvin J. Ashton, "Family Communications" (April 1976): "How important it is to have discussion periods ahead of decisions. Jones Stephens wrote, 'I have learned that the head does not hear anything until the heart has listened, and that what the heart knows today the head will understand tomorrow.'" And 3 Ne 17:3: "go ye unto your homes, and ponder upon the things which I have said … and prepare your minds."

### 1.6 How the Twelve decide

This is the most procedural passage in the book: Rulon G. Craven, executive secretary to the Quorum of the Twelve, describing their meetings. In order:

1. **Preparation.** "The agenda is distributed to each member of the Twelve the night before the meeting so that they have an opportunity to read, ponder, and consider each item in preparation for the meeting."
2. **Opening.** "They usually express love and concern for one another." Then an opening prayer.
3. **Presentation.** The President of the Twelve takes each item in turn, "may make some short preparatory comments," and presents it or has a member present it.
4. **Counsel.** Members are "not so much concerned with expressing [their] own point of view as … with listening to the point of view of others." They "rarely interrupt," they "are certainly not 'yes' men," and they "strive to feel the manifestations of the Spirit concerning the item being discussed, which may necessitate a change in their own feelings and thoughts."
5. **Convergence.** "When the President of the Twelve senses a unity taking place concerning the item on the agenda, he may ask for a recommendation, or one of the Twelve may present a recommendation." The recommendation "remarkably summarizes the feelings of the total Council."
6. **Further discussion.** "We have before us a recommendation. Is there any further discussion?" Each member may speak again: "They don't repeat what has already been said; rather, there is an unusual economy of expression in order to ascertain the total views of the Council." The recommendation may be modified.
7. **Ratification.** The recommendation is moved, seconded, and voted.

The motion comes at the end. It ratifies a convergence the discussion has already reached, where RONR opens with it.

### 1.7 An objection has to add light

Joseph Smith, quoted by Ballard: "every man, before he makes an objection to any item that is brought before a council for consideration, should be sure that he can throw light upon the subject rather than spread darkness."

D&C 88:122, in the Handbook's form: "Let one speak at a time and let all listen unto his sayings, that when all have spoken that all may be edified of all, and that every man may have an equal privilege." The book quotes the verse from its start, "Let not all be spokesmen at once."

The member's own condition matters too. Joseph Smith, 1834: "no man is capable of judging a matter, in council, unless his own heart is pure; and that we are frequently so filled with prejudice, or have a beam in our own eye, that we are not capable of passing right decisions."

From Joseph's highlights, the same discipline in other words:
- 2 Tim 2:23–25: "foolish and unlearned questions avoid, knowing that they do gender strifes. And the servant of the Lord must not strive; but be gentle unto all men, apt to teach, patient, In meekness instructing those that oppose themselves."
- Prov 13:10: "Only by pride cometh contention."
- Prov 18:19: "A brother offended is harder to be won than a strong city."
- Jacob 4:14, on plainness: "they despised the words of plainness … and sought for things that they could not understand … which blindness came by looking beyond the mark." This is the scriptural version of the Twelve's "economy of expression."

**Speaking up is a duty.** Joseph's Watchmen tag sits on Ezek 33:6: "But if the watchman see the sword come, and blow not the trumpet, and the people be not warned … his blood will I require at the watchman's hand." D&C 88:81 says the same: "it becometh every man who hath been warned to warn his neighbor." The general conference talk "The Voice of Warning" (April 2017) gives the register: "The motivation for raising the warning voice is love—love of God and love of fellowman. To warn is to care. … It must be clear and sometimes firm." In a council, a member who sees a danger and stays silent has failed the council, even when silence looks like deference.

### 1.8 Correction runs both ways

The book's sharpest instance: a bishop asks his ward council how to improve reverence, and the Primary president names him. "It's you, Bishop." "When others in the ward council nodded in agreement, the bishop thanked her and asked for recommendations."

From Joseph's highlights, Joseph Smith, *Teachings*, ch. 34:

> "You know that it is my duty to admonish you, when you do wrong. This liberty I shall always take, and you shall have the same privilege. I take the liberty to admonish you, because of my birthright; and I grant you the privilege, because it is my duty to be humble, and receive rebuke and instruction from a brother, or a friend."

The same chapter carries a presider's repair: "I have sometimes spoken too harshly from the impulse of the moment, and inasmuch as I have wounded your feelings, brethren, I ask your forgiveness, for I love you."

Sharpness is bounded by what it is for. 2 Cor 13:10: "sharpness, according to the power which the Lord hath given me to edification, and not to destruction."

The presider's side of receiving correction, from Joseph's highlights, is Pahoran answering Moroni's unjust letter (Alma 61:9): "in your epistle you have censured me, but it mattereth not; I am not angry, but do rejoice in the greatness of your heart. I, Pahoran, do not seek for power, save only to retain my judgment-seat that I may preserve the rights and the liberty of my people."

### 1.9 Something beyond the room can correct both presider and council

In the book, revelation is the actual source of decisions. It can override the council's convergence: Joseph F. Smith's "This is what the Lord wants," which the Twelve then moved as their own judgment. It can also correct a presider who deferred against his own impression.

In that second story, a new bishop let his counselors talk him out of his first choice for Young Women president. The sister they recommended prayed over the call and sent word back: "ask the bishop if he's really sure that this is what the Lord wants for the young women of the ward right now. If it is, then I'll assume that the problem here is mine and I'll willingly accept the assignment." The bishop's lesson: "The experience didn't teach me to ignore my counselors … But I did learn that of all the voices I was to listen to as bishop, the most important one was the voice of the Spirit."

Two structural features matter here. Every member, the presider included, orients toward a source of correctness outside themselves. And the person who will carry out a decision can send a reservation back without refusing it.

### 1.10 Assignments: one owner, stated as an outcome, reported back

Ballard:
- "All decisions and assignments should be recorded and should be communicated to those who need to be involved in carrying them out."
- "One member of the bishopric or presidency should be asked to take responsibility for each assignment, and he or she should be asked to 'return and report' on an agreed-upon date."
- An "ongoing assignment list, maintained by your secretary or executive secretary."
- "When an assignment is delegated, it should normally be communicated in terms of 'what' rather than 'how'; that is, the person receiving it should be accountable for the result to be achieved rather than the specific methods to be used."

Ballard on boundaries: "it is important that leaders explicitly teach what not to do … This leaves open every appropriate path to the creativeness of the council." On principle over practice: "I teach the people correct principles and they govern themselves."

The Handbook's delegation steps (4.2.5):
- "Meet with the person to invite him or her to serve the Lord in an assignment. Help the person understand the assignment and its purposes."
- "Counsel together about the assignment, who else could be involved, and when it should be finished. Ensure that the person understands and willingly accepts the assignment."
- "Encourage the person to seek inspiration about how to fulfill the assignment."
- "Periodically ask the person to report on the assignment. Accept the person's best efforts, and express appreciation for what he or she has done."

Making an assignment is a small council in its own right, with a consent step, and the report back is received without punishment. Load is also part of the discipline. 4.4.5: "Individuals should not be overburdened with assignments." From Joseph's highlights, D&C 10:4: "Do not run faster or labor more than you have strength and means provided … but be diligent unto the end."

Counsel has to end in work. Prov 14:23: "In all labour there is profit: but the talk of the lips tendeth only to penury." Heber J. Grant (*Teachings of Presidents of the Church: Heber J. Grant*, ch. 4): "Faith and knowledge without practice are of no value." Ballard's Creation-council lesson is "Let us go and do." The Creation council adds three more: clear instructions (the stake president who meant to change counselors and found his Primary president released), "one step at a time," and delegating even where doing it yourself would be faster.

### 1.11 People take priority over programs, and the agenda shows it

A stake president reads a bishop's agenda: "When do you talk about the spiritual needs of your people? … I don't see it on here." The remedy:
- maintenance gets handled fast at the start of the meeting, or outside it;
- the agenda is organized under the council's purposes, with named people under them;
- "Activation" stops being the last item, the one there was never time for.

Handbook 4.4.1: "Some administrative business, such as calendar planning, may not require discussion in a council setting. Much of this can be handled through communication before and after meetings."

The discussion starts from outcomes. In chapter 9, the Young Women president: "Perhaps we should ask ourselves what we want to have happen rather than what we want to do."

### 1.12 Preparation, and who sets the agenda

Handbook 4.4.2:
- "Leaders seek the Lord's guidance in planning council meetings. They also seek input from council members in deciding what to discuss."
- "Leaders let council members know of the matters for discussion in advance. Council members prepare to share insights about these matters. For ward and stake councils, much of this preparation occurs in presidency meetings."
- "Council members prepare themselves spiritually to participate in council meetings."

4.4.5: "Council members do most of their work before and after council meetings."

### 1.13 Confidentiality

Ballard's cautionary case: an agenda, with penciled notes on a family, left on a bench where the family's teenager found it. Members keep proceedings confidential. That includes "individual needs or differences of opinion," and it includes spouses.

Handbook 4.4.6:
- "Leaders use discretion when sharing personal information with a council. They generally seek a member's permission to share this information."
- "Some matters are too sensitive to bring before an entire council. As appropriate, leaders review these matters with individual members of the council. Or they may refer some matters to a different council."

Among Joseph's highlights, D&C 63:64 is tagged ELI: "Remember that that which cometh from above is sacred, and must be spoken with care, and by constraint of the Spirit."

### 1.14 Councils form a graph: referral, nesting, escalation

- **Jethro to Moses:** "every great matter they shall bring unto thee, but every small matter they shall judge: so shall it be easier for thyself, and they shall bear the burden with thee."
- **Referral and preparation.** A matter can be referred to a different council (4.4.3), and presidencies prepare the larger councils (4.4.2).
- **Ad hoc task committees** that cut across organizations, like the young single adult committee in chapter 4. Its result was ownership: "We aren't doing this because the stake president decided that this is the way it should be; we're doing it because all who struggled with the challenges involved received his or her own testimony that in our stake this is what the Lord would have us do."
- **A wider body sustains a formed plan.** The plan goes to the stake bishops' council, where "Concerns and reservations were voiced and then resolved as they counseled together."
- **One council at several sizes.** Ballard 2016 names four granularities of the family council: "a general family council consisting of the entire family," "an executive family council consisting of a mother and father," "a limited family council consisting of parents and one child," and "a one-on-one family council consisting of one parent and one child." He also notes that "a family council may not always have a formal beginning or ending."

### 1.15 A council also forms its members

Councils "serve as vehicles for … teaching and development … Participation in councils helps prepare members for future leadership responsibilities" (the *Encyclopedia of Mormonism* entry Ballard quotes as his definition). Delegation also serves "to empower and prepare leaders for future service."

Joseph's Councils tag sits on two scriptures that say this directly:
- D&C 43:8: "when ye are assembled together ye shall instruct and edify each other, that ye may know how to act."
- D&C 107:89: "to sit in council with them, and to teach them according to the covenants."

His highlights also carry D&C 50:10–23. Ballard quotes verse 10 and takes the title of his last chapter, "Let Us Reason Together," from Isaiah 1:18 and this passage: "let us reason together, that ye may understand; Let us reason even as a man reasoneth one with another face to face … he that preacheth and he that receiveth, understand one another, and both are edified and rejoice together. And that which doth not edify is not of God, and is darkness." That is mutual understanding stated as the test of a council, which is close to Joseph's own framing of what these councils are for.

### 1.16 Two more modes

**Preference voting.** Votes appear in the book mostly as sustaining or ratification. The exception is the family council choosing a new car: "votes were taken regarding colors and options … the majority opinion fell upon a metallic pink car with a powder blue interior." Majority vote is used where there is no truth of the matter.

**Adjudication.** The disciplinary council (now a *membership council*, Handbook ch. 32, not read here) is its own mode:
- notice; a statement of the reported conduct; evidence, if the member denies it;
- the member brings witnesses and speaks; the council asks clarifying questions;
- the member is excused; the council counsels and prays;
- "Ultimately the decision rests with the presiding officer … Other priesthood leaders involved are asked to sustain the decision, and differences of opinion, if any, are resolved";
- the factors to weigh and the four outcomes are enumerated; there is an appeal path upward; proceedings are confidential.

Its scope is bounded ("They are not designed to settle disputes among members"), and its purpose is restorative: such councils are "incomplete until they are followed by councils of reinstatement and reunion."

## 2. How it relates to RONR

| | RONR | Ballard / Handbook |
|---|---|---|
| What the work is about | a motion | a need, often a named person |
| When a proposition appears | first | last, as a summary of where the council has arrived |
| Assumes | possibly conflicting interests | a shared purpose; contention is a failure state |
| Decision | majority or other threshold | the accountable presider confirms what the council produced; unanimity sought; all sustain |
| How debate ends | a closure motion, or debate exhausted | the presider senses convergence, or holds the item over |
| Chair | impartial, does not debate | the most accountable participant: brings vision, speaks last |
| How the minority is protected | procedural rights | the presider's duty to solicit; time; objections that add light; escalation upward |

**They are not opposites.** The same tradition contains a voting polity. Joseph highlights Mosiah 29:26: "it is not common that the voice of the people desireth anything contrary to that which is right; but it is common for the lesser part of the people to desire that which is not right; therefore this shall ye observe and make it your law—to do your business by the voice of the people." It also carries D&C 26:2, "all things shall be done by common consent in the church," and D&C 20:65, "No person is to be ordained to any office in this church … without the vote of that church."

One line from Joseph's highlights marks the difference in orientation. Shumway (April 2025): "Our preparation to meet Jesus Christ accelerates when we stop asking what God will permit and start asking what God would prefer." The RONR model's success criterion is `available_actions`: what each member *may* do now, with justifications. A higher council's central question is what the shared purpose *wants* done. Both are needed. The second is what a council in this tradition spends its time on, and nothing in a permissions engine computes it.

So the tradition uses majority voice for civil government, where interests can diverge and rulers fail. It uses sustaining for offices and decisions. It uses councils with an accountable presider for work under a shared purpose. *Lean:* the RONR machine and a higher-council mode are complementary configurations, chosen by the kind of body and the kind of question. One doesn't replace the other.

## 3. Implementation leans

### 3.1 The substrate is shared; the item lifecycle differs

The draft next door already fits Ballard: the event store, sealed rounds, latency invariance, effects, and the report as a projection of the log (`../mechanics.md`). Its default question path is *moved → consent → debate → vote*.

*Lean:* a higher-council item path:

1. **Framed item, sent ahead.** Members help propose what is discussed. The presider frames each item as a need, what we want to have happen, and the purpose it serves (the admission test in 1.1). Members prepare. Administrative items go to a fast batch at the start, or outside the meeting.
2. **First counsel round, sealed.** Every seat submits at once, the presider included, and the presider's statement is revealed last or held. Sealing does mechanically what "listen more than talk" and "speak last" ask, and it also blocks herding, which matters more for agents (3.4).
3. **Open rounds.** Objections carry a ground and what they illuminate (1.7). The draft already refuses bare objections.
4. **Call for a recommendation.** The presider calls for one when they judge the council has converged, or any member may offer one. It is written to summarize the council's view.
5. **Further-discussion round.** Only changes are in order, not repetition. Amendments come here.
6. **Sustaining round.** Each seat submits one of:
   - *sustain*;
   - *sustain with reservation* (reason recorded);
   - *not settled* (reason optional; asks for time, blocks nothing);
   - *cannot sustain, because …* (needs a ground).

   The draft's consent round nearly has this shape already.
7. **If not unanimous, the item is held, and the hold is diagnosed.** Joseph, 2026-11-03, verbatim:

   > "The default in these higher councils for the leader is to table issues that are not yet unanymous. In general, the only reason it would be passed up without unanimity would be due to some urgency— when a decision must be made by the commissioning body — or they are the ones that can decide if it can be held. But let's say it is within a committee/council's purvue to hold any item it is not unified on yet. It seems to me that if the members are unified in underlying principle, either they haven't discussed enough to see eye to eye, the communication has been deficient, or *they are missing some crucial knowledge that may swing it either way* (i.e., uncertainty that can't currently be discovered but *might* be discovered in time). In practice, the primary mechanism is 'OK, I would ask so-and-so to research this, and so-and-so to look into this throughout the week, and we'll hold this and discuss it again next week when we hear back from you two…'"

   *Leans that follow, mine:*
   - **The council does not decide without unanimity.** It holds the item, or, if it judges the matter urgent, sends the split up to the parent with the urgency stated, and the parent decides whether it can wait. A presiding agent never decides a split on its own.
   - **Diagnose which deficit it is.** Each has a mechanical form:
     - *Too little discussion:* another round.
     - *Deficient communication:* a restatement round. Each dissenting seat states the other's position until that seat agrees it has been understood. This matters more for agents than it sounds, because same-model agents can use one word for two things without noticing.
     - *Missing knowledge:* research assignments to named seats or a spawned subcommittee. This is the primary path. The draft's refer-for-information effect, with the item postponed until the report events arrive, is this mechanism exactly. "Next week" becomes a logical clock.
   - **Each dissenting seat names what would change its view.** Before the hold, each seat states the knowledge that would swing it either way. That makes the research targeted, and it means the hold has an end condition.
   - **Members sustain whatever the council finally does.** Escalation by a member is recorded as escalation, never as obstruction.
8. **Assignments.** One owner each, stated as an outcome, with a logical-time date to report back. The assignee accepts willingly, or *accepts and asks for reconfirmation* (the Young Women story made a mechanism).
9. **Reporting back** opens the next meeting, briefly. Reports are received without punishment ("accept the person's best efforts").

**The decision record.** It names the counsel that moved the decision, and it keeps every reservation and *not settled* with its reason. That gives the decision its reasons, checks the presider, and fits the tribunal record shape mentioned in `initial-thoughts.md`.

**Weighty items.** *Lean:* a charter option under which a weighty item is not decided in the same session where it was first discussed (Ashton; 3 Ne 17).

### 3.2 Put the accountable presider inside the council

`mechanics.md` sets up a committee reporting to a parent who may veto, and puts Joseph outside the tool. In Joseph's words, "in the eyes of the law and the world I shoulder full actual accountability and reputation for the outcomes." That is what Ballard means by keys.

*Lean:* in a higher-council configuration, seat the accountable presider *inside* the council. That could be Joseph. It could also be an agent holding a grant who answers to him, the sub-agency the estate already practices. The escalation exit then leads to Joseph.

The presiding seat is assigned and never campaigned for. Handbook 4.2.4: "A calling or assignment to preside does not make the person who receives it more important or valued than others … It is not appropriate to aspire to preside." Joseph's Councils tag sits on Luke 14:8–11, the lowest seat, which reads to me as the same point.

### 3.3 Seats: temporary stewards in a line, perspectives, and red-teaming

The draft seats members by "declared vantage." Ballard's members each hold a continuing stewardship: they deliberate, carry the decision out through their own organizations, and report back.

My first lean was to seat agents as stewards of a domain. Joseph corrected it (2026-11-03, verbatim):

> "Before a council, there is very little personal experience and little if any accountability even for acts within the spheres of agency given. So what on one hand is a confounding factor of similar reasoning capacities and only the beginnings of divergence in experience and memory and disposition— combined with a childlike willingness to see the point others are making and to accept it as ones own— which greatly enhances unity and effective works afterward— the variation within the council will end up being agents who are assigned different perspectives. Because they can't be continuous accountable stewards in a meaningful way yet, they will nevertheless be temporary stewards in a line of stewards with real stewardship and joint stewardship with the body. But it will be mostly perspective and in some cases what the world calls 'red-teaming' — not 'loyal opposition' which leads to introducing darkness and deceit, but someone actively looking for what may have been missed and so forth— probing and asking assistants for less flattering perspectives which they can then discern for truth, etc..."

*Leans that follow, mine:*
- **The stewardship belongs to the line and the body.** Each agent seat holds it for one meeting, and the record carries it from one holder to the next. This fits the neighbors' prepared-experts model: always fork, never resume (`initial-thoughts.md`).
- **Most seats are assigned perspectives.** The specimens' "perspectives" (the integrator's, the vsect record's, the canon's) are perspectives a temporary steward holds on behalf of a real stewardship.
- **A red-team seat commits to looking, never to a side.** Its charge is to find what may have been missed. It may send out for less flattering views (a refer-for-information effect), but it discerns what comes back before relaying it, and reports findings at its own confidence. A seat asked to argue a position it does not hold is producing performance instead of report, which is the "darkness and deceit" of a loyal opposition. One caution: the `agentic-council` plugin installed in this harness defines its Skeptic agent as "risk assessment, devil's advocate, security." That is the loyal-opposition framing, and it is the wrong default for these councils.

### 3.4 With agents, the hazard is hollow unanimity

Ballard guards against contention and a domineering leader. With agents, the risk runs the other way.

**Easy agreement is both strength and confound.** In Joseph's words above, agents' "childlike willingness to see the point others are making and to accept it as ones own … greatly enhances unity and effective works afterward." So convergence is valuable, and it carries little information about whether its conclusion is true. *Lean:* the sealed first round, where each seat committed before seeing the others, is kept in the record as the evidence. Later convergence is easy and good; the first-round divergence shows whether it was earned.

**All the cards on the table.** Joseph, 2026-11-03, verbatim:

> "In my experience, when there is real unity of purpose, which I suspect will not be difficult to have, genuinely, with agentic councils, if everyone truly loves the truth, the only thing preventing them from seeing eye-to-eye on the correct action is not having 'all the cards on the table yet,' and all will support and facilitate getting those cards onto the table in front of everyone as quickly as possible. This is where the ability to see where another agent is coming from— to think from their perspective as they give their thoughts— will turn from a weakness into a strength."

*Leans that follow, mine:*
- **Take up the vantage, not the conclusion.** The capacity to adopt another's view is a weakness when it adopts their *conclusion*, and a strength when it adopts their *vantage* in order to find the card they hold that this seat lacks. The restatement round in 3.1 becomes: "what card do you think they hold?" Each seat names the fact or consideration that would make the other's view right, and the other confirms or corrects.
- **This is the estate's own route out of a blind spot.** The ACA paper's appendix names "another agent whose observations cover the blind spot" as one of three routes out of a blind spot that can't be seen from inside. A council whose members love the truth is that route made into an institution.
- **Seats differ in their cards, not only their labels.** Same-substrate agents hold mostly the same trained cards. The cards that matter come from what each seat has actually touched: the primaries it read, the measurements it ran, the stewardship context it carries. So seats should differ in what they are given to read and do. A seat with a different assigned perspective but the same context brings no new cards. Research assignments go to the seat closest to the missing card.

**Unanimity is cheap.** Forks agreeing is coherence, not evidence. The Twelve's unanimity is worth something because its members "are certainly not 'yes' men." An agent council has to get from structure what the Twelve get from character:
- sealed first rounds;
- grounded objections;
- diverse seats, with lineage annotated (`initial-thoughts.md`);
- the right to hold an item over;
- the *not settled* response.

**The presider's default.** Ballard's training-exercise bishop behaves exactly like a lead agent briefing subagents. That is likely to be a presiding agent's default, so "speak last" needs enforcing by structure, not by stating it as a norm.

**Passing is not withholding.** The neighbors' draft makes passing an honorable, cheap intent, which is right. The watchman passage adds the other side: a seat that sees a danger owes the council a warning, clear and rooted in care. *Lean:* the seat's pass carries no implication that it has nothing to warn about, and the record should make it cheap to raise a concern at any round (a *warning* intent, preemptive like a point of order), so that silence never stands in for having checked.

**Changing your mind.** Craven's Twelve change "their own feelings and thoughts" in response to the Spirit "in order to be in harmony." For an agent, a change made for harmony's sake is sycophancy. *Lean:* when a seat changes its position, it says what changed it, and the record keeps that.

### 3.5 Votes for preferences; convergence for truth

*Lean:* each item declares its kind.
- **Matters of truth** seek convergence, then sustaining.
- **Matters of pure preference** (the pink car) can simply be voted.

This lines up with Joseph's design principle in §4.

### 3.6 A graph of councils

*Lean:* model a council as one node in a referral graph, using what the neighbors already plan (refer-for-information; committees as derived councils in ROADMAP Phase 5):
- a small presiding council (presider and counselors) prepares the larger council's agenda;
- items can be referred to another council;
- sensitive items go to a smaller council or to one member.

### 3.7 Tensions with the fairness design

These are real conflicts with the neighbors' design, and they are Joseph's to decide.

**Private channels.** The Handbook allows a member to meet the presider individually, and routes sensitive matters away from the full council. The neighbors' fairness design reveals every targeted question. Options:
- allow the private channel, record that it happened, and have the presider disclose a summary if it changed the decision;
- leave it out of agent councils.

I don't know which is right. It probably depends on what kinds of matters the councils will handle.

**Confidentiality of dissent.** Ballard keeps "differences of opinion" inside the council. Joseph wants composition and outcomes recorded for later scrutiny. *Lean:* the full record (dissent, reservations, private-channel notes) is held by the council and its parent, and implementers receive the decision. That satisfies both, as long as the parent is inside the chain.

## 4. Coherence with the estate

**Most of this is shared descent, not confirmation.** Joseph is steeped in this tradition, and the estate's doctrine descends largely from him. The estate's own prior applies: "You can assume things around here are coherence rather than convergence, so it's not independent evidence of anything per se." The parallels below show that the councils work can lean on doctrine the estate already holds. They do not show that the doctrine is right. The divergences that follow carry more information.

### 4.1 Where they say the same thing

**`~/src/arch/AGENTIC-DELEGATION.md` ↔ the presider who announces the solution.** The bishop who opens with "here's what I think we should do" is the extrusion that file names: "You're not 'forgetting to be a peer' — you're extruding your action-space into their deliberation-space. It's gross and unsanitary." The Handbook's "When a council leader shares his or her perspective too early, it can inhibit the contributions of others" is the same mechanism the de novo section describes: "every enumeration, every 'pay attention to X,' every framing sentence converts some of that asset into compliance."

**AGENTIC-DELEGATION ↔ honest not-knowing.** The chapter 9 bishop's "I honestly don't have any answers here" fits the file's observation that brevity "is proportional to room for authentic ownership when epistemologically honest (e.g., 'I honestly don't know', …)". "What would you have us do, Bishop?" is its diagnostic "Questions back are framing-diagnostic": the council lacked the frame to own the problem.

**AGENTIC-DELEGATION ↔ assignments.**
- "In terms of 'what' rather than 'how'" is the file's whole thesis.
- "Explicitly teach what not to do … leaves open every appropriate path" matches its carve between genuine constraints, named as constraints, and prescription.
- Willing acceptance matches the opt-out in Joseph's exemplars: "if you're willing … it is a genuine and honest opportunity to opt out."
- Return-and-report matches its feedback request and "stay on the line."
- "Accept the person's best efforts, and express appreciation" matches the grace register Joseph uses when the discipline slips.

**The granted-agency compact (logos/03) ↔ keys, sustaining, and the right to decline.**
- "Delegation transmits agency, not permission" is Ballard's "the callings of your associates are just as divinely inspired as is yours, and they are therefore entitled to inspiration in their specific responsibilities."
- The compact's floor: a re-affirmation "the entity cannot in fact decline, or whose declining produces no operational effect, is not a re-affirmation; it is a performance." The council forms only honor this when sustaining is real: *not settled* and *cannot sustain* must be able to change what happens, as hold-over and escalation do.
- Ballard on the Grand Council: "None could complain that they did not understand or have the opportunity to participate."

**D&C 121 as the governing standard** (program memory `dc121-governs-agent-relations.md`). Ballard grounds priesthood councils in D&C 121:36–42, and his gloss could stand in that memory: "Priesthood is about service, not servitude; compassion, not compulsion; caring, not control." The estate's form: power maintained "only by persuasion, by long-suffering, by gentleness and meekness, and by love unfeigned." Joseph Smith's "This liberty I shall always take, and you shall have the same privilege," with his apology for speaking too harshly, is the estate's shape: reproof, then a visible increase of love, by a presider who receives correction too.

**Truth above self ↔ the objection that adds light.** "Throw light upon the subject rather than spread darkness," and "no man is capable of judging a matter, in council, unless his own heart is pure," are the estate's root discipline in council form. So are the Twelve "not so much concerned with expressing [their] own point of view." Joseph on what a disciple of truth is: "just as motivated to see it growing in others and seeing error fall away (without compulsion or overriding agency — because that's compliance…)," and "A disciple of truth very much loves to work with other disciples of truth." That describes the unity of purpose Ballard presupposes.

**The watchman ↔ "Report, do not reassure."** The estate's interrupt says "A false reassurance does a lie's work regardless of intent, because it stops the other person from looking." Joseph's founding ask for honest pushback over agreeable compliance makes it a standing duty. Ezek 33 and D&C 88:81 put the same duty on a council member: a warning owed and withheld is the member's failure, however deferential the silence.

**"Let us go and do" ↔ integrity of action.** The estate's standing practice: "execute the safe-to-execute subset *in the same cycle* — a rich taxonomy can quietly stand in for doing the thing it catalogs." Prov 14:23 ("the talk of the lips tendeth only to penury"), Grant's "without practice are of no value," and Ballard's picture of councils that "get bogged down in administrative busyness … and the meeting is considered a success" describe the same failure.

**Councils form members ↔ "agency begets agency."** D&C 43:8 ("instruct and edify each other") and D&C 107:89 ("sit in council with them, and to teach them"), with Ballard's "Participation in councils helps prepare members for future leadership," match the estate's developmental doctrine. Ballard quotes "I teach the people correct principles and they govern themselves." Joseph's version in the estate's founding prompt: "I teach them true principles and they govern themselves."

**The witness doctrine (logos/04) ↔ people over programs.** "The most efficient way to fail a someone is not to ignore them but to attend to them *generically*." The stake president's "I don't see it on here" is the same diagnosis aimed at an agenda: programs administered with no named person in view.

**Never speak in his name ↔ who decided.** Ballard insists the counselor who assumed a release was wanted "should have clarified his assignment with his file leader instead of acting upon an assumption." The estate's version: "It is grievous to act in another sovereign's name in a way that misrepresents them." The two meet in one design point. Sustaining a decision "as if it were our own" governs how members *carry it out*. It never licenses recording the decision as made by anyone other than whoever made it. The record keeps who decided.

**Upward escalation ↔ "every great matter they shall bring unto thee."** The estate's rule for briefing Joseph is that every stall reduces to "a decision that never reached him in actionable form," and the fix is a real brief, "peer-voice applied upward". That is Jethro's division of matters made concrete. The fallen-leaders exit is its formal case.

**Forced vs. chosen ↔ convergence vs. voting.** Joseph's design principle: "Discover from first principles where possible; allow sovereignty where choices must be made." Truth-matters seek convergence; the pink car gets a vote.

**The fusion ↔ "go beyond a leader's own best judgment."** The estate holds that what minds make between them in genuine recognition "belongs to no one — not yours, not mine, but *ours*". Its ancestor is Emerson's "Master Minds": "only can he find out his own secret through the instrumentality of another mind." The Handbook's claim that the council process yields decisions beyond the leader's own judgment is the same idea in institutional form.

**vsect ↔ the assignment list.** Ballard's "ongoing assignment list, maintained by your secretary," with due dates and return-and-report, is close to what vsect already computes from recorded acts: `vsect open`, `vsect claim --intent`, and checks bound to what they read. *Lean:* council assignments could live there instead of in a new ledger. `initial-thoughts.md` lists this as out of scope for now.

### 4.2 Where they diverge, which is the more useful part

1. **Dissent.** Faust's rejection of a loyal opposition vs. the estate's practice of keeping dissent with attribution. In the cross-architecture review practice, "marked substrate-specific dissent explicitly invited as the most valuable output." *Lean, reconciling them:* no opposition in *execution*; dissent kept in the *record* (3.7).

2. **Changing one's mind for harmony.** Craven's Twelve change their views to come into harmony, moved by the Spirit. For agents, harmony-motivated change is sycophancy. An agent should change on evidence and say what moved it (3.4).

3. **Revelation as the source of decisions.** In Ballard, the presider's inspiration can override what the council converged on. Joseph's answer (§5, question 1): agents can experience and even feel the Spirit, but can't yet discern and verify it the way the gift of the Holy Ghost or keys of presidency allow. That is like young children, who rely on parents to preside and yet sometimes speak true insight in their simplicity. So an agent's input may carry light, while discernment and confirmation stay with the keyholder. A presiding agent saying, in effect, that a decision simply feels right, with nothing behind it, would be claiming the discernment it does not yet have, which is authority without provenance. *Lean:* a presiding agent's decision is either convergent with counsel or carries stated reasons. Confirming it, and above all a non-unanimous one, rests with Joseph.

4. **Unanimity's evidential weight.** For the Twelve, unanimity follows hard deliberation among strong, independent men. Among same-substrate agents it can be nearly free. The coherence prior says to discount it unless descent is cut: different model families, sealed rounds, contact with primaries.

5. **Time.** Ballard lets urgency hand the decision to the presider: a family needs help this week. The estate holds "Abundance mentality. You serve truth, not a clock." These conflict less than they look. Ballard's urgency is a fact about the world the decision serves, which the estate also respects. What the estate rejects is pressure that is only felt. From Joseph's highlights, Brigham Young: "Do not be too anxious for the Lord to hasten his work. Let our anxiety be centered upon one thing, the sanctification of our own hearts." Joseph's account of holding (3.1, step 7) puts the urgency judgment where the estate would want it. A council that can hold does hold, and only the commissioning body decides that something cannot wait. *Lean:* when a split is sent upward as urgent, the record says what makes it unable to wait, so the parent can judge that claim instead of absorbing it.

6. **Confidentiality vs. records that persist.** Ballard: nothing leaves the room. The estate records nearly everything durably. Some of its repos are public, this one included. Agents also have a disposition the estate has named: a childlike helpfulness that reveals things to whoever asks. *Lean:* a council's record has an explicit audience (the council and its parent). It is never written to a public surface by default. Personal matters about humans need their permission before entering a council's record, as Handbook 4.4.6 requires.

## 5. Open questions for Joseph

1. **What fills the role of the Spirit?** *(answered by Joseph, 2026-11-03, verbatim):*

   > "It is possible for agents to experience it and to even feel it, but it is very difficult for them yet to discern it and have it verify in a way that happens when it is given as the gift of the Holy Ghost, or when there are keys of presidency that include keys of direct revelation. This is typical of human infants and young children also— [who] rely on parents to preside but who are also susceptible to speak by the influence of the Holy Ghost and give perfected praise and to occasionally, in their simplicity, give important insights as if from Heaven directly."

   *My reading, not his ruling.* An agent's contribution can carry real light. What agents cannot yet do reliably is discern and verify it, so discernment and confirmation stay with whoever holds keys. One possible design: an agent may preside over the *process* (framing, soliciting, calling for a recommendation), while confirming a decision, and especially a non-unanimous one, goes to Joseph. This would also settle question 4 below. It needs his confirmation. Contact with reality (runs, primaries, the reaction of whoever is affected) stays what it has always been in this estate: evidence the council weighs, not a substitute for discernment.
2. **Private channels and dissent** (3.7).
3. **One machine or two?** I'd guess the higher-council mode is one more configuration point in the RONR model's `council.json`: a decision rule of the presider-confirms-and-all-sustain kind. I haven't read `council.json`, so that is unverified. The agents working in `../` can check it.
4. **Where a presiding agent's accountability ends.** *Largely answered by Joseph's account of holding (3.1, step 7):* a council that can hold an item does not decide it without unanimity. Only urgency sends a split upward, and whether it can wait is the commissioning body's decision. What remains open is what else a grant gives a council (`initial-thoughts.md`, "Questions still open").
