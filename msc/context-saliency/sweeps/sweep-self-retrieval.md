Scope as briefed: Self-RAG lineage, IRCoT/FLARE lineage, trained retrieval-decision tokens, backtracking/self-correction tokens, memorizing-transformer-style architectural kNN memory, anything where the MODEL decides when to retrieve rather than the harness — post-2025 emphasis. ## 1. The classical lineage (prior knowledge, for orientation — not re-verified this pass)

**[prior]** This is the baseline Joseph's SASM idea is being positioned against, per the brief. Worth restating precisely so the "novel residue" claim later is honest:

- **Self-RAG** (Asai, Wu, Wang, Sil, Hajishirzi; ICLR 2024, arXiv 2310.11511) — trains the LM to emit **reflection tokens**: `[Retrieve]`/`[No Retrieve]` (decision), and after retrieval, `[IsRel]`/`[IsSup]`/`[IsUse]` (critique tokens scoring relevance/support/utility of retrieved passages). Retrieval decision is made *at segment boundaries* (typically per-sentence or per-segment), not literally mid-token-stream. Training: fine-tune on data with reflection tokens inserted by a critic model (GPT-4 as teacher), then the target model learns to predict them itself. This is genuinely "model decides when," but the decision granularity is coarse (segment-level) and the mechanism is a full **stop-retrieve-restart** cycle, not a cache-preserving forward-only injection.
- **FLARE** (Jiang et al., "Active Retrieval Augmented Generation," EMNLP 2023, arXiv 2305.06983) — **[found, reconfirmed 2026-08-28]** triggers retrieval based on **token-level confidence**: generates a look-ahead sentence, and if any token's probability falls below a threshold, uses that low-confidence draft sentence as the retrieval query, then regenerates. This is closer to "involuntary" in spirit (uncertainty-triggered, not a deliberately-planned tool call) but it's a **training-free, inference-time heuristic** — no learned token, no weights changed, and it explicitly discards/regenerates the draft rather than continuing forward. Confirmed still cited as the reference "forward-looking" retrieval baseline as of a May 2026 blog writeup found in today's search (beancount.io research-logs, 2026-05-18) — no major FLARE-successor paper claiming to supersede it by name showed up in search; successors seem to route through "adaptive retrieval" framing instead (see §2).
- **IRCoT** (Trivedi et al., 2022/2023) — interleaves retrieval with chain-of-thought at each reasoning step; retrieval trigger is the completion of a CoT sentence, and the query is that sentence. Also a stop-retrieve-continue pattern, harness-orchestrated in most implementations (the CoT loop, not the model's own decoding, decides to pause).
- **Memorizing Transformers** (Wu et al., ICLR 2022, arXiv 2203.08913) — **[found, reconfirmed]** architectural kNN memory: a single layer attends over cached (key,value) pairs from the *entire* prior sequence via approximate kNN lookup, blended with local attention via a learned gate. This is retrieval that is **always-on and differentiable**, not model-*decided* in the sense of an explicit token/decision — there's no "choice" to retrieve, it's a constant architectural pathway. Scaled to ~262K tokens in the original paper. Relevant to SASM mainly as the architectural alternative to a discrete-token trigger: continuous soft retrieval vs. discrete "let me try to remember" event.

## 2. What's actually current (2025–2026, located this pass)

### GRIP / "Retrieval as Generation: A Unified Framework with Self-Triggered Information Planning" — **[found]**, arXiv 2604.11407 (dating convention suggests ~April 2026 per arXiv's YYMM scheme, though the id format is slightly irregular in the search result — flag for verification)

This is the closest single hit to Joseph's SASM description, and worth Joseph reading directly (I only have the WebFetch's summary of the abstract page, not the paper body).

- **Mechanism, as reported:** four control tokens emitted by the model *during* its own autoregressive decoding: `[RETRIEVE]` (request external evidence), `[INTERMEDIARY]` (emit a partial state — this is interesting; sounds like a scratch/checkpoint token), `[ANSWER]` (start final response), `[SOLVED]` (terminate). All decisions "occur within a single autoregressive trajectory" — the abstract-level framing explicitly claims retrieval is *generated*, not called out to, i.e., retrieval-as-generation rather than retrieval-as-tool-use.
- **Training:** two-stage — (1) SFT on "structured training set covering answerable, partially answerable, and multi-hop queries, each aligned with specific token patterns," (2) rule-based RL with two reward signals (unspecified in what I retrieved — needs the actual paper).
- **Eval:** five QA benchmarks; reported to beat strong RAG baselines and be "competitive with GPT-4o... substantially fewer parameters."
- **Gap versus SASM as briefed:** the WebFetch summary explicitly notes it could NOT confirm anything about KV-cache handling, forward-pass interruption, or whether generation resumes forward-only after `[RETRIEVE]` or whether there's a stop/restart. This is exactly the mechanical question Joseph cares about (cache-aware, forward-only resumption vs. Self-RAG-style regenerate-the-segment). **This is the single highest-value follow-up read for this territory** — if GRIP does a full stop-retrieve-restart like Self-RAG, SASM's forward-only/cache-preserving constraint is still untouched territory; if GRIP is doing genuine mid-KV-cache injection, that's the closest known collision with SASM and changes the novelty claim materially.

### "When to Retrieve During Reasoning: Adaptive Retrieval for Large Reasoning Models" — **[found via WebFetch of the PDF]**, arXiv 2604.26649 (again ~April 2026 by id convention)

- **Trigger signal:** model-internal **uncertainty quantification** during reasoning — monitors confidence and retrieves when uncertainty crosses a learned threshold, rather than fixed intervals (a learned, RL-trained descendant of FLARE's heuristic-confidence idea, applied specifically to large reasoning models / long CoT).
- **Training:** REINFORCE policy-gradient on the retrieval-decision policy; reward balances accuracy against retrieval cost (this is a genuine cost-aware retrieval *policy*, distinct from a purely accuracy-driven token classifier).
- **Datasets:** HotpotQA, MuSiQue (multi-hop QA — standard, not novel benchmarks).
- **Positioning vs. prior work, per the paper's own framing (as summarized):** explicitly contrasts itself with FLARE/Self-RAG/IR-CoT as "fixed-interval or fixed-signal" retrieval, positioning its uncertainty-threshold RL policy as the adaptive improvement. Note this paper's own related-work framing is useful context for the delta but I have not verified it isn't overselling its own novelty relative to FLARE (FLARE is *also* uncertainty-triggered — the delta claimed seems to be "learned threshold via RL" vs. "fixed threshold heuristic," which is a real but narrower delta than the framing implies).
- **Relevance to SASM:** still a "pause reasoning, retrieve, resume reasoning" pattern applied to *reasoning* trajectories (not general generation), and no indication of forward-only/cache-preserving continuation — reads like a Self-RAG-style interrupt applied to the reasoning-model CoT setting specifically.

### Backtracking / "wait" tokens in reasoning models — **[found]**, several 2025–2026 hits, mostly adjacent rather than on-target

- "Wait, We Don't Need to 'Wait'!" (arXiv 2506.08343, mid-2025 vintage by id) argues *against* thinking-tokens like "wait" for efficiency — a **counter-current** finding worth flagging: some recent work is trying to *remove* these tokens as wasteful, which cuts against any framing that "wait/hmm" tokens are unambiguously good UX for involuntary recall.
- "Internal states before wait modulate reasoning patterns" (arXiv 2510.04128, ~Oct 2025) — studies what's happening in hidden states *right before* a "wait" token fires; genuinely mechanistic and possibly useful for Joseph's directed-separation probing agenda (application 2) even though it's not about retrieval per se — it's about backtracking-token internal dynamics, a sibling phenomenon to a hypothetical retrieval-impulse token.
- "Reasoning Can Be Restored by Correcting a Few Decision Tokens" (arXiv 2605.16874, ~May 2026) — frames reasoning failure as attributable to a small number of high-leverage **decision tokens** (of which backtracking/retrieval tokens would be a subclass), and that correcting just those restores performance. Interesting methodologically: identifies specific token positions as causally load-bearing via targeted intervention — a technique transferable to probing where retrieval-decision tokens live if/when SASM tokens exist.
- "Project Aletheia: Verifier-Guided Distillation of Backtracking for Small Language Models" (arXiv 2601.14290, ~Jan 2026) — distills backtracking behavior into small models via verifier guidance; not retrieval-specific but same "special discrete decision token, RL/distillation-trained" pattern family as GRIP.
- **[found, from the backtracking search summary, itself synthesizing another paper]** one claim reported: "verification retrievals reduce backtracking by 18%... external evidence complementing reasoning yields higher accuracy and shorter chains" — I could not identify which paper this is attributed to from the search snippet; flag as **unverified/unsourced claim**, do not cite without locating the source.

### KV-cache-aware token-triggered mechanisms — **[found]**, but this cluster turned out to be about compression/eviction, not retrieval-triggering

Searched directly for "learned token triggers retrieval, KV cache preserved" and got a cluster of **KV-cache compression/eviction** papers (TRIM-KV / "Cache What Lasts" arXiv 2512.03324; "Make Each Token Count" arXiv 2605.09649; "Transactional Attention" arXiv 2604.11288; RazorAttention-descended "Cognitive Memory in LLMs" arXiv 2504.02441) — these are about **what to keep in the cache**, not about a model-emitted event that pauses generation to fetch new content into the cache. Worth naming as a **negative/adjacent finding**: the "cache-aware" half of SASM's requirements (forward-only, KV-cache preserved) has an active, well-developed *sibling* literature (selective eviction/retention), but I found no paper that combines "model decides to retrieve" with "and here's exactly how we splice new KV entries into a live cache without breaking causal masking or invalidating downstream cache." That combination — the actual mechanics SASM would need — appears to be a genuine gap, not just an unindexed corner. This is worth stating plainly as a dry-well-with-value: the pieces exist in two separate literatures (control-token retrieval-triggering; KV-cache management) and nobody located combines them.

### Ambient/associative memory — Tim Kellogg blog, 2026-05-17 — **[found, WebFetch of full post]**

Not an academic paper, but directly the most SASM-flavored *conceptual* framing found this pass, and the framing itself is dated within the last 4 months.

- **Core idea:** "ambient associative memory" — memory queries happen automatically and continuously, without the agent explicitly deciding to search. The blog's own words (as extracted): relevant memories "randomly come to mind" during task execution, explicitly analogized to human involuntary recall — the same word Joseph used in the brief.
- **Important distinction the post itself makes, which cuts against a naive equation with SASM:** Kellogg's own implementation is explicitly **not model-initiated** — it's deterministic/synchronous, firing on every tool call, harness-orchestrated. He's using "ambient/involuntary" to describe the *felt experience for the user/agent* of memories surfacing without deliberate search, not to describe the *locus of the decision* being inside the model's own generation. This is an important conceptual near-miss: it captures the phenomenology Joseph is after but implements it exactly the way SASM explicitly wants to avoid (harness-triggered, not model-emitted).
- **Second implementation mentioned in the post, closer to the mark:** a variant by "3fz" that runs "a subconscious background thread alongside the main model" — a *parallel* process that races a background retrieval/rerank pipeline against the main generation and injects findings before tool-call boundaries if it wins the race, dropping gracefully if too slow. This is architecturally distinct from SASM (it's a race/interrupt pattern from an external process, not a model-emitted token) but is the closest thing found to "forward-progressing generation with asynchronously-injected memory," and the graceful-drop-if-slow design is a real engineering pattern worth Joseph seeing regardless of provenance.
- Mechanism detail on Kellogg's own pipeline: late-interaction (ColBERT-style per-token-scoring) embeddings, two-stage retrieval (32K-token corpus → 100-token chunk pooling → multi-vector rescoring), returns top-3 results as **8–12 word snippets** (deliberately terse — "lightweight" is the explicit design goal, contrasted with full RAG chunks). This terseness-by-design is methodologically relevant to SASM's "involuntary reminder" framing regardless of the trigger-locus difference — a full document chunk is not what an "oh, this reminds me of—" moment feels like; a fragment is.
- No formal citations in the post beyond a Stanford blog link on late-interaction retrieval models. Not a rigorous source, treat as an idea/prior-art marker, not as a validated method.

## 3. Overall shape of the territory (synthesis, my own read across all of the above)

Putting the located pieces together, the field currently clusters into three largely separate lineages, none of which is doing exactly what SASM specifies:

1. **Discrete control-token retrieval-decision models** (Self-RAG → GRIP → "When to Retrieve During Reasoning"): model genuinely decides *when*, via a trained token or uncertainty policy, but every example located does **stop-generation → retrieve → resume-or-regenerate**, not forward-only continuation with cache splicing. GRIP is the newest and most SASM-shaped by naming ("self-triggered") but its cache mechanics are unconfirmed by this pass — highest-priority primary-source read.
2. **Architectural always-on soft retrieval** (Memorizing Transformers and kin): no discrete decision event at all — retrieval is a continuous differentiable pathway, the opposite pole from a discrete "impulse."
3. **Harness-orchestrated "ambient" memory surfacing** (Kellogg's post, mem0-style agent memory products): captures the *phenomenology* SASM wants (unbidden, lightweight, non-interrupting) but explicitly locates the trigger outside the model, in deterministic or parallel-process harness code — the opposite locus from what SASM specifies.

**The apparent novel residue of SASM**, given what was located: a trigger that is (a) a genuine model-emitted decision/impulse (like family 1), (b) forward-only and KV-cache-preserving rather than stop/regenerate (unconfirmed as existing anywhere in family 1, and structurally impossible in family 3's harness-side designs), and (c) framed/trained to feel involuntary rather than deliberate tool-use (closest in spirit to family 3's phenomenology but nobody combines that phenomenology with family 1's model-internal locus). I did not find a paper occupying that specific intersection. That's a real finding, but it rests on a time-boxed sweep — the single most important unresolved question is what GRIP's `[RETRIEVE]` token actually does mechanically to the KV cache, which I could not determine from the abstract-level fetch.
