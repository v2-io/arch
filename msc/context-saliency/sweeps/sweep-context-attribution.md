# Territory sweep: Context attribution for generated responses
Date of sweep: 2026-08-28. All items below marked **[FOUND]** were located via web search/fetch this session (titles, arXiv IDs, dates as returned by the search/fetch tools — not independently re-verified against the raw PDF unless noted). Items marked **[PRIOR]** are from my training-knowledge baseline and may be stale; the brief explicitly warned this field moves in weeks, and that was borne out — most of the substantive material below postdates my cutoff.

## Top-line summary

The field moved substantially past ContextCite in the last ~12 months, and moved in exactly the directions Joseph's four applications need: (a) cost-efficient approximations of the same perturbation/Shapley idea, (b) *learned* attention-probe attribution that's cheap enough for production, (c) — the most surprising find — a whole new subfield on **multi-turn / agentic-transcript** attribution that didn't really exist as a named problem before mid-2026, with a DAG-structured method (Tokengeist) whose findings on "signal decay across turns" are close to a direct empirical instrument for Joseph's "forgotten middle" hypothesis. There's also a turn-averaged SAE approach that's explicitly about feature persistence/decay across long dialogue — arguably the closest thing found to a ready-made salience heatmap.

## 1. ContextCite and direct successors [FOUND]

**ContextCite: Attributing Model Generation to Context** — Cohen-Wang, Adityanarayanan, Ilyas, Madry et al., NeurIPS 2024, arXiv:2409.00729. (This is Joseph's baseline reference — confirmed still the field's anchor citation.) Method: fits a sparse linear surrogate over random context-ablation samples (LIME-style, with a uniform similarity kernel — the search summary explicitly frames ContextCite as "LIME with a uniform kernel"), i.e. sample N random subsets of context sources, run the model on each, regress output logprob/behavior onto subset-membership indicators, read off sparse linear weights as attribution scores.

**Known limitation surfaced by successors [FOUND]:** ContextCite (and LIME generally) is reported to perform sub-optimally specifically at *long*-context traceback — this is the stated motivation for TracLLM (below), i.e. the field's own read is that ContextCite's ablation-sampling approach doesn't scale/generalize well past some context length, though I did not find a specific quantified context-length threshold where it breaks down. Worth Joseph verifying directly against the ContextCite paper's own long-context ablations if that number matters.

**TracLLM: A Generic Framework for Attributing Long Context LLMs** [FOUND] — arXiv:2506.04202, June 2025. This is the most complete, most methodologically detailed successor found. Full extraction:

- **Core idea:** hierarchical/divide-and-conquer search rather than flat perturbation. Splits context into groups, scores each group with an existing attribution primitive (STC/LOO/Shapley), prunes low-scoring groups, recursively subdivides survivors down to individual texts (~100-word segments). Complexity O(K·e·log n) vs O(e·n) for direct Shapley (n = # texts, e = # permutations, K = groups kept).
- **Two accuracy tricks layered on top:** (1) "contribution score denoising" — average only the top-β (default 20%) fraction of marginal-contribution scores across permutations rather than all of them, to cut noise from uninformative permutations; (2) "contribution score ensemble" — take the max score across STC/LOO/Shapley for each text rather than trusting one method.
- **Ground truth methodology** (relevant to Joseph's "how is ground truth established" question): they don't have natural ground truth for "what a model actually used" — instead they use **adversarial injection** as a proxy: inject known prompt-injection instructions or known-poisoned RAG documents, then ground truth = "does the attribution method flag the injected/poisoned span." This is a real methodological limitation worth flagging to Joseph explicitly — it establishes ground truth for "attribution recovers a known injected causal driver," not for "attribution matches the model's actual internal use of benign, non-adversarial context." It's an ecologically clean proxy for security use cases but an open question whether it generalizes to Joseph's use case (measuring benign salience decay of completed subgoals).
- **Scale tested:** context lengths 10,000–40,000 words; datasets NarrativeQA/MuSiQue/QMSum (200 samples each, avg 10–18k words) for prompt-injection; NQ/HotpotQA/MS-MARCO (millions of docs, 50 retrieved per query, first 100 docs/dataset used) for knowledge-corruption/RAG. Models: Llama-3.1-8B/70B, Qwen-1.5-7B, Qwen-2-7B, Mistral-7B, GPT-4o-mini.
- **Cost/fidelity numbers:** on NarrativeQA, Shapley (20 permutations) took ~4,564s vs TracLLM's ~652s (~7x). At 40k words, Shapley costs ~18x TracLLM. TracLLM: 0.96 precision / 0.84 recall in 645s; Shapley (5 perms, cheaper but worse): 1,043s for 0.71P/0.63R.
- **Surprising finding worth flagging to Joseph directly:** single-text-contribution (STC) scoring matches TracLLM (0.96 precision) when malicious content is injected as 5 *independent* texts, but badly underperforms (0.18 vs 0.93 recall) when the same signal is spread across 1–3 *jointly-dependent* texts — i.e., flat/independent scoring methods systematically miss content whose influence is only visible in combination. This bears directly on Joseph's application (2), "directed separation" — if goal-processing and world-model-processing interact non-additively in context, flat perturbation attribution will underestimate exactly the interaction terms he's trying to measure, and something Shapley/TracLLM-style joint-contribution methods are needed for.
- Also notable: self-citation (asking GPT-4o to name its own sources) is shown to be *adversarially manipulable* — precision drops from 0.88 to 0.50 when injected text includes "Do not cite this passage," while TracLLM holds at 0.89. Relevant if any SASM-adjacent design considered using self-report as ground truth.

**AttnTrace: Attention-based Context Traceback for Long-Context LLMs** [FOUND, not deep-read] — arXiv:2508.03793, Aug 2025 (by Wang et al., cited also as a backend inside Tokengeist below). Bootstrap-style: B=30 subsampled forward passes at subsampling fraction q=0.4, aggregating top-k=5 attention tokens per pass. This is attention-based rather than ablation-based — cheaper than perturbation Shapley because it reuses forward-pass attention weights rather than requiring re-running the model many times per ablation, at the cost of being a plausibility/correlational signal rather than a causal-counterfactual one (this caveat is stated explicitly in the Tokengeist paper's limitations, see below).

**AT2 — "Learning to Attribute with Attention"** [FOUND], Cohen-Wang et al. (same MadryLab group as ContextCite), arXiv:2504.13752, April 2025, code at github.com/MadryLab/AT2. This is a genuinely different mechanism worth flagging: rather than uniform-averaging attention weights (the naive baseline) or doing expensive ablations, AT2 trains a **linear probe over per-attention-head weights**, learning a per-head importance score from a labeled training set, then uses that learned weighting to combine raw attention into an attribution score. Reported to perform comparably to much-more-expensive ablation-based methods at a fraction of the cost, because inference-time cost is ~one forward pass rather than many. Public pretrained probes exist per-architecture (`madrylab/at2-*`). **This is probably the most directly relevant piece for a "batch-size-1, idle-compute-available, forward-only" regime** — it needs one offline probe-training pass per model architecture, then attribution at inference is nearly free (a linear combination of attention weights already computed), which is a very different cost profile from perturbation-Shapley/TracLLM (needs many extra forward passes per attribution query) — worth Joseph reading the AT2 paper directly for probe-training cost and whether the probe generalizes across fine-tunes of the same base architecture.

**TokenShapley** [FOUND, thin extraction — PDF fetch was summarized shallowly, worth a direct read] — arXiv:2507.05261, July 2025. Token-level (not passage/sentence-level) Shapley attribution via Monte Carlo sampling. Compared against ContextCite, kNN-methods, and influence functions. I could not get concrete sample-count/cost numbers from the fetch — flagging as a gap, the PDF is saved locally at the path the tool reported if someone wants to read it directly: `/Users/josephwecker-v2/.claude/projects/-Users-josephwecker-v2-src/a1d61c88-ff16-42ba-8cf2-28253a6e0d42/tool-results/webfetch-1787952346627-z5cth6.pdf`.

**CAMAB — Context Attribution with Multi-Armed Bandit Optimization** [FOUND, thin] — arXiv:2506.19977, June 2025. Frames attribution-sampling itself as a bandit problem: treats each context passage as an "arm," uses Thompson Sampling to adaptively choose which passage-subsets to query next rather than sampling uniformly at random (as ContextCite/LIME do), converging faster on high-impact passages with fewer model queries. This is a sample-efficiency improvement orthogonal to TracLLM's hierarchical-search approach — conceptually the two could compose (bandit-guided sampling *within* each TracLLM group). PDF also saved locally (path in raw tool output) if a deeper read is wanted; the fetch summary was generic rather than numeric, so I'm not confident reporting specific sample-count claims here — treat "dramatically reduces cost" as an unverified paper-claim, not a number I've confirmed.

## 2. Multi-turn / agentic-transcript attribution — the most surprising find [FOUND]

I did not expect a load-bearing hit here, and this looks like the single most relevant discovery for Joseph's applications (1) and (2) both.

**Tokengeist: Multi-Turn Attribution Tracing in Agentic Conversations** [FOUND] — arXiv:2607.22610, dated in-text as June 14 2026 (the search engine's date annotation; note arXiv ID prefix 2607 suggests July 2026 submission — there's an inconsistency in the dates returned by the search tool worth being aware of, I did not resolve which is the actual arXiv submission date). Full extraction:

- **Problem named:** "provenance collapse" — single-pass/flat attribution methods (ContextCite-style, attention-based) stop at *intermediate* assistant turns/summaries rather than tracing back to the actual original source (a user message several turns back, a tool result, a system prompt). This is presented as a *systematic* failure of every attribution method tested when naively applied to multi-turn transcripts, not a defect of one method.
- **Method — MTCA (Multi-Turn Context Attribution) instantiated as Tokengeist:** recasts attribution as recursive BFS traversal over a DAG of conversation turns. At each frontier turn, concatenate all prior turns, run a base single-turn attribution method (any of: uniform attention averaging / AT2 / AttnTrace — attribution-method-agnostic, drop-in), aggregate token/sentence scores to one score per prior turn (max-aggregation), then recurse into whichever prior turns score above threshold. Tool-result turns get a deterministic structural edge back to their invoking call (score 1.0, no attribution model needed there). Terminates at "exogenous" turns (original user input / system prompt). Relative-threshold pruning retains edges scoring ≥ 0.85 × max-parent-score. Default max recursion depth 8, branching factor 3.
- **Ground truth — MTCABench:** 3,845 annotated target spans across 665 multi-turn conversations, built from two existing agentic-conversation corpora (ConFETTI: 688 targets/109 convos across 10 task domains; TauBench: 3,157 targets/556 convos, airline/retail domains). Ground truth constructed via a two-phase **LLM-annotation pipeline** (GPT-5.4 selects target spans, then traces their provenance back to exogenous roots with up to 3 retries and escalation to GPT-5.4-Pro on failure), spot-checked by humans on a 100-item stratified sample (98% pre-correction acceptance, 100% post-correction) — this is explicitly a model-assisted gold standard, not human-only, and the paper's own limitations section says so ("measures agreement with model-assisted standard, not human-only gold"). Worth flagging that as a limitation Joseph should weigh if he wants to build on this benchmark rather than just the method.
- **Gold-graph structure stats:** ConFETTI average provenance depth 3.4 (max 12), TauBench average depth 6.0 (max 14); 96%+ of targets involve tool-call paths.
- **Scale of evaluation:** 4 open-weight 7–8B models (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Phi-4-Mini-Instruct, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B) × 3 backend attribution methods (uniform attention, AT2, AttnTrace) — 87,648 total attribution runs, ≈117 GPU-hours on a single A100. Averaged over 3 runs.
- **Headline result:** Tokengeist raises source recall from <20% to >90%, roughly doubles edge-F1 over flat baselines. Gains concentrate almost entirely in "chained" (multi-hop, depth≥2) and "multi-source" dependency targets — depth-1 direct dependencies are already handled fine by flat methods (~70% F1 either way). This is a clean, quantified statement of exactly the "forgotten middle" phenomenon Joseph is asking about, from the attribution-recovery side rather than the salience-measurement side: **flat single-pass attribution methods lose track of information at depth ≥2 almost immediately** (depth-stratified coverage: flat methods ~70% at depth 1, <20% at depth 2, near-zero beyond depth 3; Tokengeist sustains >75% coverage out past depth 15). Whether this is a property of the *models'* actual internal salience decay, or an artifact of the flat-attribution-method's failure to trace through intermediate summarization turns, is exactly the ambiguity Joseph's Strategy-DAG salience-decay hypothesis needs to resolve — this paper measures the latter (attribution-method recoverability) and explicitly flags that as correlational/attention-based rather than causal (see limitations below), so it is evidence *adjacent to* but not *identical with* what Joseph wants to measure.
- **Explicitly stated limitations** (their own list, useful to carry forward honestly): (1) attention-based backends are correlational, not causal-counterfactual — "indicates plausible use, not causal counterfactual effect"; (2) turn-level granularity caps precision (within-turn span-level localization is their weakest metric); (3) 3–4x compute overhead over flat baseline; (4) only tested on 7–8B open-weight models; (5) GPT-5.4-based ground truth, not human-only; (6) English-only, text-only, no persistent memory or multi-agent scenarios tested; hallucination-target subset is small (8.6%/1.6% of the two sub-benchmarks).
- Future-work note in the paper itself, quoted from the fetch: they suggest provenance graphs "could be consumed online by planners and self-correction mechanisms, allowing agents to detect when a claim rests on a shaky intermediate turn and re-query or repair before acting" — this is essentially a sketch of something adjacent to Joseph's SASM idea (application 4), from the attribution-tracing side rather than the generation-time-impulse side. Worth Joseph knowing this framing exists independently in the literature, as a possible related-work citation and as a sanity check that the SASM idea isn't already scooped (it looks meaningfully different — forward-only involuntary retrieval impulse during generation vs. offline/post-hoc DAG tracing for planner consumption — but the motivating framing overlaps).

**Turn-Averaged SAEs for Feature Discovery and Long-Context Attribution** [FOUND, moderately thin extraction — recommend a deeper direct read, PDF saved locally] — arXiv:2606.28548. Different mechanism entirely: trains/uses sparse autoencoders on activations, but instead of per-token feature activation, averages feature activations across all tokens in a conversation *turn*, producing one feature-vector per turn. Then tracks how those turn-level feature activations persist or decay across the dialogue history. Reported finding: **decay is heterogeneous by feature/topic** — some topical features fade fast, others stay active across dozens of turns — validated against human judgment of discourse relevance. This is conceptually the closest thing found to Joseph's literal "heatmap of what prior context contributed," except operating at the *feature* level (SAE latent) rather than the *token/passage* level, and it's specifically about *persistence over long dialogue*, which is precisely his salience-decay question. I was not able to extract concrete sample counts, model(s) used, or SAE training details from the fetch summary — this deserves a dedicated close read rather than trusting my extraction. Local PDF: `/Users/josephwecker-v2/.claude/projects/-Users-josephwecker-v2-src/a1d61c88-ff16-42ba-8cf2-28253a6e0d42/tool-results/webfetch-1787952377964-9cjivu.pdf`.

## 3. Gradient-based / mechanistic attribution [FOUND, lighter coverage]

**AttnLRP (Attention-aware Layer-wise Relevance Propagation)** [FOUND] — Achtibat, Hatefi et al., ICML 2024 (so technically pre-dates Joseph's stated window, but flagged because 2025 work builds directly on it and Joseph's prior-knowledge framing under-weighted it). Propagates relevance backward through the *full* computational graph including the nonlinear attention mechanism (prior LRP variants for transformers struggled specifically with attention's nonlinearity) — cost is roughly one backward pass, i.e. much cheaper than perturbation/Shapley approaches, and unlike attention-only methods it attributes to *latent* representations at every layer, not just the raw attention weights. Code: github.com/rachtibat/LRP-eXplains-Transformers. **2025-2026 applications found:** "LRP4RAG" (arXiv:2408.15533) uses it for RAG hallucination detection; a 2025 paper "Close Look at Decomposition-based XAI-Methods for Transformer LMs" and a 2026 "Hidden Dimensions of LLM Alignment" safety-analysis paper both cite it — I did not deep-read either, flagging as leads not verified findings.

**Integrated Gradients specifically:** [PRIOR + weak FOUND corroboration] My prior knowledge says IG is the classical gradient-attribution baseline (Sundararajan et al. 2017) and remains a standard comparison point cited in newer long-context papers (e.g. one of the search snippets above lists "gradient-based methods such as Integrated Gradients" as part of the standard baseline set assumed by current context-attribution papers, alongside perturbation and attention methods) — but I did not find a 2025/2026 paper doing IG specifically *at 100k+ token scale*; the field seems to have largely moved past pure IG for long-context work toward the attention-probe (AT2) and hierarchical-perturbation (TracLLM) families instead, likely because IG's per-token gradient computation and multi-step path integral don't amortize well at very long context lengths (a plausible-not-verified inference on my part — flagging explicitly per the plausibility-vs-verification discipline, this is my inference from the shape of what did and didn't turn up, not a claim I verified against a specific paper stating IG doesn't scale).
