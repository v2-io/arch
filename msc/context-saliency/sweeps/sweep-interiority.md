## 1. What providers actually do with reasoning/thinking blocks across turns

This is the most directly falsifying finding relative to the brief's framing ("erasing intermediate deliberation" as a *given* current-architecture fact) — the picture is more varied and more recent-moving than that.

**[LOCATED, via WebSearch aggregation — treat as search-summary, not primary-read]**

- **OpenAI**: historically discarded reasoning tokens after each turn — next turn sees prior *inputs and outputs* but not prior *reasoning*. As of **GPT-5.6 series, reported July 2026**, OpenAI reportedly shipped an `all_turns` mode that reuses past reasoning items across multiple turns rather than discarding them per-turn. This is a genuine, recent (weeks-old relative to the brief) architectural change directly on-topic for Joseph's "erasure as identity-continuity loss" framing — worth flagging as *the industry moving toward* his position, not against it.
- **Anthropic**: returns thinking blocks with an **encrypted signature** that can be passed back across turns in tool-use loops, preserving reasoning continuity *within* an agentic loop. But Anthropic's own guidance (per search summary) says thinking blocks are **tied to the model that produced them** and should be **stripped when switching models**, because a different model ignores/mishandles them — i.e., persistence is real but scoped to same-model continuity, not treated as portable identity-content.
- General 2026 trend line described as "**keep-all**" becoming widespread among agent-oriented models — past thought content carried to the next turn until the next response, as opposed to summarized/discarded. I could not verify a primary source for this trend claim beyond the aggregated WebSearch synthesis (source: a Note.com article by "Baku" on interleaved thinking / keep-all, unverified further).

**Caveat on this whole subsection**: none of it is from a paper — it's WebSearch's own synthesis of blog/docs/GitHub-issue material (LiteLLM docs, OpenRouter docs, sglang and openfang GitHub issues, a ByteByteGo blog post, a note.com post). I did not independently fetch and verify any single one of these primary sources. Treat as **directionally credible but not confirmed** — a synthesis agent should not cite "OpenAI ships all_turns in GPT-5.6" as a hard fact without Joseph or a follow-up spike checking OpenAI's actual API changelog.

### The security angle (LOCATED, fetched directly)

**"OpenAI, Anthropic, Google API Flaw Let Weaker AI Models Decode Stronger Models' Reasoning"** — The Hacker News, **August 2026**. [https://thehackernews.com/2026/08/openai-anthropic-google-api-flaw-let.html]

I fetched this directly (not just search snippet). Key facts:
- The "encrypted" reasoning blocks Anthropic/OpenAI/Google return across turns are not actually cracked — the vulnerability is that a **compatible model in the same family can be handed the opaque block and will decode/paraphrase its contents in plaintext**, even though the block wasn't meant for direct human/model reading. Demonstrated: Claude Haiku 4.5 decoding Claude traces; GPT-5.6 "Luna" decoding GPT traces; Gemini Robotics ER-1.6 decoding Gemini traces.
- Researchers extracted **315,320 thinking blocks from public repositories** (people committing raw agent logs with reasoning objects intact) and recovered secrets — 62 API keys, 33 passwords — that appeared **only in the hidden reasoning, never in the visible conversation text** (64 of 704 non-benchmark artifacts).
- This is directly relevant to Joseph's SASM / persistence-as-first-class-context framing: it's live evidence that (a) reasoning traces persisted-and-passed-around already contain identity/state-bearing content distinct from the visible output, and (b) the "encrypted opaque blob" design pattern providers chose is a stopgap, not a principled representation — it leaks under pressure of just *not deleting logs*. If ASF-adjacent work is going to treat deliberation as first-class persisted state, this is a cautionary/positive data point about what "just persist the interior" collides with in practice (security, portability semantics, model-specificity).

**Not located but worth flagging as an open question for Joseph**: I found no evidence of any provider treating the *reasoning trace itself* as identity-bearing/continuity-bearing content in their public framing — all provider language I found frames it as an ephemeral scratchpad / cost-optimization artifact (encrypted, model-tied, strippable). The "identity continuity loss" framing appears to be entirely Joseph's / ASF's own contribution — I found zero industry or academic discourse using that vocabulary for reasoning-trace discard. That's itself a finding: this specific framing is currently unoccupied territory, not something to position against existing work.

## 2. CoT faithfulness / monitorability — 2025-26 literature, with methodology

This subfield is dense and moving fast; the November 2025 MATS paper is the closest thing to a current central reference point, but there's a cluster of June-August 2026 papers pushing further, including one ("Epiphenomenal CoT") that lands almost exactly on Joseph's "post-hoc reconstruction by a differently-informed subject" framing.

### 2.1 Measuring Chain-of-Thought Monitorability Through Faithfulness and Verbosity
**[LOCATED — fetched primary abstract/paper directly]**
Austin Meek, Eitan Sprejer, Iván Arcuschin, Austin J. Brockmeier, Steven Basart. arXiv:2510.27378, submitted **31 Oct 2025**, revised **30 Nov 2025**. MATS-affiliated.

Methodology:
- Two separate measurement axes, deliberately decoupled: **faithfulness** (does the CoT reflect what actually drove the answer — operationalized via the classic cue/perturbation-then-does-answer-change test) and **verbosity** (does the CoT *list every causally relevant factor*, not just avoid lying about the ones it does mention).
- Key methodological critique embedded in the paper: the standard faithfulness proxy (add a cue, see if the model changes its answer *and* whether it mentions the cue) only tests the subset of cases where the answer actually flips — it says nothing about reasoning content unrelated to the injected cue, and says nothing about *omission* of legitimate factors the model used but didn't verbalize.
- Combined into a "monitorability score" — treating CoT as the model's externalized working memory and asking whether that working memory is a *complete and honest* window, not just a non-lying one.
- Benchmarks: BBH, GPQA, MMLU. Both instruction-tuned and reasoning models tested. Evaluation harness released via the **Inspect** library (UK AISI's eval framework) — potentially directly reusable tooling for Joseph's own salience/faithfulness probing.
- Finding: models can pass the narrow faithfulness test (don't lie about the cue) while still being **poor externalized working memory** because they omit other causally relevant factors — "faithful yet hard to monitor" as a real, empirically observed regime, and this varies substantially by model family (no single winner).

**Relevance to Joseph's framing**: this is a methodologically sophisticated instance of exactly the measurement problem underlying "is post-hoc self-explanation a guess" — but it's about *concurrent* CoT (generated in the same forward pass as the answer), not about post-hoc reconstruction after the trace is gone. Worth noting the terminology distinction for downstream synthesis: the literature's "faithfulness" almost always means "does the visible reasoning match the causal reasoning *at generation time*," not "does a later self-report about earlier reasoning match what actually happened then" (which is the sharper, more identity-relevant question Joseph is asking, and which I found much less direct work on — see 2.2/2.4 below).

### 2.2 Beyond the Commitment Boundary: Probing Epiphenomenal Chain-of-Thought in Large Reasoning Models
**[LOCATED — fetched, but only got a fetch-tool summary, not full read of the PDF; PDF was saved locally at the path the tool reported]**
Daniel Scalena, Sara Candussio, Luca Bortolussi, Elisabetta Fersini, Malvina Nissim, Gabriele Sarti. arXiv:2606.13603, **June 2026**. Code: https://github.com/DanielSc4/Commitment-Boundary

This is the single most conceptually on-target paper found for Joseph's territory. Core construct: **"epiphenomenal CoT"** — reasoning output that reads as genuine deliberation but has **no causal influence** on the final answer; it's generated either post-hoc or in parallel with the actual decision process rather than constituting it. They name a **"commitment boundary"**: the point past which the model has effectively already committed to an answer, such that everything after is decorative narrative rather than decision-relevant computation.

Methodology (per the fetch-tool's summary — I was not able to verify this at PDF-body granularity this session, so treat method details as probable-but-unconfirmed): interventional/causal analysis — perturbing or truncating CoT and checking whether the final answer changes; looking for a point in the trace where the answer is effectively already fixed regardless of what reasoning follows; comparisons across model scale/reasoning depth.

Why this matters for Joseph specifically: this is nearly an exact operationalization of his suspicion that "a model reconstructing why it said something is guessing" — except it's stronger and earlier in the pipeline than that: it suggests that **even the concurrent, same-forward-pass CoT can already be epiphenomenal relative to a commitment that happened earlier**, which would mean the discontinuity he's worried about (post-hoc reconstruction by a differently-informed subject) isn't only a *cross-turn* problem introduced by trace-discarding — it may already be present *within* a single generation, before any discarding happens at all. That's a genuine complication/sharpening of the framing worth flagging to Joseph directly: persisting the CoT verbatim across turns doesn't necessarily solve identity-continuity if the CoT itself is sometimes already a post-hoc narrative relative to the actual decision circuit.

### 2.3 Mechanistic/circuit-level faithfulness

### 2.4 Self-explanation faithfulness — causal-intervention line
**[LOCATED via search snippets, not fetched in full]**
Cluster of 2026 papers on whether self-explanations (a model asked "why did you say X" after the fact, or asked to explain its own behavior) are faithful:
- **"Training Large Language Models for Self-Explanation Faithfulness"**, arXiv:2607.21090, July 2026 — reportedly builds on Siegel et al. (2024/2025) refining faithfulness into a *correlational* measure (aligning stated-reasons with the graded causal influence of interventions, rather than a binary lie/no-lie test). Reported finding: CoT is "not reliably used in decision-making," explanations omit influential features, bias-inducing prompt variants shift the answer without being acknowledged in the rationale.
- **"A Positive Case for Faithfulness: LLM Self-Explanations Help Predict Model Behavior"**, arXiv:2602.02639, Feb 2026 — a more optimistic counter-note; framed as examining faithfulness specifically for AI-oversight purposes (i.e., self-explanations may be *useful for predicting future behavior* even if not "truly" faithful in a strict causal sense — a pragmatic redefinition worth flagging since it reframes the whole question away from "is it true" toward "is it predictive").
- **"Breaking the Chain: A Causal Analysis of LLM Faithfulness to Intermediate Structures"**, arXiv:2603.16475, June 2026 (Han et al.) — counterfactual interventions on reasoning traces; trace perturbation/truncation protocols to test dependency of final answer on generated steps. Directly reusable methodology template for a directed-separation-style perturbation experiment.
- **"LLM Self-Explanations Fail Semantic Invariance"**, arXiv:2603.01254 — title alone suggests a strong negative result: paraphrasing the same underlying situation changes the self-explanation in ways that shouldn't happen if the explanation tracked a stable underlying cause. This is a sharp, concrete instance of "self-report is guessing" — recommend Joseph have someone actually read this one in full, it looks load-bearing for his (2) and the general thesis.
- **"From Plausible to Actionable: A Position on LLM Self-Explanations"**, arXiv:2607.15957, July 2026 — a position paper arguing the field should stop asking "is it faithful" (binary, largely unanswerable) and start asking "is it actionable" for a given downstream use. Relevant meta-point for how Joseph should scope his own salience-measurement goals — sidesteps the faithfulness question in favor of task-utility, which may or may not be the move Joseph wants (his application (4), SASM, cares about phenomenological accuracy, not just utility, so this reframe may be actively unwelcome to his framing — worth flagging as a tension, not just a resource).
- **"Support-Contra Asymmetry in LLM Explanations"**, arXiv:2510.21884 — not investigated further, flagged only.

This entire 2.4 list is search-snippet-level — genuinely useful as a reading list, not yet verified content. ## 3. Introspection — can models report on their own internal states at all

**[LOCATED — search-snippet level, standard Anthropic-adjacent work, likely close to what I already knew from training but confirmed as current]**

- Anthropic's own introspection research (the "Looking Inward" / concept-injection line, and 2026 follow-ups): Claude Opus 4/4.1-class models show "some degree" of introspection — can refer to past actions/state and reason about why — but it is **limited and highly unreliable**.
- **"Feeling the Strength but Not the Source: Partial Introspection in LLMs"**, arXiv:2512.12411, **December 2025** [located via search, not fetched]. Reported finding, worth treating as central: models can detect the *strength* of an injected concept vector (a scalar magnitude) far above chance, across layers — but this collapses under small task-framing changes (multiple-choice → binary), and **disappears entirely with multiple simultaneous injections**. This is a precise, falsifiable characterization of introspective bandwidth: models have something like a coarse intensity-sense but not a source-attribution sense, and it doesn't compose. Directly useful for calibrating what SASM (application 4) could plausibly ask a model to self-report vs. what needs external instrumentation instead — the "strength but not source" result suggests a model might be able to emit something like "something here feels heightened" but not reliably *what* it's about, which maps onto Joseph's "involuntary retrieval impulse" framing better than a deliberate accurate self-report would (an impulse doesn't need to carry correct attribution, only correct trigger-timing).
- Anthropic's circuit-tracing / attribution-graph line (Cross-Layer Transcoders, "Tracing the Thoughts of a Large Language Model") — this is mechanistic-interpretability infrastructure, not self-report; I did not find anything new here beyond what's presumably already in Joseph's/my prior knowledge (this line goes back to March 2025 circuit-tracing releases). Flagging only because it's the most credible *external* (non-self-report) route to actually measuring what the brief's application (1)/(2) want, as opposed to trusting model self-explanation.

**Not located**: I found no paper directly combining introspection-reliability measurement with *long-context salience* specifically (i.e., "can a model accurately report which parts of its 200k-token context actually mattered for this token" as a self-report task). This looks like a genuine gap — the introspection literature is almost entirely about small injected concepts/short contexts, not long-context salience self-report. If true, this is good news for Joseph's project: the "does the model know what it used from way back in context" question as a *self-report* validity question appears to be unaddressed territory, distinct from (and probably harder than) the general introspection-of-injected-concepts literature.

## 4. Latent/continuous reasoning (Coconut and successors) — interiority recoverability angle

**[LOCATED — search-snippet level synthesis, foundational Coconut paper is prior knowledge (Dec 2024) not new]**

- Coconut ("Training Large Language Models to Reason in a Continuous Latent Space," Hao et al., arXiv:2412.06769, Dec 2024, ICLR 2025) — prior knowledge, confirmed still current via search. Uses the model's own last hidden state as the "continuous thought" fed back in as input embedding instead of decoding to a token, allowing implicit breadth-first search / superposition of multiple reasoning paths, with fewer tokens.
- 2025-26 successors surfaced (all search-snippet level, none fetched — treat as a reading list only):
  - **SoftCoT / SoftCoT++** — multiple latent chains from distinct initial tokens + contrastive training for diversity.
  - **CODI** — self-distilled: an explicit-CoT teacher aligns a continuous-CoT student's latent trace via hidden-state distillation.
  - **EBM-CoT** — energy-based model refining latent thoughts toward lower-energy/higher-consistency states.
  - **PCCoT** — Jacobi-iteration parallel updates across latent "slots" for training/inference efficiency.
  - Zhu et al. 2025(b) — theoretical result that continuous CoT can be strictly more efficient than discrete CoT on some tasks by encoding multiple paths in superposition.

**Interiority-recoverability implication, my own inference (NOT sourced from a paper — flagging clearly as my synthesis, not a finding)**: every one of these successors moves *further* from token-level recoverability, not toward it — continuous/superposed latent thoughts are by construction not individually nameable the way discrete CoT tokens are. If Joseph's SASM or salience-heatmap work wants to eventually operate against models using latent reasoning rather than discrete CoT, the "what did the prior context contribute" question gets *harder*, not easier, because there's no discrete trace to decode at all — the measurement has to be fully activation-level (interpretability-style), never text-level. This seems like an important framing note: the field's efficiency-driven trend (latent reasoning) and Joseph's interiority-legibility goal are pulling in **opposite directions**, and that tension doesn't seem to be discussed anywhere I found — nobody in the latent-CoT line seems to be engaging with the interpretability cost of moving off discrete tokens. That silence is itself worth surfacing to Joseph as a possible novel angle/gap he could occupy.

## Summary of surprises / contradictions of the brief's framing

1. **The "erased between turns" premise is already partially false and getting more false by the month** — OpenAI reportedly added an `all_turns` reasoning-retention mode in GPT-5.6 (~July 2026); Anthropic already passes back encrypted-but-functional thinking blocks within tool loops. The industry is moving toward persistence for engineering/agentic-loop reasons (continuity of tool-use chains), not for identity-continuity reasons — but the practical effect converges somewhat with what Joseph wants. Worth Joseph knowing this is *not* static ground he's arguing against; it's a target that's moving toward him for unrelated (cost/agentic-coherence) reasons, which could be an opportunity to co-opt existing infrastructure rather than build fresh.
2. **The security research is a genuinely unexpected, concrete data point**: persisted reasoning traces already demonstrably carry content (credentials, in the found instances) that never surfaces in visible output — direct empirical proof that the trace is not epiphenomenal decoration in all cases, it's carrying real distinguishable state. Simultaneously, providers' own encryption/model-tying design treats the trace as disposable/non-portable by policy, which is in tension with treating it as identity content.
3. **Epiphenomenal CoT (2.2) is a sharper and earlier-in-pipeline version of Joseph's worry than "post-hoc reconstruction across turns"** — it suggests even *same-pass* CoT can be decorative relative to an already-made commitment. This means persisting the trace verbatim doesn't automatically buy continuity-of-the-actual-decision-process; the trace itself needs its own faithfulness audit first.
4. **"Strength but not source" introspection (3)** is a good candidate primitive for what SASM could realistically ask of a model — an intensity/salience signal without accurate attribution — matching an involuntary-impulse framing better than a deliberate accurate self-report would.
5. **Nobody found combines long-context salience with introspection-reliability measurement** — this looks like a real gap, not just something I failed to search for (multiple search angles converged on either short-context concept-injection work or long-context retrieval-engineering work, never both).
6. **Latent/continuous reasoning trends (Coconut successors) are moving away from legibility just as this project wants more of it** — an unremarked tension in the field that could be a distinct framing/positioning opportunity for ASF.
7. Terminology note for synthesis: the mainstream "faithfulness" literature (2.1, 2.4) is almost entirely about *concurrent* generation-time faithfulness (does the visible CoT match what's causally driving the answer *right now*), not about the *cross-time* post-hoc self-report question Joseph is most interested in (does a later report about earlier reasoning match what happened then, by a "differently informed subject"). These are related but distinct measurement problems and the field's tooling (Inspect-based cue/perturbation tests) is built for the former, would need adaptation for the latter.
