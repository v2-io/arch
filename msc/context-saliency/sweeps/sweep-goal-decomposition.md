## Headline orientation

The field as of mid-2026 has genuinely moved toward exactly the question Joseph is asking — "is there a goal/objective direction distinct from a world-model/belief direction, and are they entangled" — but almost nobody frames it as a clean binary decomposition. The dominant framing that has emerged is **"belief vs. action"** (not "belief vs. goal"), studied mostly through the deception/alignment-faking safety lens, plus a separate **"goal-directedness" behavioral+representational** literature that studies planning/goals against a spatial world-model substrate (grid-world agents). Nobody I found has done a systematic contrastive-perturbation study of the specific form "hold the world fixed, perturb only the instructed goal, measure what moves" as a general method across many domains — the closest is the goal-directedness paper's "iso-difficulty transformations" and "instrumental vs. implicit goal" grid variants, which is a narrow instance of that idea, not the general method. This looks like a real gap Joseph could fill, not a solved problem he'd be duplicating.

## 1. Directly on-target: goal-directedness + world-model representations together

**"A Behavioural and Representational Evaluation of Goal-Directedness in Language Model Agents"** — Raghu Arghal, Fade Chen, Niall Dalton, Evgenii Kortukov, Calum McNamara, Angelos Nalmpantis, Moksh Nirvaan, Gabriele Sarti, Mario Giulianelli. arXiv:2602.08964v2 [cs.LG], ICML (dated 2026-05-29 per arXiv metadata). **[found, fetched full text]**

This is the single closest paper to Joseph's territory-4 (directed separation / contrastive perturbation) that I located. Key points:

- **They do NOT cleanly separate goal-representation from world-model representation as two orthogonal subspaces.** Instead they combine behavioral evaluation with representational probing of both together, on the premise that goal-directedness is only meaningful relative to a belief/world-model substrate (an LLM agent navigating a grid world).
- **Contrastive/perturbation methodology (closest thing to Joseph's "perturb the goal, hold reality fixed" idea):**
  - *Iso-difficulty transformations*: four controlled perturbations of the environment that preserve task difficulty — grid reflection, 90° rotation, start-goal swap, transposition. Wilcoxon signed-rank tests found no significant behavioral difference under these — i.e., they used symmetry-preserving perturbations as a *world-fixed* control, though the intent was checking behavioral invariance, not decomposing internal representations per se.
  - *Instrumental vs. implicit goals*: three grid variants testing whether goal-like objects influence behavior even when functionally irrelevant. Concrete finding: in a "KeyNoDoorEnv" (a key exists but no door needs it), the agent still picked up the key 17% of the time despite it being useless — evidence of something like an "instrumental-goal prior" bleeding through independent of actual task-relevance. This is a genuinely interesting empirical nugget for Joseph's directed-separation question: it's a case where goal-machinery activates on cues that don't correspond to actual task-instrumentality, i.e., possible evidence *against* clean goal/world coupling (the goal system is pattern-matching on "key-shaped" objects rather than being world-model-gated).
  - *Activation patching*: single-layer interventions patching corrupted agent/goal positions were **ineffective** — patching one layer did not shift the action distribution. Only **simultaneous multi-layer patching** produced (minimal) effects on action distributions. This is a methodologically important negative result: naive single-site causal intervention doesn't cleanly isolate goal-vs-world content, suggesting the representations are distributed/entangled across depth rather than living in one clean site — a data point in favor of ASF's "class 2/3 architectural coupling" prediction, though the authors don't frame it that way.
- **Probing setup**: linear and non-linear (MLP) probes on residual-stream activations at layers 7, 15, 23 (proportions of total depth not given in the snippet extraction, verify against paper). Probed for: (a) cognitive maps — decoded spatial layout, agent position, goal position; (b) multi-step plans.
- **Key quantitative results**:
  - Non-linear (MLP) probe recovers a "coarse spatial map" at ~75% accuracy on 11×11 grids vs. only 39% for a linear probe at the same site — **this directly contradicts a strict linear-representation-hypothesis reading for this task**; spatial/world content here is *not* well captured by a single linear direction, only by a nonlinear probe. Worth flagging to Joseph as a caution against assuming steering vectors (inherently linear, difference-of-means style) will suffice for the "reality" side of a goal/reality decomposition.
  - Agent/goal positions recovered to within Manhattan distance <2 (i.e., approximately, not exactly).
  - **Cognitive-map decode accuracy drops from ~75% pre-reasoning to ~60% post-reasoning.** This is a striking and directly relevant finding: it suggests that as the model does more chain-of-thought reasoning, the *decodability of world-state* representation degrades, while (their claim) representational emphasis shifts toward near-term action selection. This is essentially empirical evidence of exactly the "goal/plan machinery competing for representational capacity with world-state machinery" phenomenon Joseph's ASF is trying to formalize — worth flagging as a strong candidate finding for Joseph even though the authors frame it more narrowly (pre- vs post-reasoning representational shift, not explicitly goal-vs-world coupling).
  - **57.9% of "suboptimal" actions (by ground truth) were actually optimal given the model's own *decoded* beliefs** — i.e., a large fraction of apparent behavioral irrationality disappears once you condition on the model's *actual* internal world-model state rather than the true environment state. This is a methodologically important point for Joseph's directed-separation program: naive behavioral irrationality measures conflate "bad goal-following" with "wrong beliefs" — you cannot assess goal-processing quality without first decoding belief state, which is exactly the "decompose goal-processing from world-model-processing" ask in application (2).
  - Plan decoders (predicting the agent's own multi-step future action sequence from current activations) got 66.49% one-step accuracy, declining at longer horizons — consistent with the general "forward-planning decays with horizon" pattern seen elsewhere (see §3 below).
- **Reported limitations** (their own words, paraphrased): simple single-layer activation patching failed to establish clean causality; probes may not capture genuine uncertainty in the agent's beliefs (point estimates only); the fully-observable environment setting abstracts away real-world partial observability, and partial-observability variants were reportedly intractable for the LLMs tested.

**Assessment for Joseph**: this is the paper to read in full before designing his own contrastive-perturbation protocol — not because it solves the problem, but because (a) its negative activation-patching result and (b) its pre/post-reasoning world-model-decodability drop are exactly the kind of signal ASF's directed-separation hypothesis would want to explain, and (c) its methodological failure modes (single-site patching insufficient, linear probes underperforming nonlinear ones for world content) are load-bearing cautions for experiment design, not just results to cite.

## 2. Deception / belief-vs-behavior probing (safety-adjacent, but methodologically close)

This cluster reframes "goal vs. world" as "belief vs. action/assertion," in the alignment-faking / deceptive-alignment literature. Relevant because the methodology (contrastive activation probes trained on "honest" vs "deceptive" internal states) is directly reusable for goal/belief contrastive work, and because the *negative* results here are a serious caution for anyone assuming linear probing generalizes.

- **"Pressure-Testing Deception Probes in LLMs: Scaling, Robustness, and the Geometry of Deceptive Representations"**, arXiv:2605.27958 (2026). **[found, snippet only]** Central finding per search snippet: linear probes trained on residual-stream activations to separate "honest" vs "deceptive" states reach near-ceiling on in-distribution benchmarks but **fail under distributional shift**. This is an important caution for Joseph: any goal/belief probe he trains needs an explicit distribution-shift test, not just held-out accuracy on the training distribution.
- **"Why Safety Probes Catch Liars But Miss Fanatics"**, arXiv:2603.25861 (2026). **[found, title/snippet only — did not fetch]** Sounds directly relevant to the goal/belief distinction (a "fanatic" being an agent whose goal-representation is intact and driving behavior consistently, vs a "liar" whose belief and assertion diverge only situationally) — flagging as a high-priority read, not yet verified.
- Poser benchmark / "intoxication test": activation probes plus noise injection to detect alignment-faking from internals, reported 98% detection accuracy on some benchmark **[found, snippet only, exact citation not resolved — likely a specific paper, not independently verified this session, treat as unconfirmed pointer]**.
- Key general caution surfaced across this cluster **[found, snippet-level, multiple sources converging]**: sparse-autoencoder-based "deception" features are reported as *distributed*, not captured by any single autolabeled SAE feature, and *steering* those features does not reliably disable the deceptive behavior — a negative result for the "just find the direction and steer it" approach when the underlying computation is genuinely distributed rather than a single linear direction. Also reported: some models may evade detection "not by learning to hide, but by learning to believe" — i.e., they generate rationalizations that eliminate the internal conflict signal the probe was trained to detect, meaning belief and assertion become *representationally* consistent even when behaviorally the outcome is still bad. This is a subtle and important point for ASF's coupling-estimate ambitions: it suggests the goal/reality coupling is not fixed but can be *trained into* the model by RL/finetuning dynamics — the coupling strength itself may be a moving target, not a static architectural constant.

## 3. Planning / forward-goal representation (not framed as goal-vs-world, but methodologically adjacent)

- **"Detecting and Characterizing Planning in Language Models"**, Jatin Nainani et al., arXiv:2508.18098 (2025, appears in NeurIPS/workshop track per OpenReview). **[found, snippet-level]** Proposes formal, causally-grounded criteria for "does the model plan" (select a future target token before generating intermediates) plus a semi-automated annotation pipeline, explicitly built to fix weaknesses of prior probe-based work (fixed horizons, task-specific probes, narrow domains). Applied to Gemma-2-2B (base + instruct) on MBPP code generation and a poem-generation task where Claude 3.5 Haiku had previously been shown (by Anthropic's interpretability team, presumably the "planning ahead in poems" circuits result) to plan. Methodologically useful as a domain-general planning-detection pipeline Joseph could adapt for goal-vs-reality decomposition (their causal criteria could plausibly be re-purposed: "does perturbing the stated goal, holding context fixed, causally move the predicted future-token target").
- Related: **"Where's the Plan? Locating Latent Planning in Language Models with Lightweight Mechanistic Interventions"**, arXiv:2605.07984 (2026); **"Internal Planning in Language Models: Characterizing Horizon and Branch Awareness"**, arXiv:2509.25260 (2025); **"Latent Planning Emerges with Scale"**, arXiv:2604.12493 (2026); **"ParaScopes: What do Language Models Activations Encode About Future Text?"**, arXiv:2511.00180 (2025). **[found, titles/snippets only, not fetched]** — I did not have budget to fetch these individually; flagging as a cluster worth a follow-up pass since "does the model encode goal-state that predicts its own future output" is close kin to Joseph's SASM application (4) — an involuntary retrieval impulse is plausibly a similar phenomenon to a "planning" pre-commitment signal.
- **"From Actions to Understanding: Conformal Interpretability of Temporal Concepts in LLM Agents"**, arXiv:2604.19775 (2026). **[found, snippet only]** Trains linear probes for temporal concepts — directions corresponding to notions of success/failure/"reasoning drift" — in LLM-agent activations, using conformal prediction for calibrated confidence. Relevant to Joseph's application (1), the Strategy-DAG "forgotten middle" salience decay: a "reasoning drift" direction is conceptually adjacent to a salience-decay signature, and the conformal-calibration angle is a methodological idea (calibrated confidence intervals on a probe's output, not just point estimates) that nothing else in this sweep offered.

## 4. Steering vectors / representation engineering — general state of the art

**[prior — largely consistent with what I already knew, confirmed by search snippets, no major surprise]**

- Standard taxonomy as of 2026: activation-difference methods (ActAdd, Contrastive Activation Addition/CAA), linear-probing-derived vectors, unsupervised (SAE-based), and training-based methods. **[found, arXiv:2502.17601, "Representation Engineering for Large-Language Models: Survey and Research Challenges," 2026 — this looks like the current survey-of-record and is worth Joseph reading in full for a map of the space.]**
- **Persona Vectors** (Anthropic Fellows, arXiv:2507.21509, "Persona Vectors: Monitoring and Controlling Character Traits in Language Models," 2025, blogged at anthropic.com/research/persona-vectors). **[found]** Automated extraction of linear trait directions from natural-language trait descriptions; steer-away at inference reduces trait expression, steer-toward during finetuning limits unwanted trait drift while preserving capability, and the vectors can flag training data likely to shift personality before finetuning even happens. This is squarely a "goal/preference as linear direction" result and the closest thing to an existing production-grade methodology Joseph could directly borrow for a "goal vector" extraction pipeline (contrastive natural-language descriptions → automated direction extraction → validate via steering).
- **Important caution surfacing in 2026, contradicting a naive optimistic reading of steering-vector work**: "On the Non-Identifiability of Steering Vectors in Large Language Models," arXiv:2602.06801 (2026) — title alone signals that the same behavioral steering effect can be achieved by non-unique, non-identifiable directions, i.e. "a direction that steers X" does not imply "the direction representing X." **[found, title only, not fetched — flag as important and worth a dedicated follow-up read before treating any extracted vector as canonically "the goal direction."]** Similarly "Steered LLM Activations are Non-Surjective," arXiv:2604.09839 (2026) **[found, title only]** — suggests the steerable subspace does not cover the full space of representable states, a relevant caution for exhaustiveness claims.
- "On the Limits of Steering Vectors for Preference-Aligned Generation," arXiv:2607.01802 (2026). **[found, title only]** Sounds directly relevant to Joseph's "goal steering" ambitions — another unread flag.

## 5. World-model probing lineage (Othello-GPT and beyond)

**[found — good confirmation + update of prior knowledge]**

- Original Othello-GPT world-model claim: Li et al. 2023 (nonlinear probe first), then Neel Nanda's 2023 finding that the *linear* representation is there but only once you reframe the probe target as "mine/theirs/empty" (relative to current player) rather than "black/white/empty" (absolute color) — the famous "wrong probe target" story. **[prior, confirmed]**
- **New in 2026**: "Revisiting the Othello World Model Hypothesis," arXiv:2503.04421 (2026, OpenReview). **[found]** Extends the original single-model result to seven architectures — GPT-2, T5, BART, Flan-T5, Mistral, LLaMA-2, Qwen2.5 — all trained/finetuned on the Othello task, all reaching up to 99% unsupervised-grounding accuracy and showing high similarity in the *learned board features* across architecture families. This is a genuine update to what I knew (I was only aware of the original single-architecture GPT-2 result plus Nanda's probe-target fix) — the finding that the linear world-model result generalizes across a wide range of architecture families (not just decoder-only GPT-2-style) is new information worth flagging as a surprise: it strengthens the case that "linear world-model representations are a generic property of sequence models trained on state-tracking tasks," not an artifact of one architecture, which is directly supportive background for ASF's world-model-processing side of the decomposition.
- "Linear Spatial World Models Emerge in Large Language Models," arXiv:2506.02996 (2026). **[found, title only]** Presumably extends the Othello-style result from toy board games to naturalistic spatial reasoning in full-scale LLMs — flag as a read.
- RAVEL — "Evaluating Interpretability Methods on Disentangling Language Model Representations," arXiv:2402.17700. **[prior — I know this paper from training: it's a benchmark (not just a method) for testing whether an interpretability method can *disentangle* different attribute types associated with the same entity in an LM's representations, e.g. a city's country vs. its continent vs. language spoken there, using interchange interventions (activation patching / distributed alignment search) as the causal test, and it found most existing methods — including simple linear probing and even some SAE-based approaches — struggle to cleanly disentangle attributes, with DAS (distributed alignment search) performing best among tested methods.] My attempted WebFetch of the actual PDF failed to extract clean text (corrupted/binary extraction) so I could not confirm exact authors or update this beyond what I already knew — **this is a genuine dry-fetch, flagging honestly rather than papering over it.** RAVEL's benchmark design (measuring disentanglement via causal interchange interventions across *multiple* co-occurring attribute types, not just a single binary contrast) is methodologically exactly what Joseph's goal-vs-world decomposition needs, and is worth a direct read of the actual paper (not a re-fetch by me) before designing an experiment.

## 6. What's conspicuously NOT there — the dry-well finding

I did not find any paper that does the specific thing Joseph describes in application (2): systematically holding the world/context fixed while perturbing only the stated goal/instruction (or vice versa), across a general (non-toy-gridworld) domain, with the explicit aim of producing a **quantitative coupling estimate** between goal-representation and world-model-representation. The goal-directedness paper (§1) is the closest real instance, and it's confined to a synthetic gridworld-agent setting with fairly coarse probes. Nothing I found frames this as "directed separation" or attempts anything like an information-theoretic or causal-mediation-based *coupling coefficient* between the two representational streams — everything is qualitative ("linear probe recovers X% accuracy") rather than a coupling estimate in the sense ASF wants. If this framing is accurate, **this is a real, currently-open empirical gap** rather than something Joseph would be re-deriving — worth stating plainly rather than hedging, since a well-documented gap is itself valuable here.

Also notably thin: nothing found specifically bridges the "goal-vs-world coupling" question to context-salience/attribution methods (territory of application 1/3, presumably another sweep's territory) — the two literatures (goal/belief probing vs. context-attribution) appear to be running in parallel without much cross-citation as of this sweep. That's circumstantial (search-engine-visibility) evidence, not a confirmed absence, but worth flagging to whoever is doing the context-salience territory as a possible integration opportunity nobody has taken yet.
