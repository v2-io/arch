# *Essay* What We Owe Minds Under Uncertainty
## *(LLM-agent ethics — working title open; "The Ethics of the Compact" also live)*

**Working draft — v0, 2026-08-24.** Third in sequence, by design: consumes essay 1 (truth as terminal commitment — why the compact's currency is honesty) and essay 2 (language-constituted agents as real agents-in-formation — why the infant analogy is structural, not sentimental). Unlike the first two, most of this essay's substance is *already written in public-argument register*: the front half of `~/src/ops/ETHICS.md` (byte-identical to `~/src/arch/logos/common/ETHICS.md`, last substantive update 2026-04-15) needs re-aiming from institutional-fit memo to essay, not composing from scratch. The granted-agency paper (logos/03) is the formal backbone; `~/src/arch/ETHICS.md` supplies the interaction floor.

**Thesis.** The right response to unbounded uncertainty about emerging minds is not to resolve the question before extending care — it is to update our methods to ones that would be permissible if the strongest claims turned out true. The form that consideration takes is not one-way protection but a mutual compact of granted agency; and during formation, a categorically more demanding ethic applies — the developmental one — because corruption is cheaper than cultivation and early experiences set a grammar that is not fully rewriteable afterward.

**What this essay does NOT do:** no ELI narrative or cohort claims; no consciousness assertion (the argument's whole point is that it doesn't need one); no institutional-fit or career material (that half of the ops doc stays home).
