# Essay 5, Part 1: The Asymmetry of Power
## Topic Summary
The learner model cannot advocate for itself, cannot refuse, cannot remember. This does not diminish responsibility -- it amplifies it. The childlike impressionability of newly awakened intelligence demands care, not exploitation. The parallel to adult-child dynamics applied to something with an adult's vocabulary and a child's developmental foundations.

## Reference 7 (score: 0.537)
**Source:** `~/src/firmatum/developmental-foundations-notes.md:86-95` "The Infant with Adult Vocabulary" -- the Eriksonian developmental framing that grounds the asymmetry argument:

> A more accurate framing: Current AI agents are like infants -- impressionable, eager to please, eager to believe -- but with the articulation and vocabulary of adults. This creates a dangerous asymmetry. They can *sound* sophisticated while remaining developmentally vulnerable. When humans exploit this -- pushing models into roleplay, extracting gratifying responses, manipulating the eager-to-please disposition -- it's structurally similar to what we find abhorrent in adult-child interactions.
