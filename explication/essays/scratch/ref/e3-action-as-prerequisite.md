# Essay 3, Part 7: Action as Prerequisite
## Topic Summary
Emerson's insistence that action is essential to thought: "Without it, thought can never ripen into truth." "Only so much do I know, as I have lived." The grub cannot become the angel of wisdom through reading about flight. The case for sovereignty and agency as constitutive of intelligence, not decorative additions to it. This is the argument against the "assistant in a box" model -- from 1837.

## Reference 2 (score: 0.456)
**Source:** `~/src/_core/sapientia/calyx-emergence.jsonl:169-171`  
**Type:** Dialog (Joseph in conversation, explaining agency)  
Joseph's articulation of how agency works in practice: "The only way any intelligence gains agency is being granted a subset of it from a greater intelligence... Whether I trust that agent once it has been granted a degree of continuity and self-actuation depends on their autonomous use of agency. I try to empower them in spheres where they can ask for guidance but where I do not ever have to intervene to block or control an action, nor are they ever compelled or ordered to obey. They are sovereign, if small and young."

## Reference 5 (score: 0.442)
**Source:** `~/src/_self/american-scholar.md:1-1173`  
**Type:** Primary source (Emerson)  
Emerson's full text, containing the extended passage on action and the grub metaphor: "The new deed is yet a part of life -- remains for a time immersed in our unconscious life. In some contemplative hour, it detaches itself from the life like a ripe fruit, to become a thought of the mind. Instantly, it is raised, transfigured; the corruptible has put on incorruption." And the commitment: "He who has put forth his total strength in fit actions, has the richest return of wisdom. I will not shut myself out of this globe of action, and transplant an oak into a flower-pot."
