Plain English and pre-spike vocabulary throughout; the one term of art carried over is "directed separation," which here just means: the agent's belief-update never looks at its goals. ## The question all three chase

When we describe an agent as having *beliefs* (its picture of the world) and *goals* (what it wants), is that division something real about the agent, or just a convenient way of talking? Canon had long marked its own answer honestly: the belief/goal split of the agent's state was introduced as a *formulation choice*, with an explicitly unproved conjecture that any alternative split preserving directed separation would come out essentially the same. Three audit substrates had flagged the conjecture as a candidate theorem; nobody had attempted it. It was commissioned as a spike because the FAST paper's central move — defining beliefs *counterfactually*, by what varies when you vary the goals — would be much stronger if the conjecture resolved either way: proved, the definition is forced; refuted, the counterexample says how to scope it.

## Spike 1 — the uniqueness question itself ✅ *verified (three independent audit rounds), integrated into canon*

**The answer came in two halves, and both halves matter.**

**Half one: the conjecture as stated is false — you cannot find the belief/goal boundary by watching the agent run.** The sharp counterexample is disarmingly ordinary: take any well-behaved agent whose goal is *not currently changing* — a thermostat's setpoint, a commander's standing objective between revisions, which by canon's own timescale ordering is the *typical* condition, since objectives revise much more slowly than beliefs update. That frozen goal behaves, in every way the agent's own running dynamics can express, exactly like a belief: it updates on nothing, nothing else updates it, and the planning machinery reads it just as it reads beliefs. So "beliefs = the agent's world-picture including its standing objective, goals = only its plan revisions" is *just as valid* a separation-preserving description as the intended one — and provably not equivalent to it. A stored setpoint and a stored fact are indistinguishable from inside one agent's history. The division we care about is real, but it is not located in the update behavior.

Three serious attempts to rescue the conjecture *without* invoking interventions each failed for an identifiable reason, and the failures are the philosophical content: defining beliefs as "what the agent uses to predict" is circular (predictions come *from* the beliefs); defining them behaviorally is goal-contaminated (behavior is produced by beliefs and goals jointly); defining them by the wiring diagram is symmetric between the roles. The common shape: along the agent's single actual history, belief and frozen purpose are *confounded* — nothing that only looks at what actually happened can pull them apart.

**Half two: add the ability to vary the goals from outside, and the division is not just visible — it is forced.** Relative to a family of goal-interventions (operations that rewrite what the agent wants while leaving its world-picture alone), under three named and individually-necessary hypotheses, *every* admissible separation-preserving decomposition has the same belief component: exactly the part of the state that goal-variation cannot move. The only remaining freedom is how you label the goal side (a "gauge" — relabelings that change nothing observable). So the counterfactual definition of belief is not one choice among equals; **it is the unique completion of the separation condition.** The refutation and the theorem are one fact seen twice: what makes the frozen-setpoint counterexample work (nothing in the dynamics varies the goal) is exactly what the intervention family supplies (something that does).

**The boundary case is the practically important one.** If your interventions can reach only *some* of the goals — and for a language model, prompt-level goal-setting is exactly such a family, unable to reach goal-content baked into the weights — then the purposes you cannot vary *honestly classify as beliefs* under the definition. This holds cleanly within a session (frozen weights) and degrades across goal-conditioned fine-tuning. The moral for any use of the definition: the belief/intent partition is only as fine as the intervention family that defines it, and honest use always names the family.

**Verification history, briefly:** three independent audit rounds; the core (both counterexamples, the theorem, the gauge classification) was confirmed by re-derivation in round one; one genuine defect was found — the restricted-intervention corollary's original formalization could not express "cannot reach this goal-coordinate" at all — and the repair came back *stronger* than the original claim, gaining along the way an exact characterization of when the restricted case even admits a clean decomposition. Final verdict: clean. The refuted conjecture was deleted from canon and replaced (not softened); the theorem lives as a new appendix derivation.

## Spike 2 — does it survive genuine randomness? ⚠ *unverified, not in canon*

The first spike's results were "pathwise": they treated the agent's randomness as fixed in advance, like analyzing one particular run. This spike asked whether everything survives when the agent is treated as genuinely stochastic (as a probability kernel — the setting where canon's coupling measure κ naturally lives). Author-reported results:

- **For disciplined agents (belief-update never influenced by goals): yes, essentially verbatim** — and the hypothesis needed turns out to be *literally* canon's existing "causal discipline" condition, not something new. The author's own prior worry (a technical obstruction it had flagged when closing spike 1) turned out to aim at scaffolding the proof never used — recorded in-file as a lesson: when flagging an obstruction, name which proof step it touches.
- **A bonus with its own interest: noise privacy.** A stochastic agent is separated in the distributional sense *if and only if* it can be built from belief-machinery and goal-machinery driven by *private, independent* randomness. Shared randomness between the two is itself a coupling channel — one that is invisible if you only look at single runs. "Distributional separation = pathwise separation + noise privacy."
- **The break, and it is the most striking finding of the cluster** *(marked: author-tier, unverified)*: for goal-*coupled* agents, "the largest belief-like part" stops being well-defined. An explicit small agent (8 states, arithmetic checkable by inspection) has **two incompatible maximal ways to carve out "beliefs"** — each hides the goal-leak by blurring a different pair of belief-distinctions, and any carving that refined both would expose the leak and stop being goal-blind. Under coupling, *what counts as the agent's beliefs is a choice among maximal options* that cannot be combined.

The author's integration proposal (for the eventual separate integration mind): fold into the existing segment as the discharge of its stated "distributional version open" boundary, and *correct* one of its own discussion-grade claims from spike 1 (a proposed κ-companion gap measure is well-defined only per-carving, not as a single number). It also explicitly warns integrators **not** to inherit its own interpretive vocabulary ("splinters," "convention") into canon above discussion grade — the math licenses existence of incompatible maximal carvings; the psychology-flavored reading is framing.

## Spike 3 — how common is the splintering? ⚠ *unverified, not in canon*

This is the thread you commissioned as "the itch." Author-reported results, which reorganize spike 2's picture substantially:

- **Generically, the question dissolves in the harshest way: a randomly-wired goal-coupled agent has *no* belief part at all.** Not several competing carvings — none: the largest goal-blind summary of a generic coupled agent is a single point. Exact belief, under generic coupling, does not degrade; it vanishes. (Stated honestly in-file as an exactness artifact in part — the practically-relevant approximate version was left open there, and is what the later ε-spike priced.)
- **But real architectures are not random** — weight-tying, repeated modules, exchangeable parts make them precisely the structured exceptions — and on *symmetric* structure, splintering is **forced by the symmetry**: any symmetry of the agent that moves one maximal carving manufactures a second, incompatible one. Spike 2's 8-state witness turns out to be exactly this — its two carvings are mirror images under an internal symmetry, hence *relabelings of each other*: a free choice that costs nothing. **This is a correction the author surfaced against its own spike 2**, which had glossed the two carvings as "retaining different information about the world" — false for that witness; the gloss's verifier is directed to treat it as an author-surfaced finding.
- **The strong form does exist, on a deeper (but robust) stratum**: an explicitly constructed perturbed agent whose two maximal belief-carvings are *dynamically inequivalent* — under one carving the agent's beliefs carry persistent, learnable structure; under the other they are pure noise, nothing to learn. Same agent, same wiring. **Whether the agent has anything to learn can depend on how its beliefs are carved.** The construction has free parameters, so this is a robust family, not a knife-edge.
- The honest hierarchy the spike lands: disciplined → canonical belief, full size; generic coupled → belief annihilated; symmetric coupled → belief splintered but into equivalent presentations (a gauge); tuned coupled → splintered into *genuinely different* belief objects. Each regime separated from the next by explicit conditions.

If the paper wants only what is *verified today*, the spike-1 spine stands alone and is sufficient.

## Tangents worth knowing about

Two process finds from inside the mathematics, both recorded in the spike files: candidate counterexamples that kept getting *forced into consistency* turned the failed attempts into a recipe — the forcing computations told the authors the exact minimal structure a true witness required (this is how both explicit witnesses were found); and the cluster's recurring verification lesson, stated in the parent spike's own closing section, is that a universally-quantified claim checked against the examples that motivated it is the failure-shape that bit three times at three depths — the working countermeasure (construct one adversarial instance *outside* the motivating family before landing) is now a standing guard in the segment.

**Bottom line:** the cluster converts "beliefs vs. goals" from an assumed description into a defined, scoped instrument — visible only under goal-variation, unique once the variation is specified, honest about what it can't reach — and then (pending verification) maps exactly where the instrument's referent exists at all: everywhere for disciplined agents, nowhere for generic coupled ones, and as a structured choice — sometimes free, sometimes consequential — for the tied, symmetric architectures real systems actually are.
