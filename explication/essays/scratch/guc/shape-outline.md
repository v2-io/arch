- Question the paper answers
  - When an agent learns about the world, can what it wants change what it comes to believe?
  - For most of today's AI agents the answer is structurally yes, and almost no one is treating that as an architectural fact with a theory behind it.
- Argument, in order
  - The distinction: goals may choose what you look at; they must not change how you process what you see.
  - Three kinds of agent: the two are built apart; they share some machinery; they are one mechanism.
  - Today's language models are the third kind, provably, by how they are wired.
  - This was an old, solved-feeling question: control theory spent fifty years mapping when estimation and control can be kept apart, and named the case where they can't.
  - But that literature kept the estimator clean by construction; the modern failure is one level deeper, inside the processing itself — so the old results neither cover it nor were carried over.
  - What follows when separation fails: familiar pathologies (believing what serves the goal, telling people what they want to hear, injected instructions becoming beliefs) are one mechanism, not separate quirks — and they admit a common measurement.
  - What can be done: wrapping a coupled model inside a structure that keeps belief-updates goal-blind — with an honest account of what that buys (a certificate vs. a promise), what it costs (speed), and what still leaks.
  - The system-level lift: whether a team of agents stays clean depends on how they are connected and what they share, not on whether they agree — which is where this meets the workshop's interest in populations.
- Register
  - A theory paper that names and organizes; the few formal results carried are already derived elsewhere and cited into place.
  - Positioning against the near-misses: recent work keeps rediscovering single faces of this (injection impossibility, sycophancy probes, engineered quarantine wrappers) without the classification or the lineage.
- What we want from the venue
  - Feedback on the classification and the measurement from the community that called for a systems theory of agents.
