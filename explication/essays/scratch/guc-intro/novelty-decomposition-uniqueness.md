## 1. Bottom line on novelty

**No hit found on the specific result.** Nothing I found proves uniqueness/canonicity of a belief/goal (epistemic/purposeful) state factorization *relative to a goal-intervention family*, with the gauge classification, or the two-sided package (separation underdetermines; interventions force; restricted access coarsens honestly). The *ingredients* are all standard and well-cited (as the segment already says), and the *underdetermination half has strong independent precedent in three separate literatures* — which is good news for the paper (the phenomenon is recognized as real) and means the novelty claim must be scoped to the canonicity theorem + gauge + restricted-access corollary, not to "separation alone underdetermines" as such. Recommended claim shape: "the observation that purely dynamical/behavioral criteria underdetermine the belief/goal split recurs across IRL identifiability, formal-agency work, and radical interpretation [cites]; what appears to be new is the exact canonicity theorem relative to an intervention family, with the residual freedom characterized as a gauge."

Standard caveat: absence of a hit in a one-session web sweep is evidence, not proof; the closest-neighborhood venues (causal-incentives group, FEP/Virgo group, causal abstraction) are active through 2026 and worth a re-check at submission time.

## 2. Citation verification (the spike's named cites)

- **Rubenstein et al. 2017 — VERIFIED, one correction available.** "Causal Consistency of Structural Equation Models," UAI 2017, arXiv:1707.00819. Authors: Rubenstein, Weichwald, Bongers, Mooij, Janzing, Grosse-Wentrup, Schölkopf. It does what the segment credits: defines **exact transformations** between SEMs — maps between models required to commute with (an order-preserving correspondence of) interventions — i.e., "intervention-commuting maps between models" is an accurate gloss. **[abstract + UAI proceedings page]** Note: first-author convention "Rubenstein et al." is right; UAI 2017 is the venue of record (not just arXiv).
- **Beckers & Halpern 2019 — VERIFIED.** "Abstracting Causal Models," AAAI 2019 (AAAI vol. 33, pp. 2678–2685; arXiv:1812.03789). It gives successively stronger restrictions of the Rubenstein et al. notion (uniform transformations → abstraction → strong abstraction → **constructive abstraction**), the constructive case being defined by a partition of low-level variables — the partition-based form closest to the segment's quotient construction. **[abstract]**
- **Chalupka, Perona & Eberhardt — VERIFIED with cite guidance.** "Causal feature learning" and its coarsest-partition constructions are real; the canonical cites are Chalupka, Perona, Eberhardt "Visual Causal Feature Learning" (UAI 2015) and Chalupka, Eberhardt, Perona "Causal feature learning: an overview" (Behaviormetrika 44:137–164, 2017). The **Causal Coarsening Theorem** is the named result. Author order differs between the two papers; cite accordingly. **[abstract]**
- **Kemeny & Snell (lumpability), Burris & Sankappanavar (congruence lattices), Park/Milner (bisimulation)** — standard-reference attributions, uncontroversial; I did not re-verify page-level content. One flag: the segment's Epistemic Status claims "lumpable partitions are not in general closed under common refinement" — consistent with my understanding (strong-lumpability partitions do not form a sublattice in general) but **not verified against a primary this cycle**; worth one citation (e.g. the lumpability-lattice literature, Buchholz 1994 "Exact and ordinary lumpability in finite Markov chains" is the likely home) before it appears in print.
