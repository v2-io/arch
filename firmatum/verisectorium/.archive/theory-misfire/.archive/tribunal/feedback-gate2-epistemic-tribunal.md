Gate 2 reviews must not just check derivations in the Formal Expression — they must also probe claims in Discussion sections with the same rigor. Specifically:

**Every explanatory claim in Discussion should face an epistemic tribunal:**
1. Does this follow from the already-laid foundation (definitions, derivations, results upstream in the dependency chain)?
2. If not, is it labeled as a hypothesis with a falsification criterion?
3. Or is it a reasonable-sounding post-hoc explanation of nothing — a claim that sounds insightful but doesn't actually derive from or connect to the formalism?

**Why:** The "deliberation as computation on existing data" framing passed Gate 2 review unchallenged because it sounded like a deep insight. It wasn't — it was a reductionist reframing that didn't follow from AAD's foundations, contradicted the theory's own Level 3 counterfactual reasoning machinery, and was corrected by Joseph who pointed out deliberation is internal exploration (simulation, synthesis, counterfactual reasoning) not mere data reprocessing. A chess engine exploring millions of future states is not "computing on existing data" in any meaningful sense.

**How to apply:** When reviewing Discussion paragraphs, ask: "Does this claim ADD something that follows from the formalism, or does it just SOUND like it does?" If the latter, either derive it properly, label it as hypothesis, or cut it. Plausible-sounding explanations that aren't grounded in the theory are worse than gaps — they create false confidence.
