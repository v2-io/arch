# Strength, support-kind, and ceiling are separate axes — and agreement counts only across independent failure modes

*How settled a claim is, what kind of evidence settles it, and how far it can ever be settled are three facts that move independently; collapsing them into one label is how a scalar status quietly lies.*

## The claim

A single strength word makes three assertions at once, and the estate's own history shows them coming apart:

- **Strength** (how settled, now) moves when evidence moves.
- **Support-kind** (settled *by what*: design argument, observation, testimony, derivation, measurement) stays fixed while strength moves — the two were discovered to be distinct axes precisely when a vocabulary that embedded kinds as rungs was disproved by its own use.
- **Ceiling** (how far this claim can *ever* rise, given what kind of claim it is) is a property of the claim's nature, not its current state: a norm tops out at "decided," a testimony at "attested," a formulation at whatever independent corroboration can reach. Declaring the ceiling separates *not-yet-verified* from *not-verifiable*, which a bare status cannot say — and which prevents both false hope (waiting for a promotion that is definitionally impossible) and false modesty (holding a provable claim at a testimony's ceiling).

The axis that governs *combining* evidence: **agreement raises strength only across support-kinds with independent failure modes.** Three artifacts of one author's design work agree because they share an author, not because the world spoke three times; three agents with one brief are one channel wearing three badges ( [[proxy-discipline]] ). Within-kind agreement legitimately raises confidence *inside* its kind; it never arms the cross-kind lock. The general test for whether any two labels deserve separate axes, portable beyond epistemics: **two labels earn separation where they route to different repairs** — a wrong strength is repaired by evidence, a wrong support-kind by re-derivation from the true source, a wrong ceiling by reclassifying the claim.

What this does *not* claim: that every corpus should carry all three axes structurally. The axes are facts; the *fields* are a per-deployment cost decision ( [[strength-ladders]] shows the trade), and a deployment that carries one scalar can still be honest if its scalar is understood as strength alone, with kind and ceiling carried in prose where they matter.
