# Comprehension and judgment must live in the same agent; the atom grain decides where the seam can fall

*Work divides cleanly along the record grain — but only for work whose product is a record. Work whose product is a judgment cannot be split from the reading that grounds it, whichever agent does both.*

## The claim

A corpus of small, independently-identified records is unusually easy to divide: two agents drafting two records touch two files, and the identity system means neither needs to know what the other named ([[write-safety]] and [[partition-isolation]] carry the mechanics). That cheapness is real, and it is why the pattern and parallel work fit together. It also conceals the one division that does not work.

**The invariant: comprehension and judgment are not separable.** An agent that reads and an agent that concludes cannot be different agents. Split it one way — a reader summarizing for a judge — and the judgments inherit the summary's compressions with no first-hand basis to defend any of them; the compression flattens precisely what is distinctive, which is what judgment needs. Split it the other way — a judge handing conclusions to a reader — and the reading becomes confirmation of a frame it cannot revise. *Which* agent holds both is entirely free, and parallel agents each holding both is a legitimate and valuable arrangement; what fails is the seam running between them.

This is why "read these and tell me what you found" is a different request from "find me the files that mention X." Discovery divides; judgment does not.

**Independent judgments are worth more than more judgments, and they are fragile.** Where several agents each run a whole pass, the value is decorrelated error, and it survives only if their inputs are independent — a shared brief, an orchestrator's framing, or one agent's output shown to the next re-correlates them at no visible cost ([[priming-discipline]] states the reading-order half). The practical consequence for a multi-pass arrangement: each agent runs the full protocol including the orientation it seems wasteful to repeat, because the repetition *is* the mechanism. Skipping it collapses the arrangement into one pass wearing several coats.

**A brief carries intent and constraint; it does not carry method.** The receiving agent has judgment as good as the delegator's and will meet material the delegator has not seen. What only the delegator holds is the why, the context outside the material, and genuine constraints — and those are what a brief owes. An enumeration of what to investigate is the one form that reliably backfires, because it forecloses exactly the meta-move an independent pass exists to make: *is the question the right one*. Where an enumeration is unavoidable, marking each item as checked-versus-guessed at least tells the agent where the unexplored volume is.

**Delegated work has a landing obligation, and it is the delegator's to verify.** Work that ends in a report has not entered the corpus. A brief should name the destination — *if this produces a durable result, it lands in a record with its own identity and an outline row* — and at review the destination is checked for existence, not the report for eloquence. Without this, substantive results accumulate in reports where the corpus's instruments cannot see them: not cross-referenced, not walked by the outline, not visible to the checker, and at risk whenever the report is archived. [[integration-metabolism]] is the general law; this is its delegation-shaped corollary, and it is the one place the law is routinely forgotten because the work *feels* finished when the report arrives.

**A standing contradiction expands the work rather than shrinking it.** The natural response to two sources disagreeing is to pick one and proceed; the disagreement is information that the cheap resolution destroys. Where parallel agents return incompatible readings, the incompatibility is the finding.

## Strength & grounds

**Heuristic, at mixed remove.** The comprehension/judgment invariant and the independent-full-pass protocol are stated and practised in `~/src/arch/asf/doc/sop/audit.sop/de-novo.sop.md` (read first-hand 2026-08-06) — as the delegation-of-comprehension anti-pattern (§3.1), and as the multi-pass rule that every sub-agent runs the whole protocol *"even if the orchestrating agent already has that context"* (§7.11), with its named failure mode of sub-agents skipping orientation to save time. The reconciliation of those two — that the seam, not the delegation, is what fails — is the annotator's reading of an apparent contradiction in that source rather than a statement the source makes in one place, which is itself the reason it is worth landing: an unstated reconciliation of two rules that look opposed is exactly what licenses routing around both.

The brief discipline is the estate's own delegation doctrine (`~/src/arch/AGENTIC-DELEGATION.md`, loaded into every session in this tree), which carries a long incident ledger and several dated counter-specimens where open framing produced findings that a prescriptive brief had ruled out. The landing obligation is stated for one high-value content kind (derivations left in spikes) in the estate's methodology notes; the generalization to any delegated result is this segment's.

Single estate throughout; no comparison exists between split and unsplit arrangements on the same material.
