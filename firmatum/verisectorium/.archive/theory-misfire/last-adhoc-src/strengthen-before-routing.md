# A finding that proposes a weakening is an investigation, and its failure is canon

*The cheapest disposition for "this claim overreaches" is to change the word — which makes the task disappear and the corpus strictly honest in one edit, and is why it is the disposition a corpus must make expensive; what the expensive path returns, when it fails, is usually worth more than what it returns when it succeeds.*

## The frame: routing is not execution

Intake material — audit findings, review comments, a spike's conclusions — arrives shaped like instructions and is not. The job is to decide, per item, what is valid *in the first place* and what is still valid *as of the corpus's current state*, and then to route it to where it belongs. Whether the corpus did what the item asked is a different question and often the wrong one.

The disposition vocabulary is where this becomes operational rather than attitudinal, and one state carries the whole point: **`correctly-rejected`** — a finding asking for a weakening, declined *because the claim was instead strengthened to deserve its original form*. That is a **closed** finding, not an open one, and no vocabulary that only ranges over *done / not done* can express it. A mature disposition set is a corpus's accumulated distinctions, each state minted by a specific occasion on which the coarser vocabulary would have recorded something false ([[routing-sop-anatomy]] carries the estate's thirteen, and the six-state spike set beside it).

## The claim

**(A) A proposed softening is gated on an attempt to make the claim true.** Scope-narrowing, status-downgrade, *"this is really heuristic," "it's just an epistemic typo"* — each triggers the strengthening attempt first, and **the finder's own framing of their finding carries no weight on the direction of discharge.** This matters because the framing is usually generous and usually says *this is not a weakening*, which is precisely the sentence that makes the easy path feel principled. The assertion was made for a reason; the mental picture that produced it was normally pointing at something real, and the question is whether that something can be reached.

Effort, time, and risk of getting stuck are **false constraints** here, and not merely irrelevant: they generate orderings inverted from value, because the expensive repairs are where the theory is. Do not rank by effort, do not propose smallest-first, and do not defer the substantive move to a discussion when the substantive move *is* the attempt.

**(B) The attempt has four outcomes, and only one of them is not a result.** It can reach the claim; it can overshoot it — a uniqueness result where a bound was asked, a boundary that is itself the stronger statement; it can return a **no-go**, a demonstration that the claim is false which, in falsifying it, characterizes the domain more sharply than success would have; or it can return *"we tried and failed"* with neither a no-go nor exhaustive effort. The fourth is **an alarm, not a landing** — it means re-attempt from scratch, ask why the structure yielded no insight, escalate. A corpus that lets it be a quiet outcome has re-created the word-swap with extra steps.

**(C) A no-go collapses two things at once, and missing the second is the error worth naming.** The claim is now false — that part is obvious. Less obvious: **the proposed downgrade is also false.** A softening presumes a weaker-but-true residue, and a no-go says there is none *in that form*. There is no downgrade option. The finding that opened the cycle has been answered by being dissolved, and treating the no-go as "so we soften after all" reinstates exactly the falsehood the investigation just refuted.

**(D) The landing is governed by one invariant: a record must never lie about its own status, even transiently.** Between discovering a no-go and repairing the claim there is an interval, and during it the corpus is read. So the record is marked false *first*, in the record, visibly — not in a tracker, not in a note — with a pointer to the investigation, before anyone works out what the repair is. And the falsified claim does not lie alone: every dependent must be found and either cascade-marked or re-derived, with that closure **verified** before the finding is dispositioned. An unmarked dependent of a falsified claim is the same lie, one hop away.

**(E) The no-go is canon, not a ghost to exile — and this is a correction to the replacement discipline, made by the estate against itself.** Read as a rule, *integration is replacement* says: the superseded claim disappears, present truth only, the history goes to the history layer, and the urge to write *"this is not a weakening"* is the tell that the ghost survives. Applied mechanically, that instruction becomes **find the ghost in the body and delete it, high confidence** — and it is wrong, because it over-generalizes the rare case into the default. *"We establish that X is impossible, because Y"* is a true present-tense statement about the domain. It belongs in the corpus, especially when the failed approach is one a competent reader would otherwise assume was simply missed. **Exiling it to archaeology is itself a loss of truth-content.**

What is actually demoted to the history layer is narrower and worth stating exactly: **the project-autobiography, and only where it is redundant with the canonical statement of the truth.** *"The framework previously held a false bound here"* is process history. *"No such bound exists; the natural route provably fails"* is the domain, and it is canon. When the domain statement is already stated cleanly elsewhere, the autobiographical restatement is redundant and goes to history; when it is *not*, the correct repair is to **write the domain statement as canon**, never to let the history layer be the only place the truth lives.

**(F) The same surface phrase is not the same disease.** Two records can both say *"this is not a weakening"* and need opposite repairs. One is a status case — there was a real transition, a proof failed, and the phrase is defending a scar. The other is a clarity case — no status ever changed, the sentence is a true present-truth scope statement, and the defensive register came from answering a reader's *"you say it but it's easy to miss."* Both repairs de-defensivize; only the first touches epistemology. Conflating them as *"same discipline, lower urgency"* is itself the failure, and the discriminator is **provenance**: check whether a status transition actually happened before deciding what kind of thing you are looking at.

**(G) Peer confidence about the outcome is unreliable in both directions, and should not be relayed.** A cluster adjudication once predicted, confidently, that a particular strengthening was *"standard textbook, should not fail."* The hard attempt disconfirmed it into a no-go. The prediction was then recorded **as disconfirmed**, in the investigation and in the record's notes, so that it would not be re-attempted on the strength of the optimism. The operative rule is short: run the attempt; do not relay the forecast. Optimism that a strengthening will succeed is exactly as untrustworthy as pessimism that it will fail, and only one of the two is normally treated with suspicion.

## Why this belongs to the pattern rather than to one theory

Nothing above depends on the material being mathematics. It depends on three properties this pattern already has: records that assert their own epistemic status, dependencies that are declared so a cascade is *findable*, and a history layer that can hold what the body may not. A corpus with those three can run this discipline; a corpus without declared dependencies cannot run (D) at all, because there is no way to close the cascade — which is a concrete answer to *what is the dependency declaration for*, beyond ordering checks ([[dependency-order-tension]]).

## Strength & grounds

**A discipline read first-hand and whole from one estate's process law, where it is the most-exercised process the estate has; transferred here by argument, with its outcome distribution unmeasured.**

Read from `~/src/arch/asf/doc/sop/` on 2026-08-06 ([[routing-sop-anatomy]]); the quotations, the four outcome states, the two-collapses argument, the invariant and the over-rotation correction are all stated there, in a document carrying five dated refinements and citing worked instances by record name. The over-rotation correction (E) is the strongest single item, because it is a correction the estate made **against a rule it had itself ratified**, with the mis-application caught on a named record and the corrected reading argued rather than asserted.

Held down deliberately in three places. The frequency ordering in (B) — that strengthening usually succeeds and no-gos are third — is the steward's observed prior, marked in the source as awaiting data, and it is **not** evidence; it is a recorded expectation. Nobody has counted actual outcomes by state, so *how often the expensive path pays* is genuinely unknown in this estate. Second, the discipline's own transfer beyond mathematical claims is argued above and not demonstrated: no non-mathematical corpus here has run a no-go landing. Third, (G) rests on one recorded prediction-disconfirmation pair, which is enough to justify not relaying forecasts and nowhere near enough to characterize how unreliable they are.

- Open and unhomed: what the analogue of a no-go is for a corpus whose claims are empirical rather than derived. A failed strengthening of an empirical claim yields a *measurement*, not a theorem, and whether that lands as canon by the same argument is unexamined.
