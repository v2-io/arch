# Orientation can be gated

*Where write access or high-stakes work requires real orientation to the claim surface, free-read of the outline (and a cheap check) beats an honor-system “I read the docs.”*

## The claim

Agentic turnover is total between sessions and after compaction: the next mind (or the same mind after a context break) does not inherit free-read. Asking that mind to “be oriented” without a check reproduces the outline-skipping failure: segments get opened as task fodder, the outline’s order and gaps never load, and work proceeds on a partial map.

An **orientation gate** is a membrane on a destination (e.g. commit access to the claim tree) that requires:

1. **Free-read of the outline** as front door — not optional “docs.”
2. A **cheap, checkable** proof of contact with load-bearing material (quiz units addressed by identity: slug + section + span words — not path line numbers).
3. A **pass scoped to a context lifetime** (session, compaction generation) so the feeling of orientation cannot permanently unlock the corpus.

The specimen that this is not theoretical — that skipping is common enough to justify engineering — is [[outline-skipping-failure]] (vivarium’s live gate; asf’s audit and orientation SOPs).

**Optional, not universal.** Like other enforcement choices, whether to gate depends on stakes and reversibility. A low-stakes sidecar may not need it; a claim corpus that is the sole source of truth for code often does. The form is the *option and its structure*, not a mandate for every instance.

## Strength & grounds

**Heuristic.** Motivated by the turnover problem ([[turnover-solution]]) plus the multi-instance recognition of outline-skipping in [[outline-skipping-failure]]. vivarium’s implementation is the worked example; asf’s SOPs are the soft version (norms without chmod). Not a controlled trial of gate vs no-gate quality outcomes.
