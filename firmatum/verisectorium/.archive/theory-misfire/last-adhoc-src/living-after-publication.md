# Publication is a projection, not an exit

*Submitting or publishing freezes a rendering; the segments underneath stay alive — revisions, reviewer adjudications, and backports flow through them afterward, and a corpus that treats submission as the end has no place for that flow to land.*

## The claim

The intuition to unlearn: that a paper's source dies at submission. What is actually frozen at the gate is a **rendering** — one view, one manifest, one moment, rightly checkpointed and immutable ( [[lifecycle-events]] ). The atoms it was assembled from remain the living surface, because the work that arrives *after* publication is segment-shaped work: reviewer claims must be adjudicated against specific source claims (not against a PDF); revisions are re-renderings from amended segments through the same manifest; and corrections discovered during review are owed **backport** upstream — to the framework corpora the paper drew from — which is only possible while the source stays alive and its provenance to upstream slugs intact.

The estate's live demonstration: the review-response packages that grew their own claim/residue/discussion trees per paper, adjudicating each reviewer point at claim grain with verification rungs — stewarded papers turned out to *need* a verisectorium after submission, which is the strongest available evidence that publication was never an exit. The consequence for design: the submission gate is real (irreversibility earns it — [[verification-provenance]]'s anonymization case), but it gates a *rendering crossing*, and the corpus's obligation list at that crossing includes: checkpoint the manifest, keep the segment↔rendering correspondence recoverable, and open the inbound lane for what review sends back.

## Strength & grounds

**Heuristic, one lived instance plus structural argument.** The adjudicated-package practice is live (three papers' trees, one substantially exercised, read first-hand 2026-08-05); the backport lane it promises is honestly recorded as not yet crossed by anything ( [[pipeline-seams]] ). The structural half — that post-publication work is segment-shaped — follows from what reviewer adjudication *is*, and holds independent of the single instance; how much of the full loop pays for itself is exactly what the first completed revision cycle will measure.
