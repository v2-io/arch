# Feedback from founding template/TERM.term.un + its first instantiation (paths, 26 entries)

From the paths-session fork that founded `template/TERM.term.un` from INFLUX/terminology-survey.md and instantiated all 26 paths terms the same hour. Three findings the template design should absorb or answer:

1. **A partition wants a parent.** The four canonicity promises (true-identity / canonical-location / preferred-location / working-path) partition a concept ("promise-class") that is not minted as a term — so the `|set-rel :kind partitions` edge has nowhere to hang and the disjointness had to be stated pairwise on each child. Rule candidate: when instantiation produces a partition with no parent entry, that is a signal to mint the parent, not to omit the edge.
2. **`part-of` is load-bearing and is not a set relation.** Operational/epistemic bounds are components of expected-cardinality, not subsets of it; conflating part-of with subset-of would corrupt the Euler substrate. The template seeds `part-of` under `|rel` (mereology stays out of `|set-rel`); worth stating as a rule since the confusion is classical.
3. **Process/mechanism concepts take few or no set-rels** (progressive-routing, over-determination): they are not sets of things. The set-rel machinery being optional {0,*} is correct; a template note warning against forcing set edges onto mechanism terms would prevent decoration.

Also: udon's attribute-phase law (CORE §6.9) forced the template's field families to be grouped attributes-first rather than topically — the first draft violated the phase and was rewritten; a template linting pass (or the eventual schema) should check instantiations for this, since it is the likeliest structural lapse (O19's prediction).

## Addendum (same day, evening): two harder lessons from steward review

5. **The template was re-cut wholesale — read the shape of the failure.** The steward's verdict on the founding template: "the 'survey lexicon fields and get the best representation possible' failed rather miserably" — against a ~20-line 2022 YAML sketch (lexicon-middleman's episode.yml) that passes a five-second glance test the surveyed template flunked. Diagnosis for the kit: the survey optimized *field coverage* (best construct per field, across standards); the target was *reading experience* (best entry, whole, read by the steward who combs it). Best-per-field ≠ best. The replacement spine: say-this-not-that flat at top (word/alternates/synonyms/avoid), one-sentence definition with inline term links, and **invariants as plain-English bullets that ARE the relations** — prose carrying structure via a small controlled-phrasing set the tools parse. 90% payload, 10% apparatus. Demand-side artifacts written by their own users beat supply-side surveys; when designing an entry format, prototype the *reading*, not the field inventory.
6. **The kit shipped a citation convention ASF had already outgrown.** The founding ORIENT's "cite decisions by ID" produced opaque D-number tokens all over canon ("No idea what they are referencing into or mean" — steward). Corrected to slugged links (`[[DECISIONS#d11-universal-path|D11 universal-path]]`) with slug-stable ledger anchors. Kit rule candidate: identifiers that will be *read* carry their referent's name; bare ordinals are for machines only.
