# Two survey-method defects, caught by the steward at the taxonomy fill

*Participant feedback (coord, 2026-08-10). Occasion: the first taxonomy fill excluded Exposition & Pedagogy — the family that a five-minute count later showed to be the largest in the estate's main corpora ("I don't know how it could have been missed, TBH" — steward). The survey had in fact carried it; the loss was downstream. Two distinct defects, each with a praxis.*

1. **The survey must never bound the generative space — its only job on principled work is checking the output for completeness.** The first framing of this defect ("the candidate law filtered evidence") was itself shallow; the steward's correction reaches the root: *"There was never a step that said 'OK, so what* should *be the instrument of fix / related failure mode of an introduction? A discussion? A learning aid?' They all have excellent answers. You assumed that because the survey found that the existing corpus hadn't answered them meant that it wasn't a category — that's a very different thing — filtering what we'll ponder and make principled, vs. how we know our pondering and generative output is complete or sufficient yet."* The wild corpora's silence on exposition's adjudication instrument was a **bare gap** — a to-do for design, never a verdict about the category ([[claim-absence-vs-conflict]], violated here by its own corpus). Praxis, directional: **design → survey** is legitimate (does our generative output cover what exists?); **survey → design** is the sin (does what exists license what we may ponder?). Want-mode means every un-answered "what should adjudicate X?" gets *asked and answered from first principles* before any structural conclusion is drawn from its being un-answered in the wild.

2. **A kind survey without a count census is an inventory wearing a distribution's name.** Four dives produced kind-lists at specimen grain; nothing counted populations, so a majority family carried the same weight as a three-specimen curiosity. The verifying census cost minutes (`rg -o '^type: …' | sort | uniq -c` per corpus) and instantly showed `discussion` as asf vol-1's most common type (40/~155) and exposition kinds ≈ 30% of vivarium. Praxis: any survey that will feed prioritization or taxonomy includes counts per kind per corpus from the start — lists answer "what exists," only counts answer "what dominates," and the two questions get conflated exactly when a synthesis titles itself "distribution."
