# Claim: The Naming Criteria Travel

The evaluation criteria refined in the estate's naming cycles — communal imagination, standalone citability with its four repair moves, scope honesty, the renamed-from-now test, and their companions — are portable discipline for any verisectorium's vocabulary, because they evaluate names against reader cognition rather than against any particular domain.

## Formal Expression

*[Claim (naming-criteria, heuristic)]*

For any name under consideration in any instance, the following axes carry the evaluation. None is scored mechanically; together they are the checklist a non-obvious naming decision's reasoning trail should touch.

1. **Self-descriptive vs baggage-carrying.** A name can describe its referent from scratch or adopt an adjacent field's baggage; both can be right, and the worst outcome is a name whose *only* content is baggage while the referent differs subtly from it.
2. **Familiarity gradient.** Zero seconds of unfamiliarity for a trained reader is usually good but dangerous when the usage differs (false confidence); high unfamiliarity is usually bad but right when novelty should be signaled.
3. **Memorable-noun potential.** Does the name render as a *thing* nameable in discussion without paraphrase? The asymmetry compounds across every conversation the community will have.
4. **Overload risk.** Collisions with other uses inside the instance or in adjacent vocabularies.
5. **Scope honesty.** The name must not promise more generality, exactness, or novelty than the concept delivers — with the meta-name special case: for names reaching across many atoms, scope honesty *dominates* memorability, because an over-narrow memorable name forces prose qualifications everywhere it fails to cover.
6. **Aging potential.** Too-cute ages into embarrassment; too-clinical never attracts use.
7. **Communal-imagination test.** Could a skilled reader, six months after first encounter, refer to the concept in conversation without looking it up? Compounding if yes; costing if no.
8. **Renamed-from-now-sounds-weird test.** Imagine the proposed name installed and routine: does the *old* name now sound quaint (strong rename), or does the *new* one sound forced (weak rename)?
9. **Standalone citability.** Extracted from instance context, does the bare name still point unambiguously back at this concept? Paired vocabulary is a legitimate route (two individually-generic words whose *pair* travels). When a name fails, four repair moves: **(a)** promote a structural qualifier the atom already implies into the citation handle; **(b)** pivot to a sharper sibling concept the atom already carries; **(c)** keep the short form as in-segment shorthand and commit a longer citation handle; **(d)** accept a deliberately-adopted standard term and discipline first-encounter citation of its source instead — legitimate only for genuine structural-identity adoption, not naming laziness.

10. **Coinage-necessity test.** *A coinage where an ancient word exists is a smell; a coinage where there is established academic vocabulary to adopt is a smell.* (Steward, 2026-08-09.) Before coining, ask whether humans have been talking about this thing for centuries — or whether a field already has it named — and whether the real work is adopting that vocabulary *correctly* rather than inventing around it. The specimen: several passes at a udon-addressing lexicon produced coinages like "containment coordinates" before the steward pointed out that paths, addresses, and places have millennia of vocabulary; adopting it yielded better theory and definitions, not just better names. The test complements axis 1's baggage caution rather than reversing it — adopted vocabulary still owes the repair moves under axis 9(d) when its established sense and the referent diverge.

Two companion disciplines travel with the axes: the **decision categories** (rename / keep / canonicalize / add-alias / name-unnamed-thing — with keep as an explicit commitment distinct from no-opinion, canonicalize distinguished by *excavated* provenance where the prose already converged on the phrase, and name-unnamed-thing as the highest-value category because the slot is empty); and the **acronym discipline** (coin only when the expansion will be used often, the acronym survives communal imagination unexpanded, and adjacent literatures are collision-checked — the estate's own framework rename was forced by exactly such a collision).

## Epistemic Status

Heuristic, and `heuristic` is the ceiling: the criteria are accumulated judgment from one estate's naming cycles (a role-prefix pilot, a framework-scale collision rename, multi-round voting cycles, a meta-segment scope-honesty lesson), not derivations, and their source discipline states the honest ground truth this segment inherits: *naming is irreducibly aesthetic — no derivation settles it; there is only the accumulated judgment of many readers.* What is claimed beyond the source is the *portability*: the criteria evaluate names against reader cognition (memory pressure, first-encounter familiarity, extraction from context), which is domain-invariant, so they should transfer to any atom kind. That transfer claim is single-estate coherence so far — the criteria and every instance applying them share one steward — and its honest test is an instance whose domain and authorship are genuinely foreign.

## Discussion

**Why these criteria and not a committee's.** Each axis earned its place by a failure it names: the decoder-ring subscripts that motivated add-alias; the meta-segment whose memorable-but-narrow name failed one of its own four instances; the collision that forced a framework rename after external literature was checked too late. A future instance adopting the criteria inherits the scars without paying for them again — which is the sense in which discipline, not taste, is what travels.

**Live worked examples in this corpus, applying the criteria to itself.** *Verisectorium* scores high on standalone citability (a unique coinage; the Googleability test returns nothing else) with communal imagination untested outside the estate. *Instrumenta* has a live overload-risk finding: it collides with an autopax component-family name, and the pending Organ II check will choose among repair moves (a) qualifier, (b) sibling pivot, or accepting scoped coexistence. The `claim-` form-prefix carries a recorded steward reservation — a renamed-from-now test deliberately left running rather than called early. And the pending term-marking convention (`[[term/x|x]]`-style) will decide how a first definition is *lexically visible* at all, which is upstream of several axes (a term the reader cannot recognize as defined cannot be evaluated for familiarity or citability).

**Relation to the stores.** The criteria are the *evaluation* half of Organ II; [[form-terminology-store]] is the *record* half (per-term entries, append-only decisions, generated lexicon views) where a non-obvious decision's reasoning trail against these axes durably lands. [[post-names-are-interface]] supplies the why: every axis above is a per-encounter interface property.
