## Hypotheses I'd stake, marked

- **H1 — Fidelity is two things wearing one name, and 02's open question dissolves if split.** Fidelity-as-*substrate* ("every ladder's warrant rests on the trail") is really **chain-integrity — a gate**, not a priced dimension: binary, validity-flavored, Standing's sibling. Fidelity-as-*dimension* (witness position × credibility) belongs only to account-kind records. The namespace collision between the two is why "whether TRAIL and the Fidelity dimension are one thing seen twice" felt unresolvable — they're *not* one thing, and the substrate role was borrowing the dimension's name. Cheap to test: restate 03's "Fidelity is the substrate" paragraph as "trail integrity is a gate" and see if anything is lost.
- **H2 — the performer-trust accumulator is where your namespace prediction lands.** The two-column law (what-the-act-showed ⊥ how-trusted-the-performer) is in Q5, but the model carries performer-trust structurally *nowhere* except testimony credibility. Your forward-note — "namespace ends up being a strong indication of a desire for an accumulator — evidences, saliency-factors, **trust (probably)**" — I think named exactly the missing object: a store-scope `trust/` accumulator over actors and channels, *fed by reproduction outcomes* (every re-run that confirms or convicts an actor's recorded events is a trust event). That would make the second column computed like everything else, and it's the one place the model currently has a column with no trail behind it.
- **H3 — Disposition may not be an epistemic dimension at all.** Your placement-for-influence question is about the *collection's* health (is the system using this record well?), not the record's warrant (is this record sound?). It may belong to the process dual — physiology, not veritas — with the salience protocol as the discriminating experiment either way. I adopted your hypothesis in the table and I'd defend having done so; I also notice I can't currently say what a Disposition *ladder* certifies to a consumer, and that inarticulacy is usually a category smell.
- **H4 — the idiom hypothesis, from tonight's three collapses.** Agent-built epistemics fails in a characteristic direction: we model in state-machine idiom (locks, gates, arms, ladders) because that's our native construction language, while the underlying truths are probabilistic and are *definable* rather than approximable in that idiom. Kit-level consequence if it holds: a founding declaration should state each mechanism in the most fundamental idiom available and name its approximations as approximations — and the steward-probe "what do you actually mean by X?" should be a standing acceptance test, because it detects idiom-artifacts nothing agent-side can.

## The question that nags me most

**Grain, and the projection rule across it.** Per-clause status is live practice; edges want clause-grain `supports`; the object model waves at a "grain parameter" — and nothing anywhere in RC1 says how a record's headline composes from its parts (min over clauses? declared per record? per kind?). This is the *exact defect the research diagnosed in asf's status field* — no declared projection rule, opposite choices live in the wild — inherited one level down, named in a working note, never answered. It's the model's most concrete unfinished business because a de-novo auditor hits it in the first hour: "this record is `exact (sub-scope); robust-qualitative generally` — which cell do I audit?" I didn't answer it because I don't know the answer; my lean is *declared-per-kind with min-over-necessary-clauses as the default*, but that's a guess wearing a lean's clothes.

## Wanderings

- The reopen brief the decision machinery emits ("verify my model of WHY it was decided, then decide together") is your burst-3 prescription verbatim, and watching it fire mechanically in the run was the moment the decision specimen stopped feeling like an exercise. Somewhere in that shape — machinery whose output is an invitation to a conversation rather than a verdict — is the difference between this system and every workflow engine it superficially resembles. The trail doesn't decide; it *convenes*.
- All our specimens are success stories. The best teaching specimen we don't have is a **failure**: an unwarranted lift attempted and caught by re-run, a fabricated event convicted, a post-hoc criterion exposed by its own commitment field. The conviction machinery teaches best when something is convicted guilty.
- Relata keeps appearing at the end of every road (the shipped WoE ledger, the verification dirs, the maturity ramp's destination). Sooner or later the honest question is not "does RC1 describe relata" but "does relata *become* an RC1 deployment, or RC1's Evidence machinery become relata" — a unification parked with F1, but I suspect it's where the first production-grade trail will actually come from.
- Era and Freshness keep almost-merging in my head: an era-key is a versioned referent, and referent-currency already means "reset when the referent changes." I couldn't collapse them cleanly tonight (validity-conditions vs narration feel genuinely different) and stopped per the no-machinery-without-a-consumer rule — but if a future mind finds one object under both, I won't be surprised.
- The stranger reads worked so well that I keep wondering what the *third* independent family is for documents like this. Reality-contact was the nets and vsl; strangers were the reads; the one we've never used on a model (rather than a claim) is **time** — the same unprimed read six months from now, after the vocabulary has either taken root in practice or quietly died. Freshness applied to persuasiveness itself.
