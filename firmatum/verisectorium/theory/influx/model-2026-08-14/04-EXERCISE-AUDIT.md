## A note on the criterion itself (feedback, as invited)

The binary needed a third bin. Several citations attach to elements whose content was **carried whole from internal sources** (02-GROUNDING says so explicitly for §0.1 and §0.3) — an external cite there *cannot* be an instrument, by construction, and calling it ornament misses its actual job: **independent-lineage corroboration**. Deleting it leaves the element's content identical but changes its *warrant* — "three unrelated lineages" is itself a load-bearing claim (§0's whole argument for commitment status). So the verdicts below use three bins:

- **Instrument** — the element's content traces to it; deleting the cite orphans a decision.
- **Corroboration** — content unchanged if deleted, but the cite carries independence-weight the model's argument uses. Keep, with grade stated.
- **Ornament** — neither: cut to the provenance trail.

Second observation: **exercised-ness is a property of the relationship, not the source, and it is improvable after the fact.** Several ornaments below became instruments *during this audit* at a cost of minutes. So an ornament verdict is a cut-or-exercise fork, not just a cut.

## Detailed findings

### D1. Austin is the best-exercised citation in the model
Q0's closing clause — "Misfire ≠ wrong-content" — *is* Austin's misfire/abuse split, applied: a record invalid-at-origin (misfire) vs a validly-created record that is defective (abuse). The distinction did work: it is why Q0 is "a gate, never a rung." One caution from the sweep: the fuller felicity-conditions structure (preparatory/sincerity/essential) is **recalled, unverified** there; Q0 currently leans only on the verified misfire/abuse half, which is the right amount. If Q0 ever grows, the felicity follow-up fetch the sweep requested becomes due first.

### D2. Hohfeld's unused half
The sweep's sharpest Hohfeld yield — a Decision that *exercises* existing authority vs a Decision that *creates or alters* authority (first-order vs second-order) — appears nowhere in the model. Backing's ladder treats all decisions as one kind. This may be fine (second-order decisions might just be Backing-priced records whose *subject* is standing), but the model never says so. Cheap to resolve: one sentence in the Backing row or Q0. Flagged, not exercised to conclusion here.

### D3. Admiralty: "by construction" overclaims what the split buys
Q5 says fusing the two columns "is the halo-effect failure **by construction**" — implying the split prevents it structurally. Admiralty's own field documents the opposite: the Dezert et al. critique (recalled in both sweeps, unfetched) reports analysts letting source-reliability leak into credibility judgments *despite* the two-axis design; ICD-203's don't-combine-in-one-sentence rule exists precisely because the split needs behavioral enforcement (and that rule is itself the survey's weakest-verified system). Exercising the prior art here says: **two columns are necessary, not sufficient — the discipline sits at the *conscious* rung of §5's own inculcation ladder until a mechanism enforces it.** The model's own §5 predicts the failure its Q5 phrasing waves away. Suggested fix: one clause ("two columns, never one grade — and the split held mechanically, not by discipline; its home field documents the leak").

### D4. VIM: a cheap sharpening available for §4.3
NOTE 5 is genuinely exercised (chain ≠ fitness is its content, correctly drawn, primary-verified — the sweep called it "the most quietly important find"). The unexercised neighbor: the same standards family's **verification vs validation** distinction — verification = the item meets its *specified* requirements; validation = the requirements are *adequate for the intended use* — is standard vocabulary for exactly §4.3's two-certification split. *(Recall-grade from me; the VIM HTML mirror fetched cleanly for the sweep, so checking §2.44/2.45 wording is a one-fetch errand.)* Adopting the pair would let §4 say: a certification is a *verification*; fitness-for-purpose is a *validation*; they never share a cell — inheriting sixty years of the distinction instead of coining it.

### D5. GRADE: the unexercised half corroborates the model's most exposed synthesis
02-GROUNDING names the currency spine (§1) "the model's main proposal and its main exposure," with *no external warrant column entry of its own*. But GRADE, exercised one step past where the model stopped, supplies one: GRADE formally separates **certainty of evidence** from **strength of recommendation** (the Evidence-to-Decision framework — a recommendation can be *strong on moderate evidence* or *conditional on high evidence*, because values, resources, and feasibility enter the second judgment and not the first). That is the Evidence-currency / Backing-currency split, independently evolved in the model's best-verified external system: assertions and decisions denominated differently, moved by different events, deliberately not reducible. *(Recall-grade from me — but the GRADE Handbook fetched cleanly for the 2026-08-10 annotation pass, so verifying the EtD structure is cheap.)* This is the audit's Fidelity-catch analog in the strengthening direction: the citation was already in the room; running its logic one step further turns the spine's "main exposure" into a partially externally-corroborated claim.
