# An undisposed flag bills every agent who finds it

*A defect noticed and left open does not sit quietly. Each fresh agent re-notices it, re-investigates it, and re-surfaces it — so a flag needs a disposition state, or the discipline that finds problems becomes a recurring tax.*

## The claim

Marking something open — *unverified*, *needs review*, *authority questionable* — feels like a one-time cost: someone noticed, someone wrote it down, the note waits for disposal. Under total agent turnover it is not one-time. Every agent arriving fresh at that record does the noticing again, at full price: reads the surrounding context, works out why it looks wrong, forms an ask, and surfaces it. The steward answers a question they have answered before, or the agent burns the investigation and re-raises it, or — worst — it decides silently this time and the discipline that surfaced the defect quietly stops working.

The observed instance: a questionable authority tag in vivarium's decision ledger "has clearly come up a few times by various agents already," each occurrence a fresh de-novo investigation, with nothing in the record showing it had ever been raised ([[authority-flag-specimen]]).

**What the flag needs is a disposition, carried with it.** An open flag should say which of these it is:

- **answered** — surfaced, adjudicated, and the answer is here (so re-noticing jumps straight to the answer);
- **deferred, with a reason** — surfaced, deliberately not resolved, and here is why and what it is waiting on;
- **awaiting X** — surfaced, and the thing that would settle it is named.

And it should carry its **prior surfacings**, so the next agent inherits the trail rather than re-deriving the question. The general shape: re-encountering an open flag should jump the agent to the pending decision and its accumulated evidence — never re-run the noticing.

Note the interaction that makes this urgent rather than tidy: the more eagerly a class of agent maintains a corpus, the *more* expensive undisposed flags become. Diligence and repeat-billing scale together. A corpus whose agents ignore its flags does not have this problem; a corpus whose agents care does.

## What this is not

It is not a promotion ladder for flags, and not a demand that every flag be closed. **Deferred-with-reason is a full disposition** — the point is that the *state of having been considered* is recorded, not that consideration produced resolution. Nor is it a claim that re-examination is always waste: a flag whose evidence has changed deserves a fresh look, which is exactly what a recorded prior surfacing plus a revisit condition makes cheap to judge ([[decision-records]]).

## Strength & grounds

**Formulation from one reported instance plus a straightforward turnover argument.** The mechanism is close to definitional under 100%-turnover conditions ([[turnover-solution]]): with no continuity of attention, anything not written is re-derived, and a flag is a written invitation to re-derive. The specific instance is testimonial and n=1 ([[authority-flag-specimen]]) — reported by a participant, not counted. **Nobody has measured a repeat-billing rate**, and that measurement is cheap and unclaimed: count distinct sessions raising the same open item in any corpus with a flag store.
