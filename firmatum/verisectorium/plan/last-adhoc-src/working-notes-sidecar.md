# The present-truth body needs somewhere for everything else to go

*A corpus that requires each claim to state present truth only has, by that same requirement, created a class of material that is real, worth keeping, and homeless — and the per-atom sidecar is the position that makes the requirement affordable rather than lossy.*

## The claim

Present-truth discipline is a strong demand. It says a claim's body carries what is true now, at honest strength, with the superseded expression replaced rather than accumulated ([[integration-metabolism]]). Taken alone that demand is unlivable, because working on a claim generates three kinds of material that are neither present truth nor disposable:

- the **open question** the current statement does not answer,
- the **route already tried and disconfirmed**, which the next capable agent will otherwise think of again and re-attempt,
- the **thing this claim is waiting on** — a measurement, a decision, another claim.

None of these belongs in the body: each would weaken or clutter a statement whose job is to be readable as what is true. None of them is safely deleted either: the second in particular is expensive to lose, because losing it costs the *rediscovery*, and turnover guarantees the rediscovery will be attempted. So the body's discipline forces a second position, attached to the atom, whose declared status is **not part of the claim**.

That is what the sidecar is, and it is why the position keeps appearing. It is not an accessory to the atom; it is the pressure release that lets replacement be practiced at all. A corpus without one either softens its bodies (keeping the old claim beside the new with a pointer, which is the failure [[integration-metabolism]] names) or loses its dead-ends silently.

**The "not canon" status is a licence with a direction, and getting the direction wrong is the common failure.** Because a sidecar is exempt from the claim's honesty apparatus, it reads as a place where anything may go — and the thing that flows in is *narration*: this used to say X, the audit recommended Y, the cycle that produced this. The estate's sharpest statement of the rule fixes the direction in one line: **not-canon licenses forward-work content, not backward-narration.** Past-work narration is the history layer's job ([[history-layer]]), and the pull toward writing it into the sidecar is strongest exactly when the change was a *deletion*, because then there is no artifact left to point at and the note feels like the only surviving witness. It is not; the history layer is.

**Three consequences follow from the position rather than from the content.**

*It attaches to the atom, not to the corpus.* The residue is about this claim; it has to travel when the claim is reordered, cited from elsewhere, or moved between views. A global notes file loses the association the moment either side moves, which is [[identities-over-locations]] at sidecar grain.

*It has to be separable by machine, not just by eye.* Every projection of the corpus that is not for the working author strips it — the published rendering, the reader's path, the assembled intermediate. That makes the sidecar a projection boundary, and projection boundaries want a declared marker rather than a name-matched heading ([[selection-and-projection]]).

*It runs on its own clock.* The body changes when the claim changes; the sidecar changes whenever anyone works on it, which is far more often. That difference in write rate — not any difference in canonicity — is what makes the sidecar a separate part rather than a separate file, and it is the same argument that makes events separate from both ([[atom-as-cluster]]).

**The naming divergence is a real cost, not a cosmetic one.** The position is reached for near-universally and named at least three different ways across four corpora, with six spellings inside one of them ([[sidecar-ubiquity-census]]). Any instrument that projects the sidecar out by matching its heading will silently pass the spellings it does not know — and passing silently, on a stripping operation, means shipping working notes into a published artifact.

## Strength & grounds

**A formulation whose forcing argument is analytic and whose ubiquity claim is measured but confounded.** The forcing argument — present-truth bodies plus non-disposable working material implies a third position — stands on its own and does not need the counts. The counts ([[sidecar-ubiquity-census]]: 164/170, 115/115, 12/12, plus a fourth corpus using different names) establish that the position is reached for reliably in *this* estate and cannot distinguish convergence from inheritance, since these corpora share authorship and at least one ports the other's conventions by reference. So read the census as evidence that the position is stable and cheap, not as evidence that independent designers arrive at it.

What is genuinely unestablished: that the *direction* rule (forward, not backward) is followed where it is written. One corpus states it authoritatively and the same corpus's sidecars are 27.3% of its prose with 57.9% of that from one abandoned process ([[working-notes-deluge]]) — which is a volume finding, not a direction finding. Nobody has classified a mature sidecar store by note kind, and that single measurement would price most of what this segment and [[working-note-lifecycle]] both argue.

## Working Notes

- The owed measurement, stated once for both segments: sample a mature store and classify each note as forward-pointer / regression-guard / dead-end / narration. Forward-heavy means the volume is inventory; narration-heavy means the direction rule is decorative.
- Open, and a genuine design question rather than a gap: whether the sidecar should be one position or several. The estate's rule admits three kinds of note into one section; a corpus that separated *waiting-on* from *tried-and-failed* would get a countable blocked-set for free, and would pay in ceremony.
- Not carried here: what earns a note and how notes leave — that is [[working-note-lifecycle]], which stands on this segment.
- Boundary worth holding: material waiting on a *person* is not sidecar content even though it has nowhere else obvious to go; it belongs on a decision surface ([[decision-records]], [[tracking-altitudes]]). A sidecar that absorbs it becomes an unowned queue.
