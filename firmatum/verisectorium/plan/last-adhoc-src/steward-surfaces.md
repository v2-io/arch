# The steward reads views, not records — and that is the design working

*A corpus grows past what its steward can hold within weeks; the surfaces built for him are the ones that let him govern material he has not read, without pretending he has read it.*

## The claim

The steward of a living corpus is the one participant with continuity across every session and the one who cannot scale. Within a short time, the fraction of the corpus he has personally read is small and falling — not through neglect but arithmetically, because agents write faster than any person reads. The pattern's response is to build surfaces for governing-without-reading, and to be explicit that this is what they are for.

**Altitude, declared per surface.** The estate's shape is a ladder: a navigator naming *areas* of work with priority; a tactical layer naming *items* within those areas; a portfolio of structural moves that cut across areas; a history layer split between a forward-going changelog and frozen archaeology; generated public surfaces; and a short decision queue holding pointers only ([[tracking-altitudes]] owns the ladder itself). What makes it usable to a steward is that each surface states its altitude, so that the question *where does this belong* has an answer that is not "the nearest file."

**Audience is declared per file, and the declaration is load-bearing.** Every surface says who may read it and when — most sharply, whether it is safe for a reader whose job is to judge the corpus, or whether it carries verdicts and therefore primes ([[priming-discipline]] is the norm; this is its manifestation on the steward's own shelf). The consequence is that the navigator has to be *written* auditor-safe, which is a real constraint on the surface the steward reads most.

**One-sitting decidability is the unit.** A steward's attention arrives in blocks, not streams. The surface that works is a set of items each carrying its own context, its options, the lead's recommendation, and a pointer to where the work lives — decidable from the entry alone, so a queue can be cleared without re-entering each subsystem. The surface that fails is one that duplicates the work, because the duplicate must be maintained and rots first ([[authority-routing]] carries the admission test for what earns a place in that queue at all).

**The steward's own voice is a distinct register and must be marked as such.** When a steward records priorities, an ordering, or a working list, that is *his view laid on the table* — not canon, not an instruction the corpus has adopted. Rewriting it into canonical voice is a specific and repeated failure: shared thinking silently becomes law, and the next session inherits it as settled. Two shapes handle it honestly. Mark the register explicitly (*this is his ordering, offered, not issued*). Or keep the primary verbatim and append a dated reconciliation beside it, naming which parts are now stale and why — which preserves both what he actually said and what is currently true, without either overwriting the other.

**What this costs, said plainly.** These surfaces are proxies. A navigator tells you where work is, not whether a claim holds; a status column locates a record's condition, it does not establish it. The whole apparatus is legitimate exactly insofar as it never becomes warrant — the discipline this corpus practises and has now legislated as [[warrant-over-authority]]'s companion. A steward governing by view is relying on the corpus's instruments and its agents' honesty; the surfaces make that reliance visible rather than removing it.

## Strength & grounds

**Heuristic, from live surfaces read first-hand 2026-08-06.** The altitude ladder, the per-file audience declarations (including which surfaces are marked safe for a de-novo reader and which are marked as carrying priming content), and the pointers-only decision queue are all running in `~/src/arch/asf` — the map is stated in `doc/sop/agents.sop.md` §File Organization, the queue is `JOSEPH-TODO.md`, and its one-sitting brief set is `msc/decision-briefs-2026-07-15.md`. The two honest-register shapes are **inherited at a stated remove**: the marked-as-his-view form from the comproprium README's priorities section, which also records the defect it is correcting (an earlier version presented his ordering *as* the ordering); the verbatim-plus-reconciliation form from a neurips processing-flow document that reproduces the steward's checklist as typed and appends a dated agent reconciliation naming which items are stale. Neither was re-read here.

One estate, one steward. The claim that the read fraction falls arithmetically is a mechanism, not a measurement — **nobody has measured what fraction of any corpus here its steward has read**, which would be a cheap and genuinely informative number.
