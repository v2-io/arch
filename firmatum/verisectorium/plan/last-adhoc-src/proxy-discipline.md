# Proxies locate; they do not settle

*Every artifact that is not itself a warranted claim — an index row, a register entry, a status label, an agent's report, multi-agent agreement — may be used to find work and cheap-screen it, and may never be cited as what settles a claim.*

## The norm

A corpus accumulates artifacts *about* its truths: outline rows, TODO entries, status fields, provenance banners, verifier reports, "the spike concluded," "checked against file F." All of these are **proxies** — cheaper than the truth they point at, and legitimately used for two things: **locating** (where is the question, who touched it, what feeds it) and **cheap-screening** (is this plausibly worth a real look). The line they may not cross: serving as the *warrant* for a present-truth statement or a disposition. A claim is settled by constitutive structure, re-derivation, or measurement held on the page — or honestly held at a lower strength with the raising action named.

Two traps recur enough to carry names:

- **Index-as-authority.** "The outline says X" is evidence about a navigator document, not about X. The degenerate form is trusting a corpus's *shape* — a full-looking outline, an empty-looking queue — as if shape were state.
- **Verified-against-artifact.** Checking that an artifact asserts P verifies the artifact, not P. The costliest local instance: a register entry *describing* information was treated as that information being preserved, and files were dispositioned as integrated on the register's authority — the delete-test failed the whole batch ( [[integration-metabolism]] carries the incident).

Multi-agent agreement is the subtlest proxy: several capable agents concurring feels like corroboration and is often one channel wearing several badges — shared brief, shared sources, shared training priors. Agreement locates a good candidate; independent failure modes settle it ( [[epistemic-axes]] territory).

Decided for this corpus; recommended for the pattern. Not truth-apt; overturn expressly — and do not soften it into discussion to avoid defending it.
