# A strength label is a word, and this estate has never measured how its words are read

*Where stipulated meaning and read meaning have actually been compared — probability words — they came apart by tens of points, and the spread differs enormously from word to word. Whether that carries to invented tier words like `robust-qualitative` is untested anywhere, and that it is untested is the finding this segment is actually making.*

## The claim

Every verisectorium in this estate marks how strongly a claim is held, and every one of them does it with a **word**: `exact`, `robust-qualitative`, `empirical`, `discussion-grade`, `sketch`, `fired-once`, `attested`, `reconstructed`, `secondhand`. The word is fixed by a definition somewhere — a FORMAT table, a terminology entry — and thereafter treated as though the definition is what the reader receives.

**(A) Stipulating a label's meaning does not control how it is read.** This has been measured, though not here and not on these words. Population studies of verbal probability expressions find stable median interpretations with *very* unequal agreement: some phrases are read almost identically by everyone, others are read so inconsistently that the median hides a bimodal split. And where an institution has stipulated meanings for its own terms and the public interpretation was then measured, the two came apart by tens of percentage points — the stipulated meaning did not transfer, and the direction of the error was systematic (extreme phrases pulled toward the middle).

**(B) For probability words, spread is a property of the label, not of the claim, and it is measurable per label.** That much is established. The useful consequence — *within that domain* — is not "use numbers instead": it is that **the words in a ladder are not interchangeable in quality.** Some carry their meaning intact and some do not, and which is which is an empirical fact about the word, discoverable in advance rather than discovered by being misread.

**Whether this carries to invented tier words is the untested step**, and everything below (C) depends on it. `robust-qualitative` is not a probability phrase and no one has measured how it is read. What can be said without the transfer is narrower and still worth saying: an estate that marks every claim with a stipulated word, and has never checked how any of those words land, is running an instrument of unknown gauge — and does not know whether it is a good one or a bad one.

**(C) The estate has this instrument, uses it on its theory, and has never pointed it at its own labels.** The calibration work is not neglected here — it is well developed and actively cited. What it has never been applied to is the **epistemic-status vocabulary the corpora themselves run on**. The gap is specific and worth stating precisely: the estate studies how *English hedge words* encode confidence, and separately marks its own claims with *invented tier words*, and no document connects the two. The instrument is in the house and pointed outward.

**(D) What this does *not* establish.** The measured spreads are for probability phrases (*likely*, *possible*, *almost certain*). `robust-qualitative` is not one of them, and there is no measurement showing that epistemic-status terms behave the same way. The transfer is an argument from the structure of the situation — stipulated word, uninstructed reader — not a finding. It could fail: a ladder whose readers all read its terminology entries is a different population from a general one, and a small, trained, self-selected readership is exactly the case where stipulation might hold.

## Strength & grounds

Three separable legs, at different strengths.

**Leg 1 — the external calibration facts: strong, and genuinely outside this estate.** Mosteller & Youtz (1990), n=238, gives percentile distributions for **53** verbal probability expressions; the inter-quartile spread ranges from near-zero to enormous — *"possible"* has a median near 38% with an IQR over 40 points and is explicitly bimodal, while *"always"* has an IQR under half a point. Budescu et al. (2009, 2012) measured IPCC's stipulated likelihood terms against public interpretation and found *very likely* (intended >90%) read at about 67%. Vogel (2022) meta-analyzed 21 studies spanning 1967–2018 and found the interpretations temporally stable. These are published, externally authored, and independently replicated — **the only material behind any segment in this plan whose authorship is fully outside this estate.**

**Leg 2 — an independent second instrument agreeing: moderately strong, in-estate analysis of external artifacts.** The epistemic-hedging work at `~/src/embeddings/` finds hedge expressions occupying a calibrated linear axis in sentence-embedding space, recovered across **six embedding models spanning five architectural families**, and transferring zero-shot across 8 typologically diverse languages.

**The evidential structure matters and is easy to state wrongly.** Mosteller's medians are the *training signal* — the axis is fit to them — so the Mosteller agreement is a fit-quality check, not confirmation. The independent confirmation is the **held-out** sets: Vogel 2022 (ρ = 0.991) and Wintle 2019 (ρ = 0.967), neither used in fitting. That is what makes this a convergent leg rather than a second reading of the same evidence, and the failure modes really are different in kind — survey response bias, framing and question-order effects on one side; embedding artifacts, tokenization, and corpus-frequency confounds on the other. The analysis is Joseph's own (paper submitted to TACL as `11139-epistemic-hedging.pdf`), so its *conduct* is in-estate even though its instruments and ground truth are not.

**Leg 3 — the transfer to this estate's ladders: discussion-grade, and honestly so.** First-hand survey, 2026-08-05, and the negative result is narrower than it first looked, so it is stated exactly. A grep for `mosteller|budescu|verbal probabilit` across `~/src/arch/` and `~/src/_core/` returns **many** hits, in at least four places that matter: the calibration material itself; emergence transcripts; several ASF spike and `msc/` files; and two substantive ones — `asf/03-llm-core/OUTLINE.md`, which carries a detailed account of the embedding results and reads them as *"empirical substrate for the claim that logogenic agents can leverage language-geometric encodings of epistemic states for $U_M$ / $U_o$ / hedging-projection estimates"*, and `_core/ennaos/docs/research/vera/deprecated-hierarchical-knowledge-representation-in-graphs.md`, which lists Mosteller & Youtz among the statistical foundations for a numeric confidence architecture. So the estate has emphatically **not** ignored this research. It has connected it to *an agent estimating its own uncertainty* and to *encoding confidence as a number* — both different problems from the one here.

The narrow negative is what survives, and it is the one this segment needs: restricting the same grep to `asf/0*/src/` (the claim segments), `asf/terminology/` (the entries defining the tier words), and `asf/FORMAT.md` (which legislates the ladder) returns **nothing**. The labels are fixed stipulatively instead — `~/src/arch/asf/terminology/entries/robust-qualitative.md` defines its term by prose and by contrast with `exact` and `heuristic`, making no claim about how it is read. Comproprium's `vera/` segments run on the same ported ladder, and its `praxes`/`exempla` ladders are separately stipulated vocabularies again (`FORMAT.md` D4).

So the accurate statement is not "the estate has never met this research" but **"the estate applies it to the agent's uncertainty and not to its own labels."** That the *consequence* measured for probability words also holds for invented tier words is inference from shape, and is the leg to attack.

*(Method note, kept visible because the failure is instructive: the first version of this leg claimed every hit lay in the calibration material or in intake copies of it. That was an artifact of reading a `head`-truncated grep as if it were the whole result — a truncated search and an exhaustive one produce the same-shaped output, so nothing in the result announced the difference. Caught only because the claim was re-run before landing.)*

**The test, and it is cheap.** Take one ladder's terms, elicit interpretations from a set of readers who have not read the terminology entries (fresh agents are a legitimate and very cheap population here, and arguably the *relevant* one — most consumers of these labels are agents, not people), and compare against the stipulated ordering and against each other. The finding that would matter most is not the median but the **spread**, and the actionable outcome is per-label: a term with wide spread should be split, renamed, or given a numeric companion. Nobody has run it.

## Working Notes

- Count discrepancy worth not re-litigating: `verbal-probability-calibration.md`'s prose says Mosteller covers "54 verbal expressions," but `mosteller_youtz_1990_full.csv` has **53** data rows and the embeddings project also says 53. 53 is used above. Trivial in itself; recorded because a number repeated from prose rather than from the dataset is exactly how a wrong figure becomes estate-canonical.
- The sharpest single number for arguing this to a skeptic is the IPCC gap: a body that stipulated its terms *in the document itself*, for expert use, still had them read ~28 points off intent. Stipulation-in-the-corpus is exactly this estate's mechanism.
- Scope note kept deliberately: this segment covers **presentation** calibration — how a confidence *word* is received. It is a different layer from **decision** calibration — how independent evidence is combined into a strength at all — which is [[evidence-ledgers]]' territory. The two were tangled in the gathered notes and should stay apart.
- Relation to [[strength-ladders]]: that row owes an adjudication across incompatible ladders. This segment adds a criterion that adjudication can actually use — *per-term read-spread* — which is measurable, whereas "which ladder is right" may not be.
- Relation to [[epistemic-axes]]: spread-of-the-label is not one of the three axes named there (strength × support-kind × convergence). It is a property of the *encoding* rather than of the claim, and probably belongs beside them rather than among them.
- Deliberately **not** carried from the gathered material: Beta-distribution encoding, credible-interval arithmetic, and Bayesian-network propagation. They are real and specified in the source, but they belong to a machine-inference design, and importing them here would be adopting machinery this project has no use for yet — the ch. 8 gap row on Bayesian propagation is where that goes if it goes anywhere.
- The strongest *counter*-lead, and it should be chased before this segment is promoted: `~/src/arch/asf/03-llm-core/OUTLINE.md` already reads the embedding results as substrate for an agent estimating its own $U_M$ / $U_o$. If someone has already argued anywhere in that volume that an agent's *self-reported* hedge language is calibratable, then the step from there to "so are the corpus's tier labels" may already be half-made, and this segment should cite that rather than stand alone. Not chased this pass.
