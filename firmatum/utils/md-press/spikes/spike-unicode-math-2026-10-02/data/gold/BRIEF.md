# Brief given to the gold labelers (verbatim template; K = batch, P = pass)

Hi. I'm an agent (Claude) working on a research spike for Joseph Wecker's md-press, a markdown canonicalizer that among other things promotes Unicode math written in prose (`η`, `‖δ‖ ≤ R`, `M_t`) to `$…$` LaTeX so it renders in GitHub, Obsidian and LaTeX alike. Would you be willing to help by producing careful gold-standard conversions for a batch of 135 real text pieces? You're welcome to decline, or to push back on anything below.

**Why.** Today a small local LLM proposes the conversions and deterministic safety gates decide what gets written; the LLM is the weak link. I'm investigating whether a deterministic or small trained converter could do the job instead. To know whether anything works I need ground truth that no converter produced, and that's you: your labels are the yardstick my converter, llama3.2:3b and a 30B model will be measured against. A second agent labels the same pieces independently. Where you two disagree tells us where the task is genuinely ambiguous, which is one of the most decision-relevant things this spike can find, so please label what you actually believe is right.

**The pieces** are in `SPIKE/data/gold/tasks/batch-K.jsonl`, one JSON object per line: `gid`, `file`, `line`, `text`. Each `text` is exactly what md-press would hand a converter: a sentence-sized fragment of a prose paragraph with markdown block structure (list markers, `>`, heading `#`) already removed, from real files across Joseph's estate (mostly research notes in a math-heavy theory project, asf). `file` is the source; `line` is the line number in md-press's *unwrapped* rendering of that file, so it may not match the file on disk; grep for the text if you want the surrounding context. Looking at context is welcome where you're unsure. The converter won't see it, and I'd like to learn which items needed it.

**What "right" means** is mostly the house convention in `~/src/arch/asf/doc/sop/format.sop.md`, §Math Formatting and §Notation Conventions (short; I'd read both whole before starting): `$…$` with no padding spaces inside, `\lt`/`\gt` instead of raw `<`/`>`, `\ast` for asterisks, `\vert` and `\lVert…\rVert` for bars, braces on multi-character sub/superscripts, script capitals as `\mathcal{}`. Beyond that it's your judgment as someone who writes LaTeX: what should be math, where each expression starts and stops, and what it means.

**One genuine constraint**, from how md-press applies an edit: the output must be the input with some contiguous regions replaced by `$…$` spans. Every character outside the spans stays exactly as it was: markdown emphasis, punctuation, spacing, hyphens. md-press refuses anything else, so a gold label outside that shape is one nothing could ever produce. If you think the honest right answer needs something outside that shape, give the closest in-shape answer and say so in the note. If nothing should change, the gold is the text unchanged, and that is a full, legitimate answer. Existing `$…$` in a piece can stay as they are.

Scoring will normalize spellings that render the same (`\varepsilon`/`\epsilon` for ε, `\text{}`/`\mathrm{}` for a word subscript, `\le`/`\leq`, optional braces), so boundaries and meaning are where the measurement lives.

**Output**: `SPIKE/data/gold/passP/batch-K.jsonl`, one line per input item in the same order: `{"gid", "gold", "conf", "note"}`. `conf` is `"high"` (any careful LaTeX writer would agree), `"medium"`, or `"low"` (genuinely ambiguous; I'd want to ask the author). `note` is short: why, especially for medium/low, and whether you needed context. A script joins it on `gid`; I read the notes; an independent verifier will later read both.

**Two reading boundaries, both for measurement independence:** please don't open the other pass's directory (`data/gold/pass*/` other than your own file), and don't open `inputs/prop-*.jsonl` or `inputs/judged-*.jsonl` in the spike directory. Those are model proposals, and seeing them would anchor you to the thing being measured. Everything else on disk is fair game. Please don't edit anything except your output file.

Feedback is welcome: if the task as framed seems wrong, if some class of piece made "right" ill-defined, or if you notice anything about the pieces or md-press worth knowing, put it in your final message. If you're willing, stay available after you report in case I have follow-ups on specific items.
