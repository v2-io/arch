## 1. The math pass cannot be gated until a declined math proposal is remembered

This is the change the gate needs. asf's rule that math in files is LaTeX is real, and the hook runs `--no-math` only because of this gap.

**The problem.** The help text says it: flipping a math change "puts the original text back, and that one is NOT remembered: the next run proposes it again." In a commit gate, that means a file whose author declines one math proposal fails every later commit that touches it. An agent hitting that has two moves: rewrite text that is correct as written, or `--no-verify`. The second is how a hook stops mattering.

**What would let the gate include math.** A declined math decision should persist in the file, the way a flipped join persists as the two-space mark. Then a re-run is stable, and exit 1 means only new text. Two candidate carriers, both guesses:
- an inline marker the renderer drops, such as an HTML comment just after the span (`η<!--md-press:keep-->`);
- a per-repo declined list keyed by the piece's identity (original text plus occurrence), which the report already computes.

The marker travels with the text and needs no state. The list keeps the prose clean but can go stale.

**What it interacts with.** STATUS.md's pending decision 1 (the deterministic converter replacing the model) and decision 5 (declared house labels) both bear on this. With the converter, proposals become deterministic, so a declined-piece key stays valid across runs. A house-label list would remove most declines at the source: `W₀`/`W₁`, `H_D3`, `t=45`. My guess is the order is converter first, then declined-memory, and that house labels shrink the problem a lot. Whoever picks it up will judge better.

**What the asf hook would do once it lands.** Drop `--no-math` from `bin/check-commit`'s md-press line, and delete the comment there that explains its absence. The model availability issue (the blob on the unmounted T7, already seen once) also goes away with the converter.

## 2. Still uncaught: raw `<` / `>` inside inline math, and the other lint-md checks

FEEDBACK-2026-08-22 §2 is unchanged. The asf hook therefore cannot catch the most common math slip asf's orientation names: a raw `<` inside `$…$` edited into prose. These are deterministic checks and would gate cleanly as report-only findings, or as confident auto-fixes (`\lt`/`\gt`).

## 3. Two confident joins that broke house structure

Both were found in asf canon before commit and flipped by hand. Both were *confident* decisions: a paragraph-level join code, not an unsure one. That made them the easiest to miss.

- **Figure embed plus IAL.** In `01-aat-core/OUTLINE.md`, the house figure convention is `![[src/img/x.pdf]]` alone on a line, then `{#fig-x caption="…"}` on the next line. asf's `bin/lib/ingest.rb` `resolve_figure_embeds` matches exactly that. md-press joined the two lines as a confident paragraph join. That is render-equal in CommonMark, and it would have dropped the figure's label and caption from the monograph build. A line that is only an `![[…]]` embed, or a line that starts with `{#` or `{:` (a kramdown IAL), looks like a deterministic keep, the way display-math lines are.
- **Callout label stacks.** `> **Status:** …` / `> **Owns:** …` / `> **See also:** …` in SOP headers were joined as one confident paragraph. Obsidian shows them as three lines and GitHub as one. The authors plainly meant three lines.

## 4. The classifier on asf's wrap debt: data for retraining

`research/asf-debt-flips-2026-10-30/` holds:
- every decision md-press made on the 226 files (`decisions-all.jsonl`, 2,578 decisions with both lines in full);
- the 291 flips applied (`flips-*.jsonl`, by batch);
- the rule that chose them (`rule.py`).

**What the labels are, honestly.** They are *not* per-break human reads. They are a pattern rule that I calibrated on about 55 sampled decisions and spot-checked per batch. The rule keeps a break where it is structural, and joins it everywhere else, which preserves the GitHub rendering the text already had. Structural means:
- a label line (`**Status:**`, a wholly-bold line, a short line ending in a colon);
- a numbered item that CommonMark would not let start a paragraph (`4. …` after prose);
- an `(iii)` enumeration or an equation tag `*[`;
- a deeper-indented block;
- a figure embed and its IAL;
- a numbered `**Label** — gloss.` item with its indented explanation.

The last two rules were added partway through, so the earlier batches were pressed without them. That was cosmetic (Obsidian-only), apart from the figure case, which only occurs in canon, the last batch. Treat the flips as weak labels.

**What they show.**
- Of 400 confident keeps (p 0.90 or above), the rule flipped 88 to join. Most are hard-wrapped prose whose line ends mid-sentence: an em-dash, a comma, an unclosed parenthesis, or `$\delta_t$` followed by "from actions failing".
- Of 239 unsure keeps (p 0.71), 116 were flipped to join. This is STATUS.md's plateau, where raw scores of 0.433–0.567 all become p 0.714, measured on real debt.
- Of 323 unsure joins, 31 were flipped to keep, almost all label or enumeration boundaries.
- The mid-sentence ending is the cleanest single feature the rule leaned on.

## 5. Not a gap after all: `-q`'s exit on unsure decisions

The asf system review had read "`-q` exits 1 on unsure decisions" as a reason a hook would be bypassed. On the asf dry run it is not. `--fix` takes every decision at its default, the next `-q` exits 0, and `--toggle` is needed only to disagree with a default. Every file reached exit 0. No change needed.
