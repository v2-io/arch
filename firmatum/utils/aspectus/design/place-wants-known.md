# What the place wants known

*Seeded 2026-08-14 with its first concrete affordance (Joseph's, verbatim below). The row's law predates it: this is a **secondary content channel** — the place may leave a note the look can surface; it never sets columns, budget, depth, or format ([[../../../principles/src/norm-caller-tunes-the-channel.md|caller-tunes-the-channel]]: the observed may speak into the channel; only the caller tunes it).*

## The first affordance: look-suggestions with reasons

Joseph, on estate norms vs agent sovereignty over defaults:

> "There are some norms in this estate that make certain depths and prominences a bit unique. But it's the agent's eyes — that can be an affordance that goes into the 'what the project wants you to know/see' row… 'This project suggests you look 3 levels deep and turn on --____ because ____...'"

The shape that keeps the law: the place's file may carry **suggestions** — proposed look-settings *with their reasons* — and the look renders them as **visible content** (a header-area note, or on the config surface), never applies them. The agent reads "this project suggests `--depth 3` because segment prominence lives at the third level," weighs it with its own eyes, and asks or doesn't. The reason is mandatory in spirit: a suggestion without a why is a tuning attempt wearing a note's clothes.

Why this threads the needle: silently applied, project settings are the Regime-I channel attack (a changing tree retunes the agent's eyes); rendered as attributed content, they are exactly what a good README does — teaching — with the tool carrying the note to the reader's attention at zero read cost. Trust stays with the reader.
