## 1. Under the walk bound, unenumerated siblings vanish with no marker at their level — the tree looks finished

**Verified** (behavior + `src/n_level.rs:156–193`). Severity: highest — it is the tool's own core promise ("never a tree that looks finished," help's closing paragraph: "The look never lies by omission") failing at default settings on this very crate.

Repro: `aspectus` in the crate dir, defaults (`--walk 10000`). `target/debug/deps` holds ~9,800 names; the bound trips mid-walk. Output (66 lines, exit 0): root children are `design/`, `impl/`, `target/`, `Cargo.toml` — **`src/`, `tests/`, `README.md`, `ASPECTUS.outline.md`, `PRACTICA.md`, `ORIGIN-DISCUSSION.md`, the seeds, `Cargo.lock`, `build.rs`, `.gitignore` are all absent**, with no `[+N: …]`, no `≥`, nothing at the root level. The only confession is `[walk bound]` appended to the header line, which reads as "somewhere deep down, an aggregate is a floor" — I genuinely believed `src/` did not exist until I diffed against `--walk 0`. As the intended end-user: this is precisely the "SURPRISE! This directory is far more than you thought" failure the tool exists to cure, produced by the tool, on itself, at defaults.

Mechanism (`gather_at`): when `walk.take()` fails the loop `break`s; the unseen `read_dir` remainder is not counted, censused, or even acknowledged on that node beyond `cut = true`, and the render only prints `[walk bound]` on the line — the listed children look like the complete set. `design/walk-bound.md` says "A directory the bound never entered is treated like a depth cutoff: its line says what is known, honestly partial" — but a directory the bound *half-entered* is the unhandled (and common) case. A cheap honest floor exists: keep draining the `read_dir` iterator for *names only* (readdir is what you already paid; it's stat you're bounding) so the level can still print `[+≥N: …]` or at least `[≥N unseen]`. Whether that's the right fix is the landing agent's call; the requirement that *the level itself confess* seems non-negotiable under the project's own law.

Corollary, same run: `--explain-budget` computed shares over the partial child set (`shares [5, 4, 4, 1]` for 4 children when the root has ~16) — the allocator's arithmetic silently inherits the lie.

## 2. The bounded walk's survivor set depends on readdir order — the determinism claim breaks exactly where it matters

**Verified** in code (`src/n_level.rs`: the bound cuts *before* `children.sort_by`), not observed as a live flake. The outline working note and `design/sort.md`/`design/columns.md` carry the binding claim "same tree + same caller state ⇒ byte-identical look." Under a walk bound, which children exist at all is a function of raw `read_dir` order — unspecified, filesystem- and history-dependent. Two aspecta of an *unchanged* tree on two machines (or after an APFS defrag) can differ, and `last-look`/after-image diffs would report phantom movement. Nowhere in `design/walk-bound.md`, `sort.md`, or the outline is the determinism claim scoped for bounded walks. Either the walk order must be made deterministic (e.g. enumerate names per level, sort, then spend the stat budget) or the claim needs an honest carve-out written where the claim lives.

## 3. The walk budget spends depth-first on noise before it has even enumerated the shallow, high-value level

**Verified** by the same repro: ~9,800 of the 10,000 stats went into `target/debug/deps` before the walk ever reached `src/` — the glance's cheapest, most valuable names. This is distinct from Joseph's broot complaint (that was breadth-first *line fill*; this is *stat order*): the line budget already balances breadth vs depth, but the walk bound is pure depth-first, so its exhaustion pattern is worst-case for a glance. A shallow-first (or at least finish-each-level's-names-first) spend order would make finding 1 nearly unreachable in practice and finding 2 easier to fix. No design file discusses walk *order*; `walk-bound.md` is three bullets. Flagging as a design gap, not prescribing the shape.

Worth one deliberate sentence in `form-exit-vocabulary`'s aspectus overlay or `walk-bound.md`, whichever way it goes.
